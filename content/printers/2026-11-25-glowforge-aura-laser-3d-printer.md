**The Glowforge Aura is a desktop craft laser cutter and engraver with a 6 W diode laser, so although people search for it as a "Glowforge Aura laser 3D printer", it does not print 3D objects – it cuts and engraves flat materials up to 12 × 12 inches.** Glowforge put it on sale in the US in July 2023 at a launch price of about US$1,199 (US retail, July 2023), aimed at home crafters rather than workshops. This page explains what it does, how it differs from a 3D printer, and how setup works.

## Glowforge Aura at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | Desktop diode laser cutter and engraver ("Craft Series") |
| Laser | 6 W diode, 450 nm; Class 1 product with an embedded Class 4 laser |
| Material size | Up to 12 × 12 in; passthrough for 12 in wide sheets of any length |
| Cutting | Up to 1/4 in |
| Max material height | 3/4 in without the tray, 1/4 in with it |
| Autofocus | Up to 0.5 in (13 mm) |
| Connectivity | Wi-Fi, 2.4 GHz b/g/n (required) |
| Size and weight | 22 × 20.5 × 5 in; 19 lb |
| Power | 110/220 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 84 W peak |
| Launch price | About US$1,199 (US, July 2023) |

## Laser cutter vs 3D printer: which one you need

A laser cutter is subtractive: it burns or vaporises material along a path to cut shapes or mark a surface. A 3D printer is additive: it melts filament or cures resin to build a part layer by layer. The Aura makes flat parts – signs, earrings, coasters, stencils, layered paper art – which you can stack or slot together into 3D shapes. If you want solid objects such as brackets, figurines or enclosures, you need a 3D printer.

## What the Glowforge Aura cuts and engraves

According to Glowforge, the Aura can **cut, score and engrave** wood, acrylic, leather, veneer, iron-on vinyl, paper and cardboard. It can **engrave only** denim, mylar, rubber, cork, sandpaper, food, stone, slate, titanium, anodised aluminium, marble and ceramic tile.

Glowforge's own Proofgrade materials carry a code the camera reads, so the app sets power and speed for you. Other materials need manual settings.

## Who the Aura suits

The Aura suits crafters who want a small, closed laser for a desk or kitchen table, with a camera-based app that places designs on the material. It is less suited to thick stock or production work: cutting stops at 1/4 inch and the diode laser is slower than the CO2 lasers in Glowforge's larger machines.

## Setting up the Glowforge Aura

Glowforge's setup guide covers these steps, at a high level:

1. Check the box for shipping damage – do not plug in a damaged unit.
2. Place the Aura on a flat, stable surface near an outlet.
3. Unbox it, clip the laser head onto its magnetic dock, remove the foam, align the arm and slide the tray in.
4. Attach the exhaust hose to the Craft Laser Filter, or route it out of a window with no more than 8 feet of hose and no kinks.
5. Plug in and power on.
6. Create a Glowforge account and connect the Aura to Wi-Fi with Glowforge's setup app.
7. Let the head centre and calibrate itself, then run a first print.

## Venting and safety

The Aura must be vented while it runs. Glowforge offers two options: an exhaust hose of 8 feet or shorter through a window that opens to the outside, or the Glowforge Craft Laser Filter, sold separately. Never cut materials that the maker does not list – some plastics release harmful fumes.

## Software and file formats

The Glowforge app runs in a web browser on Mac, PC, Linux and many tablets, and shows a live camera view of the bed. It accepts JPG, PNG, SVG and PDF files; DXF needs a Glowforge Premium subscription. Because the app is online, the Aura needs internet access to work.
