**An Ender 3D printer is one of Creality's low-cost FDM printers – a line that began with the Ender-3 in 2018 and now runs from the Ender-3 V3 SE to the large Ender-5 Max.** Most Ender-3 models share a 220 × 220 mm bed, while the Ender-5 family uses a box frame. This page lists the main models with their build volumes, motion systems and years, so you can tell them apart.

## Current Ender models

In November 2026 the main menu on Creality's site lists these Ender printers:

| Model | Build volume | Motion | Launched |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ender-3 V3 SE | 220 × 220 × 250 mm | Cartesian bed-slinger | 2023 |
| Ender-3 V3 KE | 220 × 220 × 240 mm | Cartesian, X-axis linear rail | Sep 2023 |
| Ender-3 V3 Neo | 220 × 220 × 220 mm | Bed-slinger, built-in camera | Oct 2026 |
| Ender-3 V3 Mega | 420 × 420 × 420 mm | Dual Y and dual Z motors | Oct 2026 |
| Ender-5 Max | 400 × 400 × 400 mm | CoreXY | – |

**Tip:** the 220 × 220 mm bed stayed the same for years, so older Ender-3 accessories and build plates often fit newer bed-slingers – check the exact model before you buy.

The Ender-5 box frame keeps the bed on the Z axis only, so tall prints do not wobble back and forth.

## How to choose an Ender printer

1. **Want the simplest start?** Pick a model with auto levelling – the V2 Neo, S1 or any V3 – rather than the original Ender-3 or V2.
2. **Want Wi-Fi?** The V3 KE has it; the V3 SE prints from an SD card only.
3. **Need a bigger bed?** Look at the S1 Plus, V3 Plus, V3 Mega or Ender-5 Max.
4. **Buying used?** Older models are cheap, but expect manual levelling and some tuning.
