**The Prusa INDX is a toolchanger for the Prusa CORE One that prints with up to eight materials, each from its own induction-heated nozzle.** It was developed by Bondtech, with Prusa as the exclusive integrator for the CORE One, and announced on 19 November 2025. It comes as a conversion kit with 4 or 8 tools and as part of complete CORE One+ (Gen 2) and CORE One L+ printers.

## Prusa INDX at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tools | 4 or 8 (the 4-tool kit can be upgraded) |
| Compatible printers | CORE One family, including CORE One+ (Gen 2) and CORE One L |
| Max nozzle temperature | 300 °C |
| Tool change | About 12 s (PLA) |
| Priming per change | About 13 mg |
| Build volume with INDX | 248 × 205 × 270 mm (CORE One), 298 × 275 × 330 mm (CORE One L) |
| Launch price (first batch) | US$749 / US$999; €669 / €899 |

## How the Prusa INDX toolchanger works

Most multi-colour systems push one filament out of a single nozzle and load the next. INDX does not. One active **Smart Head** rides on the gantry and holds the induction coil, the sensing electronics and the extruder drive. The tools parked at the front of the printer are passive: no wires, heaters or thermistors, only a filament path and a nozzle. When the head picks up a tool, the coil heats that nozzle to printing temperature in a few seconds, according to Prusa.

**What this means:** each material keeps its own nozzle, so there is no full unload and reload between colours. Prusa says large multi-material prints waste about 20–30 g in total.

## Prusa INDX specs

- **Nozzles:** 0.25, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 mm; a 0.4 mm high-flow nozzle is the default.
- **Filaments:** PLA, PETG, ASA, ABS, PC, PA, TPU, PVA, BVOH and other non-abrasive materials, 1.75 mm.
- **Abrasives:** Prusa describes the nozzles as surface-hardened rather than fully hardened and does not recommend them for long-term abrasive use.
- **Sensors:** LoadCell first-layer and mesh calibration, contactless temperature sensing, eddy-current tool offset calibration (about 15 seconds per tool) and an accelerometer.
- **In the 8-tool kit:** Smart Head, 8 tools, docks, mounting parts and PTFE tubes, a top cover with automatic venting, 8 spool holders and a nozzle wiper.

## Prusa INDX price and availability

Prusa opened first-batch pre-orders on 23 April 2026 and the batch sold out. Launch prices for the conversion kit, tariffs and VAT included, were:

| Kit | US | EU |

Standard kits began shipping on 20 August 2026, when complete CORE One+ (Gen 2) INDX printers also opened for orders; CORE One L+ kits and printers followed on 27 August 2026. Prusa's product pages currently show shipping dates after February 2027 for new orders.

## Setting up the Prusa INDX

At a high level, installing the conversion kit means:

1. Fitting the Smart Head in place of the standard print head.
2. Mounting the tool docks at the front of the chamber.
3. Loading a filament into each tool.
4. Running the calibration, during which the printer picks up and parks every tool and measures its offset.

Prusa publishes the step-by-step guides in the Prusa Knowledge Base under "Prusa INDX Conversion kit for the Prusa CORE One/+", with a separate unpacking and first-run guide for the complete CORE One+ (Gen 2) INDX printer.

## Firmware and slicer support

Prusa firmware adds support for up to 8 tools. Prusa says multi-colour prints can be prepared in its EasyPrint app and PrusaSlicer, and existing profiles and Prusa Connect carry over. In April 2026 Prusa still described full desktop PrusaSlicer support as planned, so update to the newest release before your first INDX print. ## Prusa INDX vs MMU3

| | MMU3 | INDX |
|---|---|---|
| Materials | 5 | 4 or 8 |
| Method | Swaps filament in one nozzle | Separate nozzle per material |
| Purge | Full unload and reload | About 13 mg per change |

Prusa calls the MMU3 an affordable way to add five filaments to a single-nozzle machine. INDX costs more but avoids filament swapping, and Prusa says its purge is five times lower than the most efficient swapping systems.
