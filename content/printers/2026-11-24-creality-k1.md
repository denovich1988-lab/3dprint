**The Creality K1 is an enclosed CoreXY 3D printer with a 220 × 220 × 250 mm build volume, a rated top speed of 600 mm/s and up to 32 mm³/s of flow.** Creality unveiled it on 9 April 2023 at a US launch price of $599, as the first model of its K1 series. It ships assembled and calibrates itself on first start.

## Creality K1 at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Build volume | 220 × 220 × 250 mm |
| Speed / acceleration | 600 mm/s / 20,000 mm/s² |
| Max flow | 32 mm³/s |
| Nozzle / bed | 300 °C / 100 °C |
| Motion | CoreXY, enclosed |
| Firmware | Creality OS (Klipper-based) |
| Connectivity | Wi-Fi, LAN, USB drive |
| Weight | 12.5 kg |

## Who the Creality K1 suits

The K1 suits people who want a fast, enclosed printer that works out of the box, with a footprint small enough for a desk. The enclosure helps with ABS and ASA. For carbon-fibre filaments the later [Creality K1C](/printers/creality-k1c/) is the better fit, and for bigger parts the [Creality K1 Max](/printers/creality-k1-max/) has a 300 mm cube build volume.

## Creality K1 features

- **CoreXY motion** with a lightweight print head and a 50 N dual-gear direct extruder.
- **Hands-free levelling** – strain sensors in the heatbed measure the nozzle touching the plate.
- **Input shaping** tuned automatically with a G-sensor during the self-test.
- **Enclosed body** with a glass lid and door, plus filament run-out detection and power-loss recovery.
- **Optional AI camera and AI LiDAR**, sold separately, for remote viewing and first-layer checks.

## Setting up the Creality K1

1. Remove all foam, including the pieces around the print head.
2. Connect the screen cable and snap the screen into the base.
3. Remove the three fixing screws that hold the motion system for shipping.
4. Power on and follow the on-screen guide: language, network, time zone, Creality Cloud binding and the **self-test**, which levels the bed and tunes input shaping.
5. Load filament and start the test print.

**Tip:** let the self-test finish without opening the lid or touching the printer; vibration during the input-shaping step affects the result.

## Connecting the Creality K1

Join the printer to **Wi-Fi** or plug in a **LAN** cable during the first setup. You can then send jobs from **Creality Print** on your computer (add the printer by its IP address) or from the **Creality Cloud** app, which also handles remote monitoring. Printing from a **USB drive** works without a network. Use our [3MF to STL converter](/3mf-to-stl/) if you need a plain mesh from a project file.

## Creality K1 manual

Creality publishes the K1 user manual and quick start guide on the Creality Wiki under K1 Flagship Series → K1. It covers unpacking, first start and self-test, the screen menus, printing over the network, maintenance and troubleshooting.

## Firmware and software

Firmware updates arrive over the air, through Creality Cloud or as a file for a USB drive from Creality's K1 Flagship download page; Creality recommends running the self-test after an update. Creality's slicer is **Creality Print**; the spec sheet also lists Cura, PrusaSlicer and Simplify3D. Creality OS is based on Klipper, and Creality has published the K1-series Klipper source on GitHub.
