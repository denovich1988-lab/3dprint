**A Creality 3D scanner turns a real object into a 3D mesh you can edit and print; Creality's range runs from infrared models like the Ferret and Otter to blue-laser models like the Raptor, Pika and Sermoon P1S.** All of them use the Creality Scan software and export STL, OBJ or PLY files. This page compares the models with Creality's own figures.

## Entry level: Ferret and Lizard

The **Ferret**, **Ferret SE** and **Ferret Pro** share 0.1 mm accuracy, a 150–700 mm working distance and a 50 mm minimum object size. The Pro scans at up to 60 fps and works wirelessly over Wi-Fi 6, including with an iPhone; the Ferret and SE run at 30 fps over a cable. The **CR-Scan Lizard** reaches up to 0.05 mm without markers and adds colour by mapping photos onto the mesh.

**Who they suit:** hobbyists scanning medium objects such as shoes, helmets or furniture parts.

## Mid range: Otter and Otter Lite

The **CR-Scan Otter** uses near-infrared structured light with four stereo lenses, captures full colour and works with objects from 10 mm to 2 m. The **Otter Lite** (launched in May 2025 at US$759 regular price) weighs 300 g, captures 24-bit colour, runs at up to 30 fps and connects by USB-C or by Wi-Fi 6 through its bridge. It works with Windows, macOS, Android and iOS.

**Who they suit:** makers who scan a mix of small and large objects and want colour textures.

## Blue laser: the Raptor family

Blue laser lines capture fine edges on small, shiny or dark parts better than infrared light. The **CR-Scan Raptor** uses 7 parallel blue lines plus NIR, with 0.02 mm accuracy and up to 60 fps. The **RaptorX** adds 34 cross lines, 24-bit colour and Wi-Fi 6 at 405 g. The **Raptor Pro** (February 2025, US$1,799 regular price) uses 22 cross lines and connects by cable or through a Scan Bridge. The **Raptor Pro Max**, available from 19 November 2026 at a US suggested price of US$1,799, has 42 cross lines and a working distance of up to 550 mm.

**Who they suit:** reverse engineering, replacement parts and miniatures where detail matters.

## Newest models: Pika and Sermoon P1S

The **Pika** went on sale on 22 July 2026 at US$699. It weighs 260 g, combines a 7-line blue laser with NIR, and lists 0.03 mm accuracy in laser mode and up to 110 fps with a PC. The **Sermoon P1S**, launched on 22 September 2026 at US$2,299, has a built-in computer and a 6-inch touchscreen so it can scan without a PC, with 34 cross lines, 7 parallel lines and a single line for deep holes.

## Creality Scan software

Creality Scan runs on a computer and as a phone app. It removes noise, simplifies meshes and maps colour textures, then exports **STL, OBJ or PLY**. For the RaptorX and Raptor Pro, Creality lists Windows 10/11 64-bit with an Nvidia graphics card, or macOS 11.7.7 or newer on Apple M1–M3.

## From scan to 3D print

1. Scan the object and let Creality Scan build the mesh.
2. Clean up noise, fill holes and simplify the mesh in Creality Scan.
3. Export as STL – or export PLY or OBJ and convert with our [PLY to STL](/ply-to-stl/) or [OBJ to STL](/obj-to-stl/) converter.
4. Check the mesh in our [STL viewer](/stl-viewer/), then slice it in Creality Print or another slicer.
