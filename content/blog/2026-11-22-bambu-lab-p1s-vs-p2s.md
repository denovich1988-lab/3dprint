**The Bambu Lab P1S and P2S share the same 256 mm build volume and 300 °C hot end, but the P2S is a re-engineered machine** with a touchscreen, better camera, stronger extruder and a quicker nozzle swap. Bambu Lab announced the P2S on 14 October 2025 and says the P1S stays on sale and supported.

All figures below come from Bambu Lab’s own wiki and announcement.

## Side-by-side

| | P1S | P2S |
|---|---|---|
| Build volume | 256 × 256 × 256 mm | 256 × 256 × 256 mm |
| Max nozzle temperature | 300 °C | 300 °C |
| Max bed temperature | 100 °C | 110 °C |
| Nozzle | Stainless steel, 0.4 mm | 0.4 mm, quick-swap hot end |
| Extruder | Steel gears | PMSM servo, up to 8.5 kg force |
| Screen | 2.7-inch, 192 × 64, buttons | 5-inch touchscreen |
| Camera | 1280 × 720 at 0.5 fps | 1080p live view |
| Flow calibration | — | Auto Flow Dynamics (eddy-current sensor) |
| AI error detection | — | Spaghetti, nozzle blobs, air printing |
| Filtration | Activated carbon | Activated carbon |

## What the upgrades mean in practice

**Touchscreen and camera.** The P1S is controlled mostly from the app because its screen is small. The P2S can be run from the printer itself, and its 1080p live view makes remote checking much more useful.

**Extruder and calibration.** A stronger servo extruder that senses resistance can catch grinding and clogs early, and automatic flow calibration reduces the tuning you do for new filaments.

**Nozzle swaps.** On the P2S the nozzle and heatsink come out together with one clip, which helps if you switch between 0.4 mm and 0.6 mm nozzles often.

**Cooling.** The P2S pulls outside air in, so PLA and PETG can print with the door closed, and it can hold heat in for ABS and ASA.

## Which should you buy?

- **Choose the P2S** if you want to control the printer from its screen, watch prints remotely in good quality, or change nozzles often.
- **Choose the P1S** if it is noticeably cheaper where you live and you are happy using the app. It prints the same size parts and the same materials.
