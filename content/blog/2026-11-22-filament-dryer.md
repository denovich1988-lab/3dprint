**A filament dryer is a small heated box that pulls moisture out of 3D printer filament, so it prints cleanly again.** Most plastics used for printing absorb water from the air. When wet filament reaches a hot nozzle, the water turns to steam, and you get popping, stringing, rough walls and weak layers.

This guide explains which filaments need drying, how to use a dryer safely and how to keep spools dry afterwards. Drying temperatures differ by brand, so always check the label on your spool.

## Signs your filament is wet

| Symptom | What you see or hear |
|---|---|
| Popping or hissing | Small cracks and sizzling sounds at the nozzle while printing |
| Steam | Thin wisps rising from the nozzle |
| Stringing | Far more hairs between parts than the same settings gave before |
| Rough surface | Bubbles, pits or a fuzzy finish on walls |
| Brittle filament | PLA that snaps when you bend it near the spool |
| Weak parts | Layers that split apart with little force |

If two or more of these appear on a spool that used to print well, drying is the first thing to try before changing slicer settings.

## Which filaments need a dryer

Water absorption varies a lot between materials:

- **TPU and other flexibles** – absorb moisture quickly. Prusa notes that flexible filaments tend to absorb moisture and are hard to print when wet.
- **Nylon (PA) and carbon-fibre nylon** – very hygroscopic; dry before every print.
- **PC and PVA** – also need drying. Bambu Lab warns that PC can become brittle after many drying and cooling cycles, so do not dry it more often than needed.
- **PETG** – picks up moisture over days or weeks; drying fixes most stringing problems.
- **PLA** – absorbs water more slowly. A freshly opened, vacuum-sealed spool usually prints fine.

Bambu Lab's filament table lists drying requirements for each material it sells, and it marks several engineering filaments as needing drying before use.

## How to dry filament with a dryer

1. **Check the label.** Find the drying temperature and time for your exact filament. If there is none, look up the maker's datasheet.
2. **Mount the spool.** Bambu Lab asks you to put refill (spool-less) filament on a spool before drying.
3. **Set the temperature and time.** Stay at or below the recommended temperature. Too hot, and the filament can fuse together on the spool.
4. **Let it run fully.** A heavily soaked spool may need the longer end of the recommended time.
5. **Print or store immediately.** Dry filament starts absorbing water again as soon as it cools in room air.

**Tip:** Weigh a spool before and after drying on a kitchen scale. If the weight drops by a few grams, water was there.

## What not to use

Bambu Lab advises against drying filament in a microwave or a kitchen oven. A microwave heats unevenly and has no temperature control. A kitchen oven has hot spots near the heating elements that can soften the spool or the filament. A dedicated dryer, or a printer's own drying function where it has one, gives steadier heat.

## Storing filament after drying

- Keep spools in **sealed bags or boxes with desiccant** (silica gel). Replace or recharge the desiccant when its indicator changes colour.
- An **AMS or other enclosed feeder with desiccant** keeps spools dry during use, according to Bambu Lab.
- A cheap **hygrometer** inside the box tells you when humidity is creeping up.
- Label each spool with the date you last dried it.

## Choosing a filament dryer

Look for these features rather than brand names:

- **Adjustable temperature** that covers the materials you print (higher for nylon and PC).
- **Airflow or venting**, so moist air leaves the box.
- **A filament outlet** to print directly from the dryer.
- **Room for the spool sizes** you use, including 2 kg or cardboard spools.
- **A timer**, so it switches off on its own.

Check the current specifications on the maker's product page before buying, because models change often.

## What this means for you

If you print mostly PLA and store spools sealed, start with a storage box and desiccant. If you print TPU, nylon, PC or PETG, or your workshop is humid, a filament dryer pays for itself in prints that no longer fail.
