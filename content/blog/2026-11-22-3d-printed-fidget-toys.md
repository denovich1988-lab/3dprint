**3D printed fidget toys are small moving gadgets – spinners, clickers, sliders and cubes – that you can print at home, often in one piece with the moving parts already assembled.** They are among the most popular 3D print ideas because they print quickly, use little filament and show off what a well-tuned printer can do.

This guide covers the main types of 3D printed fidgets, the best filament for each, the settings that make print-in-place parts move freely, and safety and licence points to keep in mind.

## Types of 3D printed fidgets at a glance

| Fidget type | How it works | Filament | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fidget spinner | Spins on a bearing or printed hub | PLA | Easy (bearing) |
| Infinity cube | Hinged cubes that fold endlessly | PLA | Easy |
| Clicker / button | Springy buttons that click | PLA or PETG | Medium |
| Slider | A knob sliding in a track | PLA | Easy |
| Gear fidget | Meshing gears turned by hand | PLA | Medium |
| Articulated snake or flexi animal | Chained segments that wiggle | PLA | Easy |
| Squishy fidget | Soft shape you squeeze | TPU | Medium |
| Puzzle fidget | Interlocking parts or a maze | PLA | Medium |

## Print-in-place fidgets

Print-in-place means the model is designed with small gaps between moving parts, so it prints as one job and moves straight off the bed. Articulated animals, hinged cubes and many gear fidgets work this way.

**Why it matters:** no glue, screws or assembly – but the gaps must stay open. That depends on your printer's tuning more than on the model.

## Settings that make fidgets move

1. **Dial in the first layer.** Too much squish creates elephant's foot that welds the bottom of moving parts together.
2. **Lower the nozzle temperature a little** within the filament's range to reduce sagging into gaps.
3. **Keep cooling on** for PLA, so small gaps solidify cleanly.
4. **Print at a normal layer height** (0.2 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle is typical for most designs). Very thick layers can bridge gaps closed.
5. **Do not scale print-in-place models down.** Scaling shrinks the clearance gaps too, and they may fuse.
6. **Free the parts while warm.** Gently twist and flex the model right after the print finishes.

**Tip:** If a design offers versions with different tolerances, print the small test piece first and pick the gap that moves freely on your printer.

## Filament for fidget toys

- **PLA** – accurate, colourful and easy. Silk and multi-colour PLA make striking fidgets.
- **PETG** – tougher, better for clickers and toys that get dropped, but strings more.
- **TPU** – for squishy and bendable fidgets. See our [TPU filament guide](/blog/tpu-filament/).

Glow-in-the-dark and matte filaments are popular too. Some speciality filaments are abrasive, so check whether your nozzle suits them.

## Fidget spinners: bearings or printed hubs

Classic spinners use a standard skateboard bearing (often a 608 size) pressed into the centre, which gives long, smooth spins. Fully printed spinners exist but spin for a shorter time. Check the model's description for the exact bearing size before you print.

## Multi-colour fidgets

Printers with multi-material systems can print fidgets in several colours in one job. Download the 3MF version of a model when available, because it keeps colour assignments. You can preview it in our [3MF viewer](/3mf-viewer/) or convert an STL with [STL to 3MF](/stl-to-3mf/).

## Safety

- **Small parts are a choking hazard.** Keep fidgets with small or detachable pieces away from young children.
- **Check for sharp edges** and sand them off.
- **Do not chew.** Printed plastic is not made for mouthing; see [is PLA food safe?](/blog/is-pla-food-safe/) for why layer lines trap bacteria.

## Selling 3D printed fidgets

Many free fidget designs carry non-commercial licences. Before selling prints, read the licence on the model page, or buy a commercial licence where the designer offers one.

## How to start

1. Pick an easy print-in-place design, such as an articulated snake or an infinity cube.
2. Open it in the [STL viewer](/stl-viewer/) to check the size.
3. Print in PLA with your printer's standard profile.
4. Free the moving parts while warm, and adjust temperature or first layer if anything sticks.

## Verdict

3D printed fidget toys are a quick, cheap way to get a lot of fun from your printer. Start with print-in-place PLA designs, keep your first layer clean and do not scale them down, and they will move straight off the bed. For more projects, see our [3D print ideas list](/blog/what-to-3d-print/).
