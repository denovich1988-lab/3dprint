**A SUNLU filament dryer is one of SUNLU's FilaDryer boxes – the S1 Plus, S2, SP2, S4, S4 Pro or E2 – which heat a spool to between about 35 °C and 110 °C (depending on the model) to drive moisture out of filament, and most of them let you print straight from the box.** The models differ mainly in how many spools they hold and how hot they get, and that decides which filaments each one can dry properly. This guide compares the whole range, matches each model to SUNLU's own drying temperatures for its filaments, and explains how to set one up and print from it. Specs come from SUNLU's product pages, official store and wiki, checked November 2026. We have not tested these dryers, and we do not compare prices.

If you want the general "why and how" of drying first, read our [filament dryer guide](/blog/filament-dryer/) and [how to dry filament](/blog/how-to-dry-filament/). This page is only about SUNLU's machines.

## FilaDryer S1 Plus – the small single-spool dryer

The S1 Plus is SUNLU's entry model. Its product page lists an adjustable range of 35–55 °C, a maximum working power of 48 W, "360°" wrap-around heating with a built-in fan for air circulation, and a 2-inch LCD that shows humidity and remaining time. The body measures 271 × 100 × 237 mm and weighs 960 g.

**What it suits:** 55 °C covers SUNLU's 50 °C drying setting for PLA, PLA+ and PETG 2.0 and the 50–60 °C range for TPU 95A. It does not reach SUNLU's 60–65 °C for standard PETG, let alone ABS or nylon.

**Tip:** treat the S1 Plus as a PLA dryer and a heated holder for the spool you are printing. If you print PETG regularly, the S2 is the better single-spool choice.

## FilaDryer S2 – one spool, up to 70 °C, print while drying

The S2 is the model most people mean by "SUNLU filament dryer". SUNLU's wiki lists a setting range of 35–70 °C, upper and lower heating plates with a circulating fan, a 48 W maximum working power, and a maximum spool size of 210 mm diameter by 85 mm wide. It accepts 1.75 mm, 2.85 mm and 3.00 mm filament. The 4.6-inch LCD shows temperature, humidity, time and the selected filament type, and the store page describes one-touch drying presets by filament type. The official store page also says you can print while it dries, with the filament leaving through the outlet as it feeds the printer.

The S2 measures 265 × 274 × 118 mm, weighs 1,109 g and works best in a 20–35 °C room.

**Why it matters:** 70 °C covers SUNLU's settings for PLA, PETG (60–65 °C), PETG-CF (70 °C) and both TPU grades. It sits 10 °C below SUNLU's 80 °C for ABS and ASA, so those dry more slowly.

**Tip:** check spool width before you buy. Most 1 kg spools fit, but wider spools, many 2 kg spools and some cardboard spools exceed 85 mm.

## FilaDryer SP2 – a dryer that doubles as a storage box

The SP2 splits into a heater base and a separate chamber that stack together, so it works as both a dryer and a sealed storage box. SUNLU lists capacity for two 1 kg spools or one 2–3 kg spool, spools up to 250 mm across and 153 mm wide, 0.25–3 kg spool weights, and both plastic and cardboard spools. It has a 250 W PTC heater with a metal roller fan, a built-in humidity meter, silicone seals on the lid and feet, and dual over-temperature protection. SUNLU says it heats to 70 °C in 15 minutes. It measures 278 × 208 × 396 mm and weighs 3.6 kg.

**What it suits:** the same materials as the S2 (up to 70 °C), but with room for large spools and somewhere to keep them afterwards. SUNLU's product page does not say whether you can print from the SP2 while it dries.

**Tip:** if you buy 2 kg or 3 kg spools to save money, the SP2 is the only SUNLU model built around them; see our [filament storage guide](/blog/how-to-store-filament/) for what to do with spools once they leave the box.

## FilaDryer S4 – four spools and up to four printers

The S4 is a long box for four 1 kg spools. SUNLU's wiki lists heating up to 70 °C from a PTC heater with a bottom heating fan and two side circulation fans, chamber temperature held within ±3 °C, a 300 W rated power and an inside space of 450 × 210 × 211 mm. Its intelligent humidity mode starts drying when humidity reaches a level you set between 30 % and 50 % and stops when it falls to 25 %. SUNLU says it can feed filament to up to four printers at the same time.

**Why it matters:** if you run more than one printer, or swap colours often, one S4 replaces several single-spool boxes and keeps all four spools dry between jobs.

**Tip:** the humidity mode is the useful part for storage. Leave spools in the box with the mode on, and it only heats when the air inside gets damp again.

## FilaDryer S4 Pro – two zones, 85 °C and annealing

The S4 Pro keeps the four-spool format but splits it into two chambers, each with its own temperature and timer shown on one screen. SUNLU's store lists a range of 45–85 °C, 380 W rated power, an inside space of 220 × 210 × 211 mm per chamber, and three modes: drying, annealing and humidity control (start at 30–50 % RH, stop at 25 %, with 24-hour monitoring and automatic re-drying). The annealing mode is meant for printed parts in materials with an annealing temperature of 85 °C or less. Two 1 m PTFE tubes come in the box for feeding filament out to the printer. It measures 458 × 218 × 310 mm and weighs 4.7 kg.

**Why it matters:** 85 °C covers SUNLU's 80 °C for ABS and ASA and its 70–80 °C for PC, and two zones let you dry PLA at 50 °C and ABS at 80 °C at the same time.

**Tip:** for nylon, 85 °C only reaches the bottom of SUNLU's 80–110 °C range. It works with longer times, but the E2 is the model designed for it.

## FilaDryer E2 – up to 110 °C for nylon and engineering filament

The E2 is SUNLU's engineering-filament dryer. Its wiki lists a maximum of 110 °C, capacity for two 1 kg spools or one 2 kg spool, 325 W rated power, a PTC heater with a rolling fan, double-chamber insulation and dual temperature protection switches. SUNLU says the outside stays at or below 60 °C even when running at 110 °C. It accepts 1.75 mm and 2.85 mm filament, measures 400 × 220 × 307 mm and weighs 7.6 kg. SUNLU's own guidance on the E2 is up to 70 °C for standard filaments and up to 90 °C for PA and PC. It also anneals printed parts at 80–90 °C for 2–3 hours.

**Why it matters:** SUNLU's nylon grades (Easy PA, PA6-CF, PA6-GF, PA12-CF) all list 80–110 °C for drying, which only the E2 fully covers.

**Tip:** check the spool before you go above 70–80 °C. Many plastic spools soften well below 110 °C; the dryer can handle the heat, but the spool may not.

These are SUNLU's drying figures for its own filaments, from its wiki, with the dryers that reach each one. Other brands may give different numbers, so the label on your spool comes first.

| SUNLU filament | Drying temperature | Time (where SUNLU gives one) | Models that reach it |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLA, PLA+, PLA+ 2.0, PLA Lite, PLA-CF | 50 °C | – | All |
| PETG 2.0 | 50 °C | – | All |
| TPU 95A | 50–60 °C | – | All (S1 Plus up to 55 °C) |
| PETG | 60–65 °C | 4–6 h if open more than 48 h | S2, SP2, S4, S4 Pro, E2 |
| TPU 90A | 60–70 °C | – | S2, SP2, S4, S4 Pro, E2 |

A 70 °C dryer can still dry ABS and ASA, but below the 80 °C on SUNLU's data sheets, so it takes longer. If you print a lot of ABS or ASA, a dryer that reaches 80 °C saves time. Our [ABS filament guide](/blog/abs-filament/) covers the rest of ABS setup.

## How to use a SUNLU filament dryer: step by step

The steps below apply to every FilaDryer model; the buttons differ, so follow your model's manual for the controls.

1. **Place the dryer on a hard, level surface** away from heat-sensitive objects, in a room between about 20 °C and 35 °C (SUNLU's recommended range for the S2).
2. **Check the spool fits** (diameter and width) and that the filament is wound tightly, so no loose loops touch the heater.
3. **Load the spool** onto the rollers, and if you want to print from the box, thread the filament through the outlet or a PTFE tube before closing the lid.
4. **Close the lid firmly** and keep it closed while drying; opening it lets the heat and damp air mix with the room.
5. **Set the temperature** from the spool label or the table above, or pick the filament preset on models that have one. Do not go above what the spool can take.
6. **Set the time.** As a starting point, use the maker's figure; with no figure, 4–6 hours is typical for a spool that has been open for a while. Add time in a cold room or for a badly soaked spool.
7. **Watch the humidity reading.** It should fall during drying. It shows the air in the box, not the moisture inside the filament, so use it as a trend, not a pass/fail test.
8. **Print or store.** Print straight from the dryer, or move the spool into a sealed bag or box with desiccant once it has cooled.

## Printing while drying with a SUNLU dryer

Printing from the dryer keeps filament from picking up moisture during long prints. The filament leaves through an outlet or a PTFE tube.

- **S2:** SUNLU's store page says it allows printing while drying, with the filament fed out through its outlet.
- **S4:** SUNLU says it can feed up to four printers at the same time.
- **S4 Pro:** ships with two 1 m PTFE tubes for feeding out of the box.
- **S1 Plus, SP2 and E2:** their product pages do not say either way, so check the manual for your unit.

**Tip:** keep the dryer close to the printer. A short, smooth PTFE path avoids extra drag on the extruder, which can cause under-extrusion.

## Common problems and fixes

- **The display never shows the set temperature.** Small, cheap dryers lose heat through the lid, especially in a cold room. Keep the lid shut, move the dryer away from draughts, and give it more time rather than a higher setting.
- **The spool will not turn or squeaks.** The spool is too wide or the rollers are not seated. Check the maximum spool size for your model (85 mm wide for the S2) and refit the rollers.
- **Filament still pops or strings after drying.** It may need a higher temperature than your dryer reaches, or more hours. Compare the setting with the table above, and see the troubleshooting steps in [how to dry filament](/blog/how-to-dry-filament/).
- **The spool warped or filament fused.** The temperature was too high for the spool or the material. Drop back to the label's figure.

## Alternatives to a SUNLU dryer

The same rules apply when you compare any dryer, SUNLU or not:

- **Single-spool dryers** with a 70 °C ceiling suit PLA, PETG and TPU and cost the least.
- **Multi-spool dryers** suit people with several printers or a multi-colour feeder, as long as the spools fit.
- **High-temperature dryers** (90–110 °C) are only worth it for nylon, PC and other engineering filaments.
- **Drying in a feeder** – some multi-material units and add-on heaters dry the spool while it feeds the printer, which saves space if you already own one.
- **An oven** works for occasional drying, with less precise temperature control.

## Verdict

For most people printing PLA, PETG and TPU on one printer, the SUNLU S2 covers SUNLU's own drying settings, shows humidity and lets you print from the box. Pick the SP2 for 2–3 kg spools, the S4 for four spools or several printers, the S4 Pro if you also dry ABS, ASA or PC, and the E2 only if you print nylon. Whichever model you choose, set the temperature from the spool label, keep the lid shut, and store dried filament sealed afterwards.
