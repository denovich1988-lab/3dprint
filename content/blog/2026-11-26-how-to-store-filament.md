**Filament storage comes down to three things: keep each spool sealed, keep the air around it dry with desiccant, and check that humidity so you know when the desiccant is spent.** Most filaments slowly take up water from the air, and wet filament prints with popping, stringing, rough surfaces and weak layers. This guide explains how to store filament at home – which humidity to aim for, the main storage options from resealable bags to gasketed bins and wall racks, how to use desiccant and hygrometers, and how to organise and label a growing collection. Humidity figures and material notes come from the makers' own documentation (Bambu Lab Wiki, Prusa Knowledge Base, Creality), checked November 2026. We do not review or rank individual storage products. If your filament is already wet, start with our guide on [how to dry filament](/blog/how-to-dry-filament/).

## Filament storage at a glance

| Option | Keeps moisture out | Best for | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original resealable bag + desiccant | Good if the seal is intact | A few spools, short breaks | Very low |
| Vacuum bag + desiccant | Very good | Long-term storage, sensitive filaments | Medium (pump each time) |
| Airtight bin with gasket lid | Very good with enough desiccant | Several spools in one place | Low |
| Dry box that feeds the printer | Good to very good | Printing TPU, nylon or PVA straight from storage | Medium |
| Multi-material unit (AMS, CFS and similar) | Fair to good, depends on seals and desiccant | Spools that are loaded and in use | Low |
| Open rack or shelf | None | PLA you use often, in a dry room | Very low |
| Dehumidified cabinet or closet | Good if the room is controlled | Large collections | High |

## Why filament storage matters

Most common filaments are hygroscopic: they absorb water vapour from the air. Prusa's knowledge base lists the signs of wet filament as a poorer surface, blobs, low layer adhesion, bubbling and even visible steam during extrusion, plus more stringing. Bambu Lab's AMS documentation names PLA, PETG and PA (nylon) as hygroscopic materials worth monitoring.

How quickly it happens depends on the material and the room. Bambu Lab's wiki says typical indoor air is usually 45–65% RH and higher in humid weather. For moisture-sensitive filaments, it says freshly dried spools stay dry for about 2–7 days at 20% RH but can become damp enough to hurt print quality within 2–12 hours at 55% RH. That gap is why sealed storage works: you are not stopping moisture forever, you are slowing it down so a dried spool stays printable for weeks instead of hours.

**Tip:** drying fixes a wet spool, storage keeps it from getting wet again. Dry first, then seal – putting a damp spool in a box with desiccant only dries it very slowly, if at all.

## What humidity should filament be stored at?

There is no single number that every maker agrees on, but the direction is clear: lower is better.

- **Moisture-sensitive filaments (nylon and nylon-CF):** Bambu Lab's wiki prefers less than 20% RH inside a sealed box and recommends adding a thermo-hygrometer to watch it.
- **PLA and PETG:** Creality's storage guide calls them forgiving and says a basic airtight container or dry box is usually enough between sessions.
- **TPU, nylon, PC and PVA:** Creality and Prusa both single these out as more moisture-sensitive; Prusa notes that polyamide, PVA and TPU need drying more often than PLA.

If your bin reads close to your room's humidity, the desiccant is spent or the seal is leaking.

**Tip:** treat 20% RH as the goal for sensitive filaments and anything clearly below room humidity as acceptable for PLA and PETG. Watch the trend over a few days rather than a single reading.

## Filament storage box options

A filament storage box is the most common home setup because it holds several spools, needs no pump and is easy to check.

- **Clear plastic bins with a gasket and clip-down lid.** Look for a continuous rubber or silicone seal and latches on every side. Clear sides let you see colours and read a hygrometer without opening the lid. Measure one of your spools and the inside of the bin before buying: spool sizes vary between brands, and the lid must close without pressing on them.
- **Cereal or food containers.** Single-spool containers with a seal work well for one open spool next to the printer. Some people add a PTFE tube fitting to print straight from the box.
- **Purpose-made dry boxes.** Filament makers and printer makers sell boxes with built-in hygrometers, desiccant compartments and spool rollers. Prusa's knowledge base mentions its own dry box as one option; Bambu Lab sells a sealed box with rollers that its wiki says reduce loading resistance, which helps flexible filaments such as TPU.
- **Toolboxes and storage drawers.** Only useful if they actually seal – most do not.

**Tip:** test a box before trusting it. Put a hygrometer and fresh desiccant inside, close it, and check after a day or two. A good box drops well below room humidity and stays there.

## Vacuum bags and resealable bags

Many filaments ship in a sealed bag with a desiccant pack. Keeping that bag and resealing it is the simplest form of storage. Prusa calls vacuum bags another popular solution, says any bag that fits a spool will work, and advises keeping a desiccant inside.

- **Vacuum bags with a valve** take the most air out and pack flat, which suits spools you will not touch for months. Use a hand or electric pump made for that bag type.
- **Large zip bags** are cheap and fast. Squeeze out the air and add a desiccant pack.
- **Original bags** are fine until the zip wears out; then move the spool to a new bag.

**Tip:** desiccant packs can puncture thin bags under vacuum. Wrap the pack in a paper towel or place it inside the spool hub.

## Desiccant: types, colours and recharging

Desiccant does the real work inside any sealed container.

- **Silica gel** is the usual choice. Indicating silica changes colour as it absorbs water. Bambu Lab's AMS guide, for example, tells users to replace its silica desiccant when it turns from fresh yellow to dark green.
- **Calcium chloride** absorbs a lot of water but turns to liquid when saturated. Bambu Lab warns against it in the AMS because saturated calcium chloride can release water and damage electronics. Keep it away from printers and multi-material units.
- **Quantity:** use enough desiccant for the container. Sizing rules vary between desiccant makers, so follow the rating printed on the pack. Too little desiccant in a big bin is a common reason the humidity never drops.
- **Recharging:** most silica gel can be dried and reused. Follow the drying temperature and time given by the desiccant's maker, and check that its container can take the heat.

**Tip:** put desiccant in breathable canisters or mesh pouches rather than loose beads, so they do not end up in the gears of a feeder.

## Hygrometers: know what is happening inside

A small digital hygrometer turns storage from guesswork into a routine. Bambu Lab recommends one for nylon-type filaments, and many dry boxes include one.

- Place one in each bin, or at least in the bin with your most sensitive filaments.
- Cheap hygrometers can disagree by several percent. To compare readings, put two side by side in the same box.
- Multi-material units often have their own sensors. Bambu Lab's AMS shows humidity as levels A–E and says that at level C or above the chamber is fairly humid, so check the seals and replace the desiccant.

**Tip:** write the date you last refreshed the desiccant on a piece of tape on the lid. When the humidity starts to climb, you will know how long the desiccant lasted in your room.

## Filament storage ideas: racks, shelves and cabinets

Open storage is not dry storage, but it has its place.

- **Wall racks and pegboard spool holders** are fine for PLA you print every day in a dry room, and they make colours easy to pick. Keep them away from windows, because sunlight and heat can fade and warp spools.
- **Shelves under the printer** are a natural home for sealed bins. See our [3D printer stand guide](/blog/3d-printer-stand/) for stands with spool shelves.
- **Closed cabinets with a dehumidifier** suit large collections. The cabinet itself does not need to be airtight if the room or cabinet humidity is controlled and monitored.
- **Multi-material units** such as the Bambu Lab AMS or Creality CFS hold the spools you are actively using and many keep desiccant inside. Treat them as working storage, not long-term storage, and keep an eye on their humidity readings. Our explainer on [what an AMS is](/blog/what-is-ams-in-3d-printing/) covers how they work.

**Tip:** keep spools away from heaters and hot cars. Bambu Lab's material guide says PLA is not recommended for parts used around 50 °C or higher because it softens; spools left somewhere that hot can deform too.

## Organising and labelling your filament

A tidy system saves time and stops half-used spools from being forgotten.

1. **Sort by material first, then colour.** Keep PLA, PETG, TPU and engineering filaments in separate bins, because they need different levels of care.
2. **Label every bin** with the material and colours inside. Clear bins plus a label on the short side work best on a shelf.
3. **Label every spool** with the brand, material, colour, diameter and date opened if the original label is missing. Some makers' spools carry RFID tags read by their own systems; a paper label still helps when you use another printer.
4. **Note the last drying date** on spools of nylon, TPU and PVA.
5. **Keep remnants together.** Small leftovers are useful for test prints and supports for single-colour parts.

## A short note on drying

Storage slows moisture down; it does not undo it. If a spool already pops, strings or prints with a rough surface, dry it before you store it. Drying temperatures and times depend on the material, and they are covered step by step in our guide on [how to dry filament](/blog/how-to-dry-filament/) and in our [filament dryer guide](/blog/filament-dryer/). Some dryers double as storage boxes and can print from the spool while heating.

## How to store filament – step-by-step

1. **Check the spool.** If it is new and still sealed, leave it sealed until you need it. If it is open and printing badly, dry it first.
2. **Choose the container** by how often you use the spool: rack for daily PLA, bin or bag for everything else, dry box for TPU, nylon and PVA.
3. **Add fresh desiccant** – enough for the container's size – in a canister or pouch.
4. **Add a hygrometer** to each bin, or at least to the one with sensitive filaments.
5. **Close the seal fully** and check every latch or zip.
6. **Label the bin and the spool** with material, colour and dates.
7. **Store the container somewhere cool and shaded**, off the floor and away from heaters, windows and the printer's warm exhaust.
8. **Check humidity weekly.** When it climbs towards room humidity, recharge or replace the desiccant and check the seal.

## Verdict

The best filament storage is the one you will actually keep sealed. For most people that means a clear gasketed bin or vacuum bags with enough silica desiccant, a cheap hygrometer in each container, and an open rack only for the PLA you print every day. Aim for less than 20% RH for nylon and other sensitive filaments, keep PLA and PETG well below room humidity, label everything, and dry any spool that already shows signs of moisture before it goes back into storage.
