**A 3D printed dragon egg is a printed egg covered in dragon scales – it can be a solid ornament, an egg that opens to reveal a baby dragon, a print-in-place hinged egg, a box with a hidden compartment or a glowing lamp.** Dragon eggs are a favourite fantasy print because they look impressive on a shelf, make easy gifts and show off silk and multi-colour filament better than almost anything else. This guide explains the main types of 3D printed dragon eggs, the slicer settings that keep hinges and threads working, filament choices, finishing and gift ideas. Settings are based on printing guides from printer makers and model libraries, checked November 2026.

## 3D printed dragon egg at a glance

| Type | What it is | Supports | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid scaled egg | A display egg with sculpted scales | Usually none; brim or stand for the base | Easy |
| Two-part egg with dragon | Shell splits, small dragon inside | Depends on the dragon; sometimes pre-supported | Easy to medium |

## Types of 3D printed dragon eggs

### Solid scaled eggs

The simplest type: an egg-shaped model with overlapping scales, printed in one piece. They print quickly, need no assembly and are perfect for trying out a new silk or gradient filament. Some designs come with a separate stand, claw or nest so the egg does not roll.

### Opening eggs with a dragon inside

Two-part eggs split along a jagged "cracked" line, with a tiny dragon printed separately or as part of the lower shell. All3DP's roundup of dragon models includes this type, noting that the hatchling can be printed on its own or attached to the bottom half. These make great surprise gifts.

### Print-in-place articulated eggs

These eggs are made of hinged segments that print already connected. When you push the segments, they fold open like petals or armour plates to show what is inside – often a small dragon or an empty space for a gift. Because the hinges are printed with tiny gaps, they need careful printing (see the settings below).

### Flexi dragons with eggs

Many articulated "flexi" dragon designs come with a matching egg that the dragon curls up inside – on model libraries such as MyMiniFactory you will find several "dragon & egg" print-in-place sets. If you enjoy these, our [3D printed dragon guide](/blog/3d-printed-dragon/) covers articulated dragons in detail.

### Box, threaded and hidden-compartment eggs

These eggs have a screw-on or lift-off top, turning the egg into a box for jewellery, dice, sweets or a gift card. Threaded designs need accurate printing so the threads engage smoothly.

### Lamp and lithophane eggs

Dragon egg lamps are thin shells designed to be lit from inside by an LED tea light or LED strip, so the scales glow. Some use a lithophane effect, where thinner areas let more light through. You can make your own flat or curved panels with our [lithophane maker](/lithophane-maker/), and our [lithophane guide](/blog/lithophane/) explains thickness and lighting.

**Tip:** always use LED lights inside printed eggs. PLA softens at fairly low temperatures, and a hot bulb or real candle can warp the shell or start a fire.

### Nesting and miniature eggs

Sets of eggs that split into halves and nest inside each other, or tiny eggs sized for tabletop games, desk decorations and party favours.

## Where to find dragon egg models

Model libraries such as Printables, MakerWorld, Thingiverse, Cults and MyMiniFactory list hundreds of dragon eggs. Search for "dragon egg", "print-in-place dragon egg", "dragon egg box" or "dragon egg lamp".

- **Read the print notes.** Designers usually state layer height, supports and the scale they tested.
- **Check the makes.** Photos from other people show whether hinges open on ordinary printers.
- **Check the licence.** Many designs are for personal use; only sell prints of models whose licence allows it.
- **Check the size.** Open the file in our STL viewer to see its height in millimetres and how many parts it has.

## How print-in-place dragon egg hinges work

A print-in-place hinge is a pin inside a socket with a small gap all round. The printer builds both at once, and the gap keeps them from fusing. Prusa recommends a clearance of at least 0.3 mm for movable parts, and All3DP gives about 0.3 mm as a value that suits most printers.

**Why it matters:** the gap is part of the model, not a slicer setting. Anything that fills it – a squashed first layer, over-extrusion, stringing, supports or scaling the model down – will lock the hinge.

## Print settings for a 3D printed dragon egg

| Setting | Suggested starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | 100% for print-in-place and threaded eggs | Scaling down shrinks hinge gaps and thread clearances |
| Layer height | 0.12–0.2 mm | Thinner layers show scales better and keep gaps open |
| Supports | Off for print-in-place; follow the notes for sculpted dragons | Supports jam hinges |
| First layer | Slow, not over-squished | Most fused hinges start at the first layer |
| Elephant foot compensation | On | Keeps the first layer out of the gaps |
| Brim or stand | Brim for upright eggs with a small contact area | Stops a tall egg from tipping |
| Walls | 2–3; more for lamp shells only if the designer suggests it | Thin shells let light through on lamps |
| Seam | Aligned at the back or hidden in a scale edge | Keeps the front clean |

Bambu Studio has **Elephant foot compensation** under Process → Quality, and other slicers have an equivalent setting.

### Orientation

- **Solid eggs** usually print upright on a small flat base. The egg's curve is a gentle overhang, so it rarely needs supports, but the small footprint benefits from a brim.
- **Two-part eggs** print each half with the cut face down, which gives a flat, stable base and no supports.
- **Print-in-place eggs** must print in the orientation the designer chose; rotating them can put hinges in a position that needs supports.

**Tip:** if you rescale a solid egg, that is fine – it has no moving parts. Only keep print-in-place, hinged and threaded eggs at 100%.

## Best filament for a 3D printed dragon egg

- **PLA** – the easy default; prints scales sharply. Our PLA filament guide covers settings.
- **Silk PLA** – a glossy, metallic sheen that makes scales look like polished metal. It can be a little weaker, so open hinges gently.
- **Gradient and rainbow PLA** – colours shift from the base to the tip without any colour changes.
- **Dual- and tri-colour (coextruded) silk** – shows different colours depending on the viewing angle, which suits scale patterns.
- **Multi-colour printing** – printers with automatic filament systems can colour scales, the hatchling and the shell separately. See our guide to [multi-colour 3D printers](/blog/multi-color-3d-printer/).
- **Glow-in-the-dark PLA** – glows after charging under light. QIDI notes glow filament is abrasive and recommends a hardened steel nozzle; it also suggests thicker walls (3–4 perimeters) for a brighter glow.
- **Translucent PLA or PETG** – good for lamp eggs, as light passes through the shell.

## Finishing a dragon egg

- **Remove brim and stringing** with flush cutters or a hobby knife.
- **Free hinges** by moving each segment gently, a little at a time, before opening the egg fully.
- **Dry brush** a lighter metallic paint over a dark base to pick out the edges of the scales.
- **Wash** with thinned dark paint to deepen the gaps between scales.
- **Gild** scale tips with metallic paint or rub-on wax for an "ancient treasure" look.
- **Seal** painted eggs with a clear coat.
- **Add a stand** – a printed claw, nest or ring stops the egg rolling.

## How to print a dragon egg, step by step

1. **Choose a model** with good makes and clear notes, and check its licence.
2. **Check the size** in a viewer and decide whether it fits your bed.
3. **Slice** at 100% for hinged or threaded eggs, with supports only if the designer asks for them.
4. **Turn on elephant foot compensation** and add a brim for upright eggs.
5. **Load your filament** – silk, gradient, multi-colour or glow.
6. **Watch the first layer**; it decides whether hinges move.
7. **Let the plate cool**, then remove the egg in one piece.
8. **Free the hinges** gently, then paint, seal or add a light if you like.

## Troubleshooting dragon egg prints

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hinges fused | Elephant's foot, over-extrusion, scaled down | Elephant foot compensation, calibrate flow, print at 100% |
| Egg tips over mid-print | Small contact area | Brim, slower first layer, or a version with a flat base |
| Thread will not screw on | Over-extrusion or scaled model | Print at 100%, calibrate flow, clean the threads with a knife |
| Stringing between scales | Damp filament or low retraction | Dry the filament and tune retraction |
| Glow is dim | Thin walls, weak charge | More walls; charge under bright or UV light |
| Silk egg cracks when opened | Weaker layer bonding | Slightly higher nozzle temperature, open gently |

## Safety notes

Small eggs, hatchlings and hinge pieces can be choking hazards, so keep them away from young children and pets. Use only LED lights inside lamp eggs, never candles or hot bulbs.

## Verdict

A 3D printed dragon egg is one of the most rewarding fantasy prints: a solid scaled egg is a perfect first try, while print-in-place and hidden-box eggs make memorable gifts. Keep moving designs at their original size, print without supports, get the first layer right and choose silk, gradient or glow filament to make the scales stand out.
