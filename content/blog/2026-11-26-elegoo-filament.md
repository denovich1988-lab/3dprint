**Elegoo filament is the 1.75 mm FDM filament range sold by Elegoo, the maker of the Neptune and Centauri printers, covering standard PLA, PLA+, PLA PRO, high-speed Rapid PLA+ and Rapid PETG, flexible TPU, ASA and a long list of decorative and fibre-filled grades.** It is sold mainly in 1 kg cardboard spools, with bulk packs and small 250 g bundles as well. This guide explains what each Elegoo line is for, the print settings Elegoo itself publishes, how the spools fit multi-colour systems such as CANVAS and the Bambu Lab AMS, and how to keep the filament dry. Settings come from the specification blocks on Elegoo's own product pages, checked November 2026; where Elegoo publishes no value, we leave it out. We have not tested Elegoo filament and we do not compare prices. The label on your spool always comes first.

## What filament does Elegoo make?

Elegoo's filament pages group the range into beginner, aesthetic and functional filaments, and by material: PLA, PETG, TPU and PC, ASA and ABS, and fibre-reinforced grades. In practice the lines fall into five groups:

| Group | Main grades | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday PLA | PLA, PLA Basic, PLA+, PLA PRO | First prints, models, household parts |
| High-speed | Rapid PLA+, Rapid PETG, Rapid TPU 95A | Fast bed slingers and CoreXY printers |
| Engineering and functional | PETG PRO, PETG Translucent, TPU 95A, ASA, ABS, PC | Heat, weather or impact resistance, flexible parts |
| Fibre-filled | PLA-CF, PETG-CF and other reinforced grades | Stiff, matte functional parts |
| Aesthetic | Matte, Silk, Galaxy, Sparkle, Marble, Metallic, Wood, Glow, CMYK | Display models, gifts, colour mixing |

Elegoo also sells PLA in bulk – 3 kg, 5 kg and 10 kg packs – plus mini 250 g multi-colour bundles, a PLA Matte version on a reusable spool and RFID-tagged PLA.

**Why it matters:** the names overlap with other brands, but they are Elegoo's own definitions. Elegoo PLA PRO, for example, is a stiffer grade printed cooler than Elegoo PLA+, not a tougher one.

## Elegoo PLA, PLA+ and PLA PRO settings

These are the grades most people mean by Elegoo filament. Here is what Elegoo's specification blocks say:

| Grade | Nozzle | Bed | Speed | Heat deflection | Elegoo's description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLA | 190–220 °C (205 °C recommended) | 50–65 °C | 30–70 mm/s | – | Low warping, easy to print |
| PLA+ | 200–230 °C (215 °C recommended) | 35–65 °C | 30–70 mm/s | 53 °C (0.45 MPa) | Tougher, higher impact strength than PLA |
| PLA PRO | 180–210 °C (200 °C recommended) | 50–65 °C | 30–250 mm/s | 57 °C (0.45 MPa) | More rigid and stiffer than PLA |

The 30–70 mm/s speed for standard PLA and PLA+ is conservative. Many current printers print far faster; if yours does, raise the nozzle towards the top of the range and check the result rather than jumping straight to the printer's maximum speed.

**Tip:** PLA+ is a brand term, not a standard, so Elegoo PLA+ is not the same as another brand's PLA+. Our [PLA vs PLA+ guide](/blog/pla-vs-pla-plus/) explains what usually changes and when the tougher grade is worth it.

## Elegoo Rapid PLA+ and Rapid PETG

The Rapid line is made for high-speed printing.

- **Rapid PLA+** – Elegoo rates it for speeds of up to 600 mm/s and mentions 250 mm/s on its Neptune 4 series. Diameter accuracy is ±0.02 mm. Elegoo's product page publishes no temperature range for it, so start from the spool label.
- **Rapid PETG** – Elegoo lists 220–250 °C at the nozzle (230 °C recommended), a 75–90 °C bed, 30–600 mm/s and the part-cooling fan at 100 %. Its heat deflection temperature is 62 °C (0.45 MPa), higher than Elegoo's PLA grades.

The speed figures are maximums. Whether you can reach them depends on how much plastic your hotend can melt per second, not on the filament alone. If a fast print looks dull, rough or under-extruded, the hotend has run out of melting capacity: slow down or raise the temperature within the range.

**Tip:** Elegoo's Rapid PETG wants a hotter bed (75–90 °C) than many generic PETG profiles. If first layers lift, check the bed temperature before anything else. Our [PETG filament guide](/blog/petg-filament/) covers bed surfaces and stringing.

## Elegoo TPU 95A and ASA

- **TPU 95A** – 210–230 °C at the nozzle (220 °C recommended), a 35–60 °C bed and 20–40 mm/s. Flexible filament needs slow, steady feeding; Elegoo also sells a Rapid TPU 95A for faster printing.
- **ASA** – 240–270 °C at the nozzle, a 90–100 °C bed, 30–50 mm/s and the cooling fan off. Elegoo says it cannot be printed on open printers, so use an enclosed machine such as the Centauri Carbon.

**Why it matters:** ASA and ABS give off more fumes than PLA and warp in cool air. An enclosure and a ventilated room are part of printing them, not an extra.

## Fibre-filled and decorative Elegoo filament

**Fibre-filled grades** such as PLA-CF and PETG-CF add short carbon or glass fibres for a stiff, matte finish. Fibres wear brass nozzles quickly – Bambu Lab's material table lists a hardened nozzle as required for carbon-fibre PLA – so use a hardened steel nozzle. Elegoo's Centauri Carbon printers ship with one.

**Decorative grades** – Silk, Galaxy, Sparkle, Marble, Metallic, Wood, Glow in the Dark and the CMYK set for colour mixing – are PLA-based. Silk and multi-colour filament generally look best at moderate speeds with slower outer walls, which keeps the shine and the colour transitions even.

**Tip:** wood and glow-in-the-dark filaments contain particles that can catch in a 0.2 mm nozzle. Use a 0.4 mm nozzle or larger.

## Elegoo cardboard spools and filament rings

Elegoo winds most of its filament on cardboard spools. Its product pages describe full mechanical winding with manual inspection to reduce tangles, give the empty spool weight as 154 ± 10 g, and note three holes in the side so you can see how much filament is left.

Cardboard spools have one weak point: they can slip or rub in feeders built for plastic spools. Elegoo's answer is a printable **filament ring** that fits over the cardboard rim. It recommends printing the ring in PETG, and if it fits too tightly, changing the slicer's X-Y hole and contour compensation to 0.1.

**Tip:** knowing the empty spool weight (about 154 g) lets you weigh a part-used spool and work out how much filament is left before a long print.

## Elegoo filament in CANVAS, the AMS and other printers

**CANVAS** is Elegoo's four-colour system for the Centauri Carbon and Centauri Carbon 2. Elegoo says its RFID-tagged filaments are recognised automatically, with recommended settings for the type and colour loaded for you. Other spools still work – you set the material and colour on the touchscreen. CANVAS lists PLA, ABS, ASA, PETG and PC, and the Centauri Carbon 2 version's spool holder takes spools with a 53–58 mm centre hole. See our [Centauri Carbon 2 page](/printers/elegoo-centauri-carbon-2/) for how CANVAS mounts and feeds.

**Bambu Lab AMS and AMS lite:** Elegoo filament is ordinary 1.75 mm filament, so it prints in Bambu Lab printers with a generic or Elegoo profile; the RFID tags on Elegoo spools are not read by Bambu's units. The AMS lite holds spools by the centre hole, which Bambu Lab says improves compatibility with cardboard spools, and it takes spools 40–68 mm wide with a 53–58 mm hole. Our [Bambu filament guide](/blog/bambu-filament/) explains how Bambu's own tagged spools differ.

**Other printers:** Elegoo describes its filament as compatible with most 1.75 mm FDM printers. Pick an Elegoo profile in your slicer if it has one, or start from the generic profile for the material and adjust it to the label.

## Drying and storing Elegoo filament

Elegoo dries its filament before packing and vacuum-seals it with desiccant, so a freshly opened spool usually prints well. Elegoo's product pages give no drying temperature for individual grades (checked November 2026), so use the value on your spool label or the general maker guidance – roughly 45–60 °C for PLA and 55–70 °C for PETG, longer and hotter for TPU and nylon. Our [guide to drying filament](/blog/how-to-dry-filament/) has a full table by material.

Elegoo also makes a filament dryer, the **H1 HT**. Elegoo says it heats to 85 °C, has 11 preset filament modes, rotates the spool while drying, vents moisture and has a bypass outlet for TPU and fibre-filled filament. All3DP reports that it takes one 1 kg spool up to 68 mm wide.

**Signs a spool needs drying:** popping or hissing at the nozzle, more stringing than usual, rough or bubbly walls, and PLA that snaps when you bend it.

## Is Elegoo filament good? How to judge it

We do not test filament, so we will not give Elegoo a score. From its published information:

- **It publishes core settings for each grade**: nozzle and bed range, a recommended temperature, speed range and, for several grades, heat deflection temperature.
- **It lists a tight diameter tolerance** of ±0.02 mm on its main PLA, PLA+ and PETG pages.
- **Some pages leave gaps.** The Rapid PLA+ page has no temperature range, and no product page gives a drying temperature. Third-party databases fill some of these gaps, but their values do not always match each other, so trust the spool label first.

A brand comparison only makes sense grade by grade:

| Compare | What to check |
|---|---|
| Diameter tolerance | A smaller ± figure means steadier extrusion |
| Nozzle and bed range | Shows whether a generic profile will work |
| Speed rating | A maximum, limited by your hotend |
| Heat deflection temperature | How warm a part can get before it softens |
| Spool type and size | Matters for multi-colour feeders and dryers |

For a similar brand overview, see our SUNLU and Bambu filament guides.

## How to set up Elegoo filament: checklist

1. **Read the label.** The temperature range printed on the spool beats any generic value, including those in this guide.
2. **Pick a profile.** Use your slicer's Elegoo preset if it has one; otherwise the generic profile for the material.
3. **Start at Elegoo's recommended temperature** – 205 °C for PLA, 215 °C for PLA+, 200 °C for PLA PRO, 230 °C for Rapid PETG, 220 °C for TPU 95A.
4. **Set the bed.** 50–65 °C for PLA and PLA PRO, 35–65 °C for PLA+, 75–90 °C for Rapid PETG, 90–100 °C for ASA.
5. **Fit a filament ring** if your feeder struggles with cardboard spools.
6. **Print a temperature tower** and a small test part before a long job, especially at high speed.
7. **Use a hardened nozzle** for fibre-filled grades and an enclosure for ASA and ABS.
8. **Store spools sealed** with desiccant, and dry them if they start popping or stringing.

Before printing a functional part, check its size and wall thickness in our STL viewer.

## Verdict

Elegoo filament covers the full range most home users need: standard PLA, PLA+ and stiffer PLA PRO for everyday prints, Rapid PLA+ and Rapid PETG for fast printers, TPU and ASA for flexible and outdoor parts, plus decorative and fibre-filled grades. Elegoo publishes nozzle, bed and speed values for its main grades, lists ±0.02 mm diameter accuracy, and adds RFID recognition on its CANVAS system. The main things to plan for are the cardboard spools in some feeders and the missing drying values. Start from the label, print a test, and keep the spools dry.
