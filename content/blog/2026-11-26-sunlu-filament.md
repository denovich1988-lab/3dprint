Facts come from SUNLU's own wiki, product pages and company page, checked November 2026. We have not tested SUNLU filament, and we do not compare prices. The label on your spool always comes first.

## Who SUNLU is

SUNLU's company page says it was founded in July 2013, has its headquarters in China and a branch office in Germany, and makes 3D filament, resin and filament dryers. It lists two main production bases in China – in Anhui and Guangdong – and a base in Vietnam that started operating in August 2024.

**Why it matters:** SUNLU makes its own filament rather than relabelling someone else's, and it publishes a wiki with print parameters for each grade. That makes it easy to set up a profile without guessing.

## SUNLU's filament lines

SUNLU groups its filament into five families on its wiki. Here is what each contains:

| Family | Main grades | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| General | PLA, PLA+, PLA+ 2.0, APLA, PLA Classic, PLA Lite, PETG, PETG 2.0, ABS, PCL | Everyday prints and functional parts |
| High-speed | High Speed PLA, PLA+, PLA+ 2.0, Matte PLA, Marble PLA, Matte PETG, ABS | Fast CoreXY printers |
| Esthetic | Silk PLA+ (single to quad colour), Matte, Rainbow, Galaxy, Twinkling, Wood, Metallic, Glow in the Dark, Transparent | Decorative prints, gifts, display models |
| Functional | LW-PLA, PLA-CF, PLA Meta, PVB, ASA, PETG-CF, TPU 90A and 95A, Silk TPU, ABS-FR, ABS-GF | Outdoor, flexible, lightweight or fibre-filled parts |
| Engineering | PP, PP 2.0, Easy PA, PA6-CF, PA6-GF, PA12-CF, PC, PC-ABS, PEEK | Technical parts on enclosed, high-temperature printers |

PCL is a special case: a low-melt plastic (melting point 60–65 °C) printed at 130–150 °C, mainly used in 3D printing pens.

## SUNLU PLA, PLA+ and PLA+ 2.0

These are the grades most people mean when they search for SUNLU filament.

- **SUNLU PLA** – the entry-level grade. SUNLU's wiki lists 185–230 °C at the nozzle, a 50–60 °C bed and 50 °C for drying, and warns to keep it away from strong acids, bases, acetone and sunlight.
- **SUNLU PLA+** – a toughened PLA. The wiki gives 205–245 °C, a 50–60 °C bed, 0.1–0.3 % shrinkage and a heat deflection temperature of 56 ± 3 °C. The product page splits the nozzle range by speed: 205–215 °C at 50–100 mm/s and 215–245 °C at 100–200 mm/s, and lists ±0.02 mm diameter accuracy.
- **SUNLU PLA+ 2.0** – a newer PLA+ that SUNLU describes as two to three times tougher than its PLA+, with the same 205–245 °C nozzle and 50–60 °C bed.
- **APLA, PLA Classic and PLA Lite** – other PLA grades for different finishes or speeds. SUNLU lists PLA Classic and PLA Lite with a matte finish, 200–260 °C and speeds up to 450 mm/s, and notes they are not heat-resistant.

**Tip:** PLA+ is a brand-defined name, not a standard, so SUNLU PLA+ is not identical to another brand's PLA+. Our [PLA vs PLA+ guide](/blog/pla-vs-pla-plus/) explains what changes and when the tougher grade is worth it. For the material itself, see our general [PLA filament guide](/blog/pla-filament/).

## SUNLU PETG and ABS

- **SUNLU PETG** – 240–260 °C nozzle, 50–70 °C bed, cooling fan on, drying at 60–65 °C for 4–6 hours if the spool has been open to the air for more than 48 hours. SUNLU notes it resists water and chemicals but not acetone.
- **SUNLU PETG 2.0** – 230–240 °C at 50–100 mm/s or 240–270 °C at 100–200 mm/s, with a 70–80 °C bed. SUNLU describes it as absorbing less moisture and stringing less than its standard PETG.
- **SUNLU ABS** – 250–290 °C nozzle, 80–100 °C bed, fan off, dry at 80 °C for 2–4 hours. SUNLU advises good ventilation.

**Why it matters:** SUNLU's PETG range starts hotter than some brands' – if your slicer's generic PETG profile runs at 230 °C and you see weak layers, raise it into SUNLU's range.

## High-speed, silk and specialty lines

**High-speed grades** are made for fast printers. SUNLU's wiki lists High Speed PLA at up to 250 mm/s, High Speed Matte PLA up to 450 mm/s and High Speed PLA+ 2.0 at 300–600 mm/s, all on a 50–60 °C bed. Only run these speeds if your printer can actually melt that much plastic.

**Silk and multi-colour grades** need the opposite: SUNLU recommends lower speeds (typically 50–200 mm/s) and slower outer walls to keep the gloss and colour transitions even. Silk PLA+ prints at 210–230 °C.

**Other specialty grades** from SUNLU's wiki:

| Grade | Nozzle | Bed | SUNLU's note |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLA Wood | 190–220 °C | 40–50 °C | Print slowly to keep the grain |
| PLA Glow in the Dark | 200–240 °C | 50–60 °C | Brittle; SUNLU advises avoiding the AMS |
| LW-PLA | 180–200 °C | 50–60 °C | Print slowly; for model aircraft and boats |
| PLA-CF | 215–245 °C | 50–60 °C | Keep dry; avoid sanding dust |
| TPU 95A | 190–230 °C | 50–60 °C | Print slowly, reduce retraction |
| ASA | 250–280 °C | 80–100 °C | Weather and UV resistant |

Carbon- and glass-fibre filled grades wear brass nozzles. SUNLU notes this for PETG-CF and ABS-GF and recommends a hardened nozzle for PA6-CF and PA12-CF; Bambu Lab's material table also lists a hardened nozzle as required for PLA-CF.

## Is SUNLU filament good? How to judge it

We do not test filament, so we will not give SUNLU a score. What we can say from its published information:

- **It publishes data.** Each grade on SUNLU's wiki has nozzle, bed, speed, drying and retraction values, and some product pages list diameter tolerance and mechanical properties.
- **It covers almost every material type**, from basic PLA to PEEK, so you can stay with one brand's documentation as you move to harder materials.
- **Its pages sometimes disagree with each other.** For example, the PLA+ product page gives two different impact strength figures, and some high-speed pages quote higher top speeds in the feature list than in the parameter table. Use the parameter table, and treat headline speeds as maximums.

The best test is your own: print a temperature tower and a small functional part, and compare with the brand you use now.

## SUNLU vs other filament brands

Questions such as "Is SUNLU better than Creality?" or "Is SUNLU the same as Bambu?" come up a lot. The honest answer is that a brand comparison only makes sense grade by grade:

| Compare | What to check |
|---|---|
| Diameter tolerance | A smaller ± figure means more consistent extrusion |
| Print temperature range | Tells you whether a generic profile will work |
| Heat deflection temperature | Shows how warm a part can get before it softens |
| Drying temperature and time | A sign of how moisture-sensitive the grade is |
| Spool size and material | Matters if you use a multi-material feeder or a dryer with a size limit |

SUNLU and Bambu Lab are different companies. Bambu Lab's own spools carry an RFID tag that the AMS reads automatically; third-party spools such as SUNLU's are set up by hand with a profile you pick. Our [Bambu filament guide](/blog/bambu-filament/) explains Bambu's own lines.

## Setting up SUNLU filament: checklist

1. **Read the label.** The temperature range on the spool beats any generic value, including the ones in this guide.
2. **Pick a profile.** Use your slicer's SUNLU profile if it has one, otherwise the generic profile for the material (Generic PLA, Generic PETG, and so on).
3. **Match the speed band.** For PLA+, PLA+ 2.0 and PETG 2.0, SUNLU gives a cooler range for 50–100 mm/s and a hotter range for 100–200 mm/s.
4. **Set the bed.** 50–60 °C for most PLA grades, 50–80 °C for PETG, 80–100 °C for ABS and ASA.
5. **Print a temperature tower** and a small test part before a long job.
6. **Dry when needed** – 50 °C for SUNLU PLA grades, 60–65 °C for PETG, 80 °C for ABS.
7. **Store spools sealed.** SUNLU recommends keeping PLA+ below 20 % relative humidity with desiccant while printing.

## Verdict

SUNLU filament is a broad, well-documented range: basic PLA, PLA+ and PETG for everyday printing, high-speed grades for fast printers, silk and matte grades for looks, and engineering grades up to PEEK. Its own wiki gives settings for every grade, and its FilaDryer range covers drying from PLA up to nylon. Treat any brand – SUNLU included – the same way: start from the spool label, print a test, keep the filament dry.
