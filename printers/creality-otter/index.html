Creality Otter 3D scanner – specs, setup and printing scans

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • The Creality Otter (CR-Scan Otter) is a handheld 3D scanner that uses four-lens stereo vision and infrared structured light, not a 3D printer.
  • Creality quotes accuracy up to 0.02 mm, 24-bit colour, objects from 10 mm to 2 m, and scanning of black and metal surfaces without spray.
  • It launched in April 2024 at a US$899 pre-sale price and exports OBJ, STL and PLY files from CrealityScan.

The Creality Otter is a handheld 3D scanner – sold as the CR-Scan Otter – that turns real objects into colour 3D models using four-lens stereo vision and infrared structured light. Creality launched it in April 2024 at a US$899 pre-sale price on its US store, for makers who want to scan parts, props or people and then edit or 3D print the result. This page covers specs, setup, the Otter Lite and how to get a scan ready for printing.

What the Otter is good at

Creality and launch coverage highlight three things: it scans black and metallic surfaces without spray, it works in sunlight up to 30,000 lux, and it covers both small parts and large objects such as furniture or car parts. Colour capture makes it useful for figurines and props where texture matters.

Tip: tiny, shiny or transparent objects are still the hardest for any structured-light scanner. Matte objects with some surface detail scan fastest.

Who the Creality Otter suits

The Otter suits hobbyists and small studios who scan medium to large objects – cosplay pieces, sculptures, body scans, replacement parts – and print them. If you mainly scan very small, finely detailed parts, look at Creality's blue-laser models in our Creality 3D scanner guide.

Setting up the Creality Otter

At a high level:

  1. Install CrealityScan on a Windows or macOS computer.
  2. Connect the scanner to the computer with the supplied cable.
  3. Follow CrealityScan's on-screen prompts to set up a new scan for your object's size.
  4. Move the scanner slowly and steadily around the object so each pass overlaps the last.
  5. Process the scan in CrealityScan, then export it.

Creality publishes the "Instruction for Otter" guide on the Creality Wiki under 3D Scanner → CR-Scan Otter.

Otter vs Otter Lite

The Otter Lite, announced in 2025, is a cheaper version. All3DP reported a US$699 early-bird price, a quoted 0.05 mm accuracy and Wi-Fi 6 wireless scanning. The original Otter has the higher quoted accuracy of up to 0.02 mm.

From scan to 3D print

  1. Export the mesh from CrealityScan as STL, OBJ or PLY.
  2. Fill holes and make the mesh watertight in the scanning software or a mesh editor.
  3. Convert PLY files with our PLY to STL converter, or OBJ files with the OBJ to STL converter.
  4. Check the result in the STL viewer, then slice and print.

Questions and answers

What is the Creality Otter?

The Creality Otter, sold as the CR-Scan Otter, is a handheld 3D scanner. It captures real objects as 3D meshes with colour, which you can edit and then 3D print.

How accurate is the Creality Otter 3D scanner?

Creality and launch coverage quote accuracy up to 0.02 mm and a point distance of 0.05–2 mm. Real results depend on the object, surface and scanning technique.

What is the difference between the Otter and the Otter Lite?

The Otter Lite, announced in 2025, is a cheaper version with Wi-Fi 6 wireless scanning and a quoted 0.05 mm accuracy, compared with up to 0.02 mm on the Otter.

Can I 3D print a scan from the Creality Otter?

Yes. Export the scan as STL, OBJ or PLY from CrealityScan, repair and close the mesh, then slice it like any other model.
