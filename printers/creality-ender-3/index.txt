2d:Te68,<p><strong>The Creality Ender 3 is an open-frame, budget 3D printer kit with a 220 × 220 × 250 mm build volume, a Bowden extruder and manual bed levelling.</strong> Released in 2018, the original Ender-3 became one of the most widely used hobby printers and started a whole family of Ender 3 models.</p>
<h2>Who the Ender 3 suits</h2>
<p>The Ender 3 suits hobbyists who enjoy tinkering: you assemble it, level it by hand and tune it over time. Creality&#39;s guide lists PLA and TPU as suitable materials. For a printer that needs almost no setup, a newer machine such as the <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-a1/">Bambu Lab A1</a> is easier.</p>
<h2>Ender 3 3D printer models</h2>
<p>Creality kept the 220 × 220 × 250 mm size across several versions:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ender-3</strong> (2018) – the original, with a printed bed sticker and a generic power supply.</li>
<li><strong><a href="/printers/creality-ender-3-pro/">Ender-3 Pro</a></strong> – Mean Well power supply, wider Y-axis extrusion and a magnetic build sheet.</li>
<li><strong>Ender-3 V2</strong> – 32-bit silent mainboard, a 4.3-inch colour screen and a glass bed. See our <a href="/printers/ender-3-v2-manual/">Ender 3 V2 manual guide</a>.</li>
<li>Newer V3-series models add auto levelling and faster motion.</li>
</ul>
<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>The Ender 3 arrives as a kit. Assemble the frame, gantry and screen following the manual, then:</p>
<ol>
<li>Set the power supply switch to 115 V or 230 V to match your mains.</li>
<li>Power on and check that the nozzle and bed heat.</li>
<li>Level the bed (below).</li>
<li>Load filament: heat the nozzle to about 200 °C, cut the filament end at an angle, open the extruder lever and push it in until it flows from the nozzle.</li>
<li>Print a test file from the SD card.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Ender 3 bed levelling</h2>
<p>Creality&#39;s method: home the printer, disable the steppers, then move the nozzle over each corner and turn the knob until a sheet of paper drags slightly between nozzle and bed (about 0.06–0.2 mm). Go round all four corners twice, then print a first-layer test. If prints warp, Creality suggests re-levelling, a 50–60 °C bed and a thin layer of glue.</p>
<h2>Connecting and slicing</h2>
<p>The Ender 3 has no Wi-Fi. Creality recommends printing offline from the SD card, though you can also print from a computer. Slice your models in <strong>Creality Print</strong>, Creality&#39;s free slicer for Windows, macOS and Linux. For PLA, Creality&#39;s guide suggests 0.15 mm layers, 200–210 °C nozzle, 45–55 °C bed and 50–60 mm/s. Our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL converter</a> can recover a rough model from an old print file.</p>
<h2>Common Ender 3 problems and fixes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>No extrusion:</strong> check the extruder gear and filament path, and clear a blocked nozzle throat.</li>
<li><strong>Nozzle leaking at the threads:</strong> heat to about 230 °C and re-tighten the hot end.</li>
<li><strong>Bed or nozzle not heating:</strong> check the thermistor reading first, then the 24 V supply to the heater.</li>
<li><strong>Blank or blue screen:</strong> reseat the screen cable on the mainboard.</li>
<li><strong>Wobbly bed or print head:</strong> tighten the eccentric nuts on the wheels.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Creality publishes the Ender-3 user manual, firmware and Creality Print on its official Download Center under the Ender-3 product. Check your mainboard version before flashing firmware, as it changed during production.</p>
