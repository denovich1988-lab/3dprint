<h2>Ender 3 S1 quick-start summary</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Unbox and assemble</strong> – fit the gantry frame to the base, then the display and material rack, and connect the cables as shown in the guide.</li>
<li><strong>Set the voltage switch</strong> to 115 V or 230 V to match your mains supply.</li>
<li><strong>Level</strong> – choose <strong>Level</strong> on the screen, then set the Z-offset with a sheet of A4 paper until it drags lightly (the guide gives 0.08–0.1 mm).</li>
<li><strong>Auxiliary levelling if needed</strong> – if the bed is tilted by more than about 2 mm, CR Touch levelling fails; turn the four knobs under the bed first.</li>
<li><strong>Load filament</strong> – cut the tip at 45° and feed it through the run-out sensor into the extruder.</li>
<li><strong>First print</strong> – slice a model in Creality Print, save it to the card and start it from the screen. Use only Latin letters and numbers in file names.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> if the screen does not show your file, rename it without spaces or special characters – the guide limits file names to letters and numbers.</p>

<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>The Ender 3 S1 firmware is based on Marlin 2.0, and Creality publishes its source on GitHub. Before updating, check which chip your mainboard uses and download the matching file from the Download Center. In outline, the mainboard update is: format an SD card (Creality specifies a 4096-byte allocation unit), copy the firmware folder to it, insert it with the printer off, switch on and wait for the update to finish. The touchscreen has separate firmware, installed from a card in the screen. Always follow the instructions included with the version you download. Slice in <strong>Creality Print</strong>, and check sliced files with our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a>.</p>
