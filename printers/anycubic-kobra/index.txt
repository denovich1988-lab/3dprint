<h2>Who the Kobra suits</h2>
<p>The original Kobra suits beginners who find a used one or want a simple, offline printer for PLA, PETG and TPU. The direct-drive extruder handles flexible filament better than a Bowden setup. It has a single Z lead screw and no network connection, and newer Kobra models are much faster.</p>

<h2>Setting up the Anycubic Kobra</h2>
<ol>
<li>Join the two main sections with the supplied bolts and screws (about 20 minutes).</li>
<li>Connect the cables between the base and the gantry and power on.</li>
<li>Run <strong>Prepare → Leveling → Auto-Leveling</strong>; the sensor probes 25 points.</li>
<li>Set the <strong>Z-offset</strong> with a sheet of paper under the nozzle.</li>
<li>Load filament with <strong>Prepare → Filament → Filament In</strong> and print a test file from the microSD card.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> LeviQ maps the bed, but the Z-offset is still yours to set. If the first layer is too thin or too squashed, adjust the Z-offset in small steps rather than re-running levelling.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Kobra</h2>
<p>The original Kobra has no Wi-Fi. Copy sliced files to the <strong>microSD card</strong>, or print over <strong>USB</strong> from a computer. Open G-code in our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a> to check it before printing.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>The Kobra runs Marlin-based firmware. It shipped with <strong>Cura</strong> and Kobra profiles on the microSD card. Before flashing any firmware, check the Kobra section of Anycubic&#39;s support pages and note your Z-offset in case a firmware update resets it.</p>
