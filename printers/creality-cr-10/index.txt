<h2>Who the CR-10 suits</h2>
<p>The original CR-10 suits tinkerers who want a big build area for props, helmets and large parts and do not mind manual levelling and upgrades. It has no filament run-out sensor and no power-loss resume, so long prints are best watched. For a modern printer with a similar size, look at the <a href="/printers/creality-ender-5-plus/">Creality Ender 5 Plus</a> or the <a href="/printers/creality-k2-plus/">Creality K2 Plus</a>.</p>

<h2>Setting up the original CR-10</h2>
<ol>
<li>Bolt the vertical gantry to the base.</li>
<li>Connect the motor, heater and sensor cables to the control box.</li>
<li>Power on and <strong>auto-home</strong> the axes.</li>
<li>Level the bed with a sheet of paper at the four corners, using the wheels under the bed. Repeat once.</li>
<li>Load filament and print the sample file from the SD card.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> with a 300 × 300 mm glass bed, re-check the centre after levelling the corners. If the glass is not flat, the middle of the first layer will show it first.</p>
<h2>Connecting the CR-10</h2>
<p>Print from the <strong>SD card</strong>, which originally held a Cura installer, a sample G-code file and the assembly instructions, or connect a computer over <strong>USB</strong>. Common slicers for it include Cura, Repetier-Host and Simplify3D. To check a file before a long print, open it in our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a>.</p>
<h2>CR-10 manual and firmware</h2>
<p>For the original CR-10, the assembly instructions came on the included SD card. For the current CR-10 SE, Creality publishes the user manual and firmware guide on the Creality Wiki under CR Series → CR-10 SE.</p>
