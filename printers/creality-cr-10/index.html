Creality CR-10 – specs, setup and every CR-10 model

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points

  • It levels by hand at four corners, prints from an SD card or USB and came as a semi-assembled kit around 2016–2017.
  • The CR-10 name now covers a family, from the 500 mm cube CR-10 S5 to the compact, auto-levelling CR-10 SE.

The Creality CR-10 is a large open-frame 3D printer with a 300 × 300 × 400 mm build volume, a Bowden extruder and a separate control box, sold as a semi-assembled kit from around 2016–2017. Creality has since used the CR-10 name for a whole family – from the 500 mm cube CR-10 S5 to the compact CR-10 SE.

Who the CR-10 suits

The original CR-10 suits tinkerers who want a big build area for props, helmets and large parts and do not mind manual levelling and upgrades. It has no filament run-out sensor and no power-loss resume, so long prints are best watched. For a modern printer with a similar size, look at the Creality Ender 5 Plus or the Creality K2 Plus.

Setting up the original CR-10

  1. Bolt the vertical gantry to the base.
  2. Connect the motor, heater and sensor cables to the control box.
  3. Power on and auto-home the axes.
  4. Level the bed with a sheet of paper at the four corners, using the wheels under the bed. Repeat once.
  5. Load filament and print the sample file from the SD card.

Tip: with a 300 × 300 mm glass bed, re-check the centre after levelling the corners. If the glass is not flat, the middle of the first layer will show it first.

Connecting the CR-10

Print from the SD card, which originally held a Cura installer, a sample G-code file and the assembly instructions, or connect a computer over USB. Common slicers for it include Cura, Repetier-Host and Simplify3D. To check a file before a long print, open it in our G-code to STL tool.

CR-10 manual and firmware

For the original CR-10, the assembly instructions came on the included SD card. For the current CR-10 SE, Creality publishes the user manual and firmware guide on the Creality Wiki under CR Series → CR-10 SE.

Questions and answers

What is the Creality CR-10 build volume?

The original CR-10 has a 300 × 300 × 400 mm build volume. The CR-10S, CR-10S Pro, V2, V3 and Smart keep that size, while the S4, S5 and Max are larger and the CR-10 SE is smaller.

How do you level a Creality CR-10?

The original CR-10 is levelled by hand. Home the printer, then slide a sheet of paper under the nozzle at each of the four corners and turn the wheel under the bed until the paper drags slightly. Repeat once more.

What is the difference between the CR-10 and CR-10S?

The CR-10S keeps the 300 × 300 × 400 mm size and adds a filament run-out sensor and power-loss resume, which the original CR-10 lacks.

Is the Creality CR-10 SE the same printer as the original CR-10?

No. The CR-10 SE is a much newer, smaller printer with a 220 × 220 × 265 mm build volume, a Sprite direct-drive extruder, CR Touch auto levelling and speeds of up to 600 mm/s.
