The spools mount on the side of the printer, so there is no separate filament box on the desk.</p>
<h2>Who the Flashforge AD5X suits</h2>
<p>The AD5X suits beginners and hobbyists who want four-colour printing at a low cost in a small footprint. It is built for PLA, PETG and TPU. With no enclosure or air filter as standard, it is not aimed at ABS or ASA.</p>
<h2>The IFS multi-colour system</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Four colours</strong> fed from side-mounted spools into one direct-drive extruder.</li>
<li><strong>Automatic loading:</strong> push PLA into the feed rollers and the IFS loads it.</li>
<li><strong>Auto refill:</strong> during a single-colour print it switches to the next spool when one runs out.</li>
<li><strong>TPU limit:</strong> soft TPU 95A works for single-colour prints only.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Flashforge AD5X speed and hardware</h2>
<p>Flashforge lists 600 mm/s travel speed (300 mm/s maximum print speed) and 20,000 mm/s² acceleration. The quick-release nozzle comes off without tools and is available in 0.25, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mm. The bed is a flexible PEI steel plate, and power-loss recovery is built in.</p>
<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>Flashforge&#39;s quick-start guide runs like this:</p>
<ol>
<li>Remove the bed nuts used for shipping.</li>
<li>Plug in, switch on, choose your language and go through the intro.</li>
<li>Connect to Wi-Fi and, if you like, bind the printer in the Flash Maker app by scanning the QR code.</li>
<li>Let the first calibration run: homing, heating, 25-point bed levelling and a vibration test.</li>
<li>Load PLA into the IFS and set the type and colour for each channel.</li>
<li>Print the built-in test model.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> levelling is fully automatic with a pressure sensor, and there is no manual Z offset to set.</p>
<h2>Connecting the AD5X</h2>
<p>The AD5X has dual-band Wi-Fi and Ethernet. Use the Flash Maker app for remote control, send jobs over the network from Orca-Flashforge, or print from a USB drive at the machine.</p>
<h2>Manual, firmware and slicer</h2>
<p>The manual is on the Flashforge support site in the AD5X documentation, with pages for unboxing, quick start, connecting the IFS, firmware and multi-colour printing. Firmware updates arrive over the air – the printer asks before installing – and the IFS firmware is updated separately under Device info. The slicer is Orca-Flashforge, and OrcaSlicer works too. Preview multi-colour files in our <a href="/3mf-viewer/">3MF viewer</a>.</p>
