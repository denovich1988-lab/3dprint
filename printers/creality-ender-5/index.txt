<h2>Assembling the Ender 5</h2>
<p>The axes come pre-assembled, and reviewers report about 30 minutes to finish the build. Two tips from reviews: the hot-end and bed thermistor plugs look alike, so check which goes where, and cut the filament end at an angle before feeding it into the Bowden tube.</p>
<h2>Levelling the Ender 5</h2>
<p>Heat the bed and nozzle, home the printer, then turn the large knobs under each corner until a sheet of paper drags slightly under the nozzle. Repeat for all four corners and check the centre. Creality notes a BL Touch can be added for automatic levelling.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> re-check levelling after moving the printer or swapping the magnetic sheet.</p>
<h2>Connecting and printing</h2>
<p>Slice on a computer, copy the G-code to the <strong>SD card</strong> and print from the LCD, or connect by <strong>USB</strong>. To inspect a sliced file before printing, open it in our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a>.</p>

<p>Creality publishes manuals and firmware for the Ender series in the Download Center on its website and on the Creality Wiki under Ender Series. The Ender 5 S1 user manual is on the Creality Wiki under Ender Series → Ender-5 S1.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>The Ender 5 runs a modified <strong>Marlin</strong> firmware. Early printers came with Cura on the SD card; today Creality&#39;s slicer is <strong>Creality Print</strong>.</p>
