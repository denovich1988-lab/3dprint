<h2>3D printing on the Artisan</h2>
<p>The printing module has two quick-swap hot ends for two colours or a support material such as PVA or breakaway PLA. The bed is zone-heated – up to 110 °C in the 260 × 260 mm centre and 80 °C at the edges – with a double-sided textured PEI sheet. Snapmaker recommends up to 180 mm/s, so the Artisan is slower than modern CoreXY printers.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> nylon and fibre-filled nylon need the optional hardened steel nozzle.</p>
<h2>Laser module</h2>
<p>The 10 W and 40 W laser modules engrave at up to 200 mm/s over a 400 × 400 mm area. Snapmaker says they cut basswood plywood up to 5 mm (10 W) or 15 mm (40 W) thick. The enclosure reduces the laser hazard from Class 4 to Class 1. You can turn SVG artwork into 3D models for printing with our <a href="/svg-to-stl/">SVG to STL tool</a>.</p>
<h2>CNC module</h2>
<p>The 200 W CNC spindle reaches 18,000 RPM and takes bits with 0.5–6.35 mm shanks. It carves wood, MDF, acrylic and PCB, and Snapmaker says it can also cut aluminium, brass and copper. Work area is 400 × 400 mm.</p>
<h2>Who the Snapmaker Artisan suits</h2>
<p>The Artisan suits makers, schools and small workshops that want one enclosed machine for printing, engraving and light carving. If you only need 3D printing, a dedicated printer is faster and costs less; see the <a href="/printers/snapmaker-u1/">Snapmaker U1</a> or the wider <a href="/printers/snapmaker-3d-printers/">Snapmaker 3D printers guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Artisan</h2>
<p>The Artisan connects over <strong>Wi-Fi</strong>, a <strong>USB cable</strong> or a <strong>USB drive</strong>. Jobs are prepared in Snapmaker Luban and sent to the machine or loaded from the drive. The 10 W laser module includes a camera for positioning the material.</p>
<h2>Snapmaker Artisan manual and software</h2>
<p>Snapmaker documents the Artisan on the Snapmaker Wiki, including setup guides and FAQs for each module. Software is <strong>Snapmaker Luban</strong>, a combined slicer and CAM program for Windows, macOS and Linux (tested on Ubuntu); third-party software also works. Preview models before slicing with our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>.</p>
