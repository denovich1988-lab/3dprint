Creality K3 – four-nozzle tool-changer specs, launch and setup

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • The Creality K3 is a tool-changer 3D printer with four docked nozzles (Creality's KliTek system), each with its own filament path, and a 260 × 260 × 260 mm build volume.
  • Creality lists 500 mm/s top speed, 20,000 mm/s² acceleration, a 300 °C hotend and a nozzle swap in about 5 seconds.
  • Creality unveiled the K3 at IFA Berlin in September 2026 and launched it in the US at US$849, with first shipments on 2 November 2026.

The Creality K3 is a four-nozzle tool-changer 3D printer: its KliTek system docks four complete nozzle assemblies, each with its own filament path, and picks up one at a time for multi-colour and multi-material printing. Creality unveiled it at IFA Berlin on 5 September 2026, opened US pre-orders on 12 October and launched it at US$849 (US), with first shipments on 2 November 2026. Creality expects European availability in December 2026.

How the K3 tool-changer works

Single-nozzle multi-colour printers such as those with an AMS or CFS cut and purge filament at every colour change. The K3 instead parks four separate nozzles; only the selected one prints while the others wait in the dock. Creality says 12 of the printer's 37 sensors handle nozzle alignment and locking, and quotes repeatability under 25 µm from its internal testing.

Why it matters: fewer purges mean less waste and shorter multi-colour prints. Creality claims up to 80% less print time and 85% less waste than single-nozzle multi-colour printing in its own tests. It also lets you combine different materials, such as a soluble or breakaway support material with PLA.

Who the Creality K3 suits

The K3 suits makers who print a lot of multi-colour or multi-material parts, flexible TPU, or models with soluble supports, and who like an open, modifiable machine (root access is enabled by default). If you mainly print single-colour PLA, a simpler printer such as the Creality K2 Plus or K1 series costs less.

Setting up the Creality K3

At a high level, based on the quick-start contents in the box:

  1. Remove the four transport screws under the bed.
  2. Twist the numbered spool holders into the side panels.
  3. Mount the PTFE brackets and the CFS Nano filament unit on the rear.
  4. Connect the tool cables and the toolhead cable.
  5. Power on; the self-check runs automatic levelling.
  6. Load filament through the CFS Nano and print a test model.

Connecting the K3

The K3 supports remote control and monitoring through Creality Cloud, and you can send jobs straight from Creality Print. The AI camera records time-lapses and watches for spaghetti failures and foreign objects on the bed. Check sliced files with our G-code to STL tool.

Creality K3 manual and firmware

Creality publishes K3 documentation on the Creality Wiki under the K3 Flagship Series, and firmware with release notes on the K3 support page. Creality says a Klipper-based firmware is being released in phases.

Questions and answers

What is the Creality K3?

The Creality K3 is a tool-changer 3D printer. It docks four complete nozzle assemblies and picks up one at a time, so it can switch colours or materials with far less purge waste than a single-nozzle multi-colour printer.

How much does the Creality K3 cost?

Creality launched it in the US at US$849 (November 2026). The Canadian store listed CA$1,189 for pre-sale.

How fast does the Creality K3 change nozzles?

Creality quotes about 4.8 seconds per nozzle swap and under 15 seconds for a complete material change.

Is the Creality K3 enclosed?

The stock K3 is open at the top; Creality sells a top cover separately. Creality's spec sheet describes both an open frame and an actively cooled chamber, so check the bundle you buy.
