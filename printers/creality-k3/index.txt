<h2>How the K3 tool-changer works</h2>
<p>Single-nozzle multi-colour printers such as those with an AMS or CFS cut and purge filament at every colour change. The K3 instead parks four separate nozzles; only the selected one prints while the others wait in the dock. Creality says 12 of the printer&#39;s 37 sensors handle nozzle alignment and locking, and quotes repeatability under 25 µm from its internal testing.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> fewer purges mean less waste and shorter multi-colour prints. Creality claims up to 80% less print time and 85% less waste than single-nozzle multi-colour printing in its own tests. It also lets you combine different materials, such as a soluble or breakaway support material with PLA.</p>
<h2>Who the Creality K3 suits</h2>
<p>The K3 suits makers who print a lot of multi-colour or multi-material parts, flexible TPU, or models with soluble supports, and who like an open, modifiable machine (root access is enabled by default). If you mainly print single-colour PLA, a simpler printer such as the <a href="/printers/creality-k2-plus/">Creality K2 Plus</a> or K1 series costs less.</p>
<h2>Setting up the Creality K3</h2>
<p>At a high level, based on the quick-start contents in the box:</p>
<ol>
<li>Remove the four transport screws under the bed.</li>
<li>Twist the numbered spool holders into the side panels.</li>
<li>Mount the PTFE brackets and the CFS Nano filament unit on the rear.</li>
<li>Connect the tool cables and the toolhead cable.</li>
<li>Power on; the self-check runs automatic levelling.</li>
<li>Load filament through the CFS Nano and print a test model.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Connecting the K3</h2>
