Ender 3 S1 Pro – specs, setup, firmware and who it suits

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • The Ender 3 S1 Pro is an open-frame bed-slinger with a 220 × 220 × 270 mm build volume and a Sprite direct-drive extruder that reaches 300 °C.
  • It levels with a CR Touch probe, prints on a magnetic PEI spring steel plate and has a 4.3-inch touchscreen and an LED light bar.
  • Creality announced it in April 2022 at a launch price of US$499; it still appears on Creality's own store.

The Creality Ender 3 S1 Pro is an open-frame bed-slinger 3D printer with a 220 × 220 × 270 mm build volume, a 300 °C Sprite direct-drive extruder and CR Touch automatic levelling. Creality announced it at its 8th-anniversary event in April 2022 with a launch price of US$499 (US), and it still appears on Creality's own store. This page covers the official specs, who it suits, setup, connection options and firmware.

Ender 3 S1 Pro at a glance

Ender 3 S1 Pro specs explained

The headline upgrade over the regular S1 is the hotend: the S1 Pro reaches 300 °C, which opens up nylon (PA) and makes ABS and PETG easier. The Sprite extruder is an all-metal dual-gear direct drive weighing 334 g, so flexible TPU feeds reliably. Dual Z motors keep the gantry level.

Creality's store page lists a 110 °C heated bed, while the spec table in the user manual says 100 °C. Plan on about 100 °C to stay safe.

Tip: without an enclosure, ABS and nylon warp easily. Print them in a draught-free room or build a simple cover.

Who the Ender 3 S1 Pro suits

It suits hobbyists who want a classic, open and repairable Ender with a better hotend and a touchscreen. It is slower than newer CoreXY printers and has no Wi-Fi or camera, so buyers who want app control and high speed should look at newer models in our Creality Ender 3D printers guide.

Setting up the Ender 3 S1 Pro

Creality says the printer arrives about 96% pre-assembled. Following the official manual, at a high level:

  1. Unpack the parts and fit the gantry to the base.
  2. Mount the touchscreen and the spool holder, then connect the cables.
  3. Check the voltage setting matches your mains supply.
  4. Run auto levelling with the CR Touch and set the Z offset.
  5. Load filament and print the test model from the SD card.

Our Ender 3 S1 Pro manual guide goes through each step in more detail.

Connecting the Ender 3 S1 Pro

The printer has no built-in Wi-Fi. Print from the SD card, or connect a computer over USB Type-C. For remote printing, Creality sells a separate Wi-Fi Box that works with the Creality Cloud app. Before printing a downloaded file, check it in our STL viewer.

Manual, firmware and slicer

Creality publishes the Ender-3 S1 Pro user manual, the firmware with release notes and the Creality Print slicer in the Creality Download Center on creality.com (Support → Download → Ender-3 S1 Pro). The manual is also on the Creality Wiki under Ender Series. Read the release notes for the update steps before flashing firmware.

Creality Print is the official slicer. The manual also lists Cura and other common slicers. To recover a model from a sliced file, use our G-code to STL tool.

Ender 3 S1 Pro vs Ender 3 S1

Both share the frame, the Sprite extruder and the 220 × 220 × 270 mm build volume. The S1 Pro adds the 300 °C hotend, the touchscreen, the PEI plate and the LED light bar. See the full comparison on our Ender 3 S1 page.

Questions and answers

What is the Ender 3 S1 Pro build volume?

220 × 220 × 270 mm according to Creality, the same as the standard Ender-3 S1.

What is the difference between the Ender 3 S1 and the S1 Pro?

The S1 Pro raises the nozzle limit to 300 °C and adds a 4.3-inch touchscreen, a PEI spring steel plate and an LED light bar. The frame, Sprite extruder and build volume are shared.

Does the Ender 3 S1 Pro have Wi-Fi?

No. It prints from an SD card or over USB Type-C. Creality sells a separate Wi-Fi Box for Creality Cloud printing.

Which slicer should I use with the Ender 3 S1 Pro?

Creality Print is the official slicer and has a ready profile. Creality's manual also lists Cura and other common slicers.
