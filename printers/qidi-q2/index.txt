<h2>Who the QIDI Q2 suits</h2>
<p>The Q2 suits users who want to print nylon, polycarbonate and fibre-filled filaments at home without buying a large industrial machine. The heated chamber and 370 °C nozzle cover those materials, while the QIDI Box adds multicolour PLA and PETG work. QIDI&#39;s FAQ notes that about 300 mm/s is typical for fast PLA and ABS printing; 600 mm/s is the toolhead maximum.</p>
<h2>Heated chamber and filtration</h2>
<p>A second-generation PTC heater warms the chamber to 65 °C on its own, independent of the bed. A three-stage filter (G3 pre-filter, H12 HEPA and coconut-shell activated carbon) cleans the chamber air.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> warm, stable chamber air reduces warping and layer splitting in ABS, ASA, PA and PC.</p>
<h2>QIDI Box multicolour</h2>
<p>The optional <strong>QIDI Box</strong> holds four spools and feeds them to the single nozzle. QIDI says up to four boxes can be chained for 16 materials, and tangle detection needs the box. QIDI filament is recognised by RFID; third-party spools are entered on the screen.</p>
<h2>Setting up the QIDI Q2</h2>
<p>QIDI&#39;s quick start guide lists these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Check the accessories and remove the foam around the toolhead.</li>
<li>Undo the four transport screws holding the bed, let it rise, take out the foam under it and unlock the platform and toolhead.</li>
<li>Plug in the screen cable and slide the screen into place; fit the spool holder arm at the back.</li>
<li>Check the voltage for your region before powering on.</li>
<li>Feed filament through the PTFE tube to the nozzle.</li>
<li>Print the built-in test model. If it does not stick, adjust the Z-offset or the bed temperature.</li>
</ol>
<p>Levelling and input shaping run at the first calibration and before each print, using the load cell in the hot end.</p>
<h2>Connecting the QIDI Q2</h2>
<p>Send files over <strong>2.4 GHz Wi-Fi</strong> or <strong>Ethernet</strong> from QIDI Studio, print from a <strong>USB drive</strong>, or use <strong>QIDI Cloud</strong> for remote access. The printer runs Klipper-based firmware, and the camera and web interface can be opened in a browser at the printer&#39;s IP address.</p>
<h2>QIDI Q2 manual</h2>
<p>QIDI publishes the Q2 guides on the QIDI Wiki under Q2, including unboxing, the quick start guide, firmware updates and release history. A USB drive in the box also holds QIDI Studio and the manuals, though QIDI notes these may be older versions.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Update the firmware with the <strong>online update</strong> on the printer&#39;s touchscreen. Slice in <strong>QIDI Studio</strong>; QIDI also lists <strong>OrcaSlicer</strong> and <strong>PrusaSlicer</strong> and accepts STL, OBJ, 3MF and STEP files. To convert a 3MF project for another slicer, use our <a href="/3mf-to-stl/">3MF to STL converter</a>.</p>
