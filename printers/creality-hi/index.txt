<h2>Creality Hi vs Hi Combo</h2>
<p>The <strong>Hi</strong> is the printer on its own and prints one filament at a time. The <strong>Hi Combo</strong> bundles one four-spool <a href="/printers/creality-cfs/">Creality CFS</a>. The CFS holds 1 kg spools, keeps them dry with desiccant and reads RFID tags on Creality filament, so colours are mapped automatically. When a spool runs out, the printer can switch to a matching spool and keep going.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> if you start with the plain Hi, you can add a CFS later – the multi-colour upgrade is the same unit.</p>
<h2>Who the Creality Hi suits</h2>
<p>The Hi suits beginners and families who want an easy first printer with a path to multi-colour prints. It is open, so it is best for PLA, PETG and PLA-CF; for regular ABS or ASA an enclosed printer such as the <a href="/printers/creality-k2-plus/">Creality K2 Plus</a> is a better fit.</p>
<h2>Setting up the Creality Hi</h2>
<p>Creality says the Hi arrives about 95% pre-assembled and takes around 8 minutes to build:</p>
<ol>
<li>Fit the gantry to the base with the supplied screws.</li>
<li>Mount the CFS hub on the gantry leg and connect the cables (Combo only); Creality&#39;s unboxing video shows this step.</li>
<li>Power on; automatic levelling and calibration run on first start.</li>
<li>Load filament into the printer or the CFS and print a test file.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Connecting the Creality Hi</h2>
<p>The Hi joins <strong>Wi-Fi</strong> and works with <strong>Creality Cloud</strong> for remote monitoring with the built-in camera. Files can also go on a <strong>USB drive</strong>, and the printer has 8 GB of internal storage. Check sliced files with our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a>.</p>
<h2>Creality Hi manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Creality publishes the Hi Combo manual, firmware and an unbox-and-setup video on the Hi Combo support page, with more articles on the Creality Wiki. The printer runs Creality OS.</p>
