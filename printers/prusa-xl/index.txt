2c:Td72,<p><strong>The Prusa XL is a large CoreXY 3D printer with a 360 × 360 × 360 mm build volume and an automatic toolchanger that holds 1, 2 or 5 toolheads.</strong> Prusa Research announced it in November 2021, started shipping in March 2023, and in August 2026 replaced it with the updated XL+.</p>
<h2>Who the Prusa XL suits</h2>
<p>The XL suits engineers, schools and small production shops that print big parts or several materials at once. Because each material has its own toolhead, a multi-material print wastes very little filament compared with single-nozzle systems that purge between colours. The segmented heat bed only heats the area under the part, which saves time and energy on small prints.</p>
<h2>Prusa XL toolheads</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Single-tool</strong> – one Nextruder; you can add more heads later.</li>
<li><strong>Dual-head</strong> – two materials, such as a model and soluble PVA support.</li>
<li><strong>Five-head</strong> – up to five colours or materials in one print.</li>
</ul>
<p>Each toolhead can carry a different nozzle size between 0.25 and 0.8 mm. Prusa notes that with three or more toolheads the printer needs an extra power supply, which ships with those versions.</p>
<h2>Prusa XL vs XL+</h2>
<p>Prusa announced the XL+ on 13 August 2026. Unshipped XL orders were upgraded automatically, and existing owners keep firmware, spare parts and support. The XL+ keeps the 360 mm build volume, 290 °C nozzle and 1–5 toolheads, and adds:</p>
<ul>
<li>an eddy-current tool-offset sensor that Prusa says is about 20× faster than the old pin;</li>
<li>360° part cooling, GT1.5 belts and high-flow nozzles as standard;</li>
<li>a nozzle wiper;</li>
<li>a new enclosure option.</li>
</ul>
<p>Prusa lists the XL+ bed at up to 115 °C. Upgrade kits from XL to XL+ are sold for each toolhead version.</p>
<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>The XL ships assembled or semi-assembled; the XL+ ships assembled, and you fit the toolheads. On first start a wizard runs. For the XL+, Prusa lists this order:</p>
<ol>
<li>Firmware check and nozzle seal height.</li>
<li>Prepare the printer.</li>
<li>Network and Prusa Connect setup.</li>
<li>Calibration tests and dock position calibration for each tool.</li>
<li>Loadcell test and filament sensor calibration.</li>
<li>Phase stepping calibration, then the first prints.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Connecting the XL</h2>
<p>The XL has Ethernet built in and optional Wi-Fi. <strong>PrusaLink</strong> gives you a local web interface at the printer&#39;s IP address, and <strong>Prusa Connect</strong> lets you send and watch prints from anywhere. You can also print from a USB drive. Slice in <strong>PrusaSlicer</strong>, which includes profiles for each toolhead setup. Prusa printers read 3MF projects; our <a href="/stl-to-3mf/">STL to 3MF converter</a> helps you package meshes.</p>
<h2>Manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Prusa publishes the XL and XL+ assembly manuals in the Prusa Knowledge Base under Original Prusa XL, with separate guides for single-, dual- and five-head versions. The XL Handbook and XL+ Handbook PDFs are in the Firmware &amp; Downloads section, alongside the latest firmware.</p>
