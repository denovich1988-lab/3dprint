<p>The Centauri Carbon suits buyers on a budget who want an enclosed, fast printer. The tempered-glass door and top cover keep heat in for ABS and ASA, and the hardened steel nozzle handles carbon-fibre-filled filaments. Reviewers also pointed to classrooms and small print farms, since the printer runs well over the local network.</p>

<li><strong>Calibration:</strong> vibration compensation and pressure advance calibration.</li>

<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>The setup follows Elegoo&#39;s included guide:</p>
<ol>
<li>Remove the bed lock screws and the transport packing.</li>
<li>Connect the touchscreen ribbon cable and seat the screen.</li>
<li>Rotate the spool holder into place and feed filament through the run-out detector.</li>
<li>Switch on and pick your language.</li>
<li>Check that the build plate&#39;s side A faces up.</li>
<li>Let the self-check run; it takes about 30 minutes.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> do not interrupt the first self-check – it measures the bed and tunes vibration compensation for later prints.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Centauri Carbon</h2>
<p>Join Wi-Fi under Settings → Network on the touchscreen. You can then send prints from <strong>ElegooSlicer</strong> over the local network, or open the printer&#39;s web interface by its IP address. The supplied USB drive holds the slicer installers and sample models, and you can print from it at the printer. Firmware updates arrive over the air.</p>
