<h2>Who the Prusa MINI+ suits</h2>
<p>The MINI+ suits beginners, students and anyone with limited desk space who prints small and medium parts. Its small footprint also makes it a common choice for print farms. Prusa lists a long material list, from PLA and PETG to ASA, ABS, PC and flexible filaments, although the Bowden extruder makes soft flexibles harder to feed than a direct-drive printer.</p>
<p>Choose a larger model if you regularly print parts over 18 cm or want multi-material printing – the MINI+ does not support Prusa&#39;s MMU. See the whole range on our <a href="/printers/prusa-3d-printers/">Prusa 3D printers</a> page.</p>
<h2>Prusa MINI vs MINI+</h2>
<p>The MINI+ replaced the original M.I.N.D.A. probe with the <strong>SuperPINDA</strong>, which Prusa says does not drift with temperature and needs no thermistor, so the first layer is more consistent. New metal clips also make the bearings easier to fit. The firmware is the same for both. Firmware 5.1.0 later added Input Shaper and Pressure Advance, which Prusa says make the printer faster.</p>
<h2>Setting up the Prusa MINI+</h2>
<p>The semi-assembled MINI+ arrives as three large parts; the kit takes longer to build. Prusa&#39;s knowledge base has a step-by-step &quot;Building your MINI+&quot; guide.</p>
<ol>
<li>Join the three parts following the building guide and fit the spool holder.</li>
<li>Place the printer on a flat, stable surface with about 30 cm of space around it, and connect the external power supply.</li>
<li>On first boot, the <strong>Calibration Wizard</strong> runs a selftest of the fans, axes and heaters, then mesh bed levelling.</li>
<li>Run <strong>First Layer Calibration</strong>: turn the knob while a zig-zag line prints until the first layer is slightly squished. This sets the Live Z value.</li>
<li>Load filament from the Filament menu (Prusa recommends PLA first) and print a test file from the USB drive.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> run First Layer Calibration again when you switch between steel sheets, because smooth, textured and satin sheets differ in thickness.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Prusa MINI+</h2>
<p>The MINI+ prints from a <strong>USB drive</strong> on the front and has an <strong>Ethernet</strong> port. Wi-Fi needs Prusa&#39;s optional ESP module. With a network connection, <strong>PrusaLink</strong> gives local control in a browser and <strong>Prusa Connect</strong> lets you upload, start and monitor prints remotely. The printer also works fully offline.</p>
<h2>Prusa MINI+ manual</h2>
<p>Prusa publishes the MINI+ handbook in the Prusa Knowledge Base, listed under Printer handbooks as &quot;Original Prusa MINI &amp; MINI+&quot;. The assembly instructions for the semi-assembled version are a separate online manual with a downloadable PDF. The handbook covers the first start, the calibration wizard, the material guide, regular maintenance and troubleshooting.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Firmware updates come as a <code>.bbf</code> file: copy the file for your language to the root of a USB drive, insert it and restart the printer. Prusa notes that a MINI+ on firmware older than 4.4.0 must flash 4.4.x first before moving to 5.1.0 or later. Slice with the free <strong>PrusaSlicer</strong>, which includes MINI+ profiles. Check a model before slicing with our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>.</p>
<h2>Compare the Prusa MINI+</h2>
<p>For a larger Prusa, see the <a href="/printers/prusa-mk4/">Prusa MK4</a> page, or compare the MINI+ with other compact printers in our <a href="/printers/compare/">printer comparison tool</a>.</p>
