27:Td5a,<p><strong>The Bambu H2C (Bambu Lab H2C) is an enclosed multi-material 3D printer whose Vortek system swaps between six induction-heated hot ends during a print.</strong> Bambu Lab announced it on 14 November 2025 at Formnext. It shares the H2 frame, the 350 °C hot end and the 65 °C heated chamber with the <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-h2d/">H2D</a> and <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-h2s/">H2S</a>.</p>
<h2>Who the Bambu Lab H2C suits</h2>
<p>The H2C is aimed at makers, designers and engineers who print many colours or materials in one job – cosplay parts, multi-colour models, assemblies that mix rigid and flexible filament. Because each hot end keeps its own filament loaded, the printer does not have to purge the nozzle at every colour change. Bambu Lab says up to 7 materials per job can run without purge cleaning; with AMS units the total reaches 24 filaments. Some prints still use a small purge tower.</p>
<p>Each Vortek hot end remembers the filament it last printed, and the rack holds nozzles from 0.2 to 0.8 mm.</p>

<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>The H2C is heavy and needs two people to lift. The setup follows the H2-series pattern: remove the transport brackets and cable ties on the bed, the hot-end rack and the toolhead; mount the AMS and connect its tube and cable; power on; run the automatic calibration and the hot-end rack calibration; then load the hot ends into the rack. Connect Wi-Fi and update the firmware before the first print.</p>
<h2>Connecting the H2C</h2>
<p>You slice in <strong>Bambu Studio</strong>, send prints over Wi-Fi and watch them in the <strong>Bambu Handy</strong> app, which can show the four cameras. LAN mode and printing from a USB drive work without the cloud.</p>
<h2>Manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab publishes the H2C guides on the Bambu Lab Wiki in the H2 series section, next to the H2D and H2S pages. Firmware updates install from the touchscreen, Bambu Handy or Bambu Studio.</p>
