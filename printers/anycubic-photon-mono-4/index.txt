<h2>Who the Photon Mono 4 suits</h2>
<p>The Mono 4 suits first-time resin users and tabletop painters who want fine detail on small parts and do not need Wi-Fi or an app. Its vat is not heated, so in a cold room you may need longer exposures or a warmer workspace. For larger parts, look at the bigger models in the <a href="/printers/anycubic-photon/">Anycubic Photon range</a>.</p>

<h2>Setting up the Photon Mono 4</h2>
<p>Following Anycubic&#39;s quick start, at a high level:</p>
<ol>
<li>Unpack, check the parts and fit the LCD screen protector.</li>
<li>Power on and attach the build platform.</li>
<li>Level: lay the levelling paper on the screen, press Home, press the platform down gently, tighten the four screws and set Z zero.</li>
<li>Run the exposure test screen, then fit the vat and fill it with resin below the max mark.</li>
<li>Print the test model from the USB drive, then run Anycubic&#39;s exposure test file to dial in your resin.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> the USB drive must be FAT or FAT32, 32 GB or smaller, with the print file in the root folder.</p>
<h2>Maintenance</h2>
<p>Anycubic&#39;s guide covers the vat cleaning function, checking the release film&#39;s use count, lubricating the Z screw and cleaning with alcohol. Wear gloves and work in a ventilated room when handling resin.</p>
<h2>Photon Mono 4 manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Anycubic publishes the Photon Mono 4 quick start guide, FAQ and firmware files on the Anycubic Wiki under resin 3D printers. With no Wi-Fi, firmware is installed from a USB drive through the system menu.</p>
