<h2>Quick start: from the box to the first print</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Unbox</strong> and check the package contents against the handbook.</li>
<li><strong>Assemble</strong> – the kit follows the online assembly guide, ending with a preflight check. The assembled printer needs only a few minutes of preparation.</li>
<li><strong>Run the selftest wizard.</strong> It checks the fans, the loadcell, the X, Y and Z axes, the nozzle and bed heaters, gearbox alignment and the filament sensor. Stay at the printer – some tests ask you to tap the nozzle.</li>
<li><strong>Print</strong> – load filament and print one of the sample G-codes from the USB drive.</li>
</ul>
<p>There is no manual first-layer probe adjustment: the loadcell in the nozzle measures the bed itself.</p>

<h2>Common problems and fixes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mesh bed levelling fails</strong> – check that the nozzle is seated and hand-tight, then rerun the loadcell calibration.</li>
<li><strong>Heating error</strong> – check heater and thermistor connections and cables; with the printer cold, check the bed connector screws.</li>
<li><strong>Fan error</strong> – clean the print-head fans with compressed air and check their connectors.</li>
<li><strong>Homing failed or a stiff axis</strong> – remove obstructions, clean and lubricate the rods, and check pulley screws and belt tension.</li>
<li><strong>Filament not extruding</strong> – check idler tension, temperature and the filament sensor, clean the feed gear, and clear the nozzle or do a cold pull.</li>
<li><strong>USB drive not recognised</strong> – format it as FAT32.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Prusa publishes MK4 firmware as .bbf files on the MK4 family downloads page. Copy the file to a USB drive and update from the printer, or let Prusa Connect offer the update over the air. Slice in <strong>PrusaSlicer</strong>, and print offline from USB, locally through PrusaLink or remotely through Prusa Connect.</p>
