Anycubic 3D printer lineup – every current model compared

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points
  • Anycubic makes both FDM filament printers (the Kobra line) and resin printers (the Photon line).
  • Most current Kobra printers support multi-colour printing with ACE 2 Pro units; the Kobra X has a four-colour changer built into its print head.
  • FDM models slice in Anycubic Slicer Next, resin models in Photon Workshop, and manuals are on the Anycubic Wiki.

An Anycubic 3D printer is either a Kobra filament (FDM) printer or a Photon resin printer, and the current range runs from the open-frame Kobra 4 and Kobra X to the enclosed Kobra S1 Max and the large-format Photon P1 Max. This page lists the models on Anycubic's global store as of November 2026, with Anycubic's own figures.

Kobra 4 and Kobra X: entry multi-colour

The Kobra 4 has a 260 mm cube build volume, a 300 °C nozzle and up to 600 mm/s, with an ACE 2 Pro feeder for four colours. The Kobra X, first shown at Formnext 2025, keeps the same build volume but moves the filament changer into the print head (Anycubic calls it ACE Gen 2), which Anycubic designed to cut waste. See our Anycubic Kobra X page for full specs.

Kobra S1 and S1 Max: enclosed CoreXY

The Kobra S1 ACE 2 Pro Combo is an enclosed CoreXY printer with a 250 mm cube build volume, a 320 °C nozzle, a 120 °C bed, 600 mm/s and 20,000 mm/s². Its ACE 2 Pro dries filament at up to 65 °C. The Kobra S1 Max scales up to 350 × 350 × 350 mm, with a 350 °C hot end and active chamber heating to 65 °C for engineering filaments. Our Kobra S1 Combo page covers the original S1.

Kobra 3 Max V2: large format

The Kobra 3 Max V2 has the biggest FDM build volume in the range at 420 × 420 × 500 mm, with up to 600 mm/s and 10,000 mm/s² and an ACE 2 Pro for four to eight colours. It suits cosplay helmets, large props and long functional parts.

The Photon P1 launched on Kickstarter on 17 December 2025 at a US$799 retail price. Resin printers give finer detail for miniatures and figures but need gloves, ventilation, washing and curing.

Anycubic software and apps

  • Anycubic Slicer Next – FDM slicer based on the open-source OrcaSlicer.
  • Anycubic app – remote monitoring and cloud printing on iOS and Android.
  • Photon Workshop – slicer for Photon resin printers.

Before slicing a downloaded multi-colour project, check it in our 3MF viewer; to use a 3MF part in another program, convert it with the 3MF to STL converter.

Where to find Anycubic manuals

Anycubic publishes user manuals and quick start guides on the Anycubic Wiki, filed by model, together with firmware update guides and software downloads.

Which Anycubic printer to choose

Pick the Kobra 4 or Kobra X for an affordable first multi-colour printer, the Kobra S1 for ABS and ASA in an enclosure, the S1 Max for large engineering parts, and a Photon resin printer for miniatures. Questions and answers

Which Anycubic 3D printer is best for beginners?

The open-frame Kobra 4 and Kobra X are Anycubic's entry multi-colour printers, both with a 260 mm cube build volume. Choose the enclosed Kobra S1 if you also want ABS and ASA.

Which Anycubic printers are enclosed?

The Kobra S1 and the Kobra S1 Max are enclosed CoreXY printers. The S1 Max also heats its chamber to 65 °C.

What slicer do Anycubic printers use?

Anycubic Slicer Next, based on the open-source OrcaSlicer, for FDM printers, and Photon Workshop for Photon resin printers.

Does Anycubic make resin 3D printers?

Yes. The Photon line includes the Photon Mono M7, M7 Pro and M7 Max, the Photon Mono 4 Ultra, and the dual-vat Photon P1 and the large Photon P1 Max.
