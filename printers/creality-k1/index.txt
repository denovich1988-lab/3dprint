<h2>Who the Creality K1 suits</h2>
<p>The K1 suits people who want a fast, enclosed printer that works out of the box, with a footprint small enough for a desk. The enclosure helps with ABS and ASA. For carbon-fibre filaments the later <a href="/printers/creality-k1c/">Creality K1C</a> is the better fit, and for bigger parts the <a href="/printers/creality-k1-max/">Creality K1 Max</a> has a 300 mm cube build volume.</p>
<h2>Creality K1 features</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>CoreXY motion</strong> with a lightweight print head and a 50 N dual-gear direct extruder.</li>
<li><strong>Hands-free levelling</strong> – strain sensors in the heatbed measure the nozzle touching the plate.</li>
<li><strong>Input shaping</strong> tuned automatically with a G-sensor during the self-test.</li>
<li><strong>Enclosed body</strong> with a glass lid and door, plus filament run-out detection and power-loss recovery.</li>
<li><strong>Optional AI camera and AI LiDAR</strong>, sold separately, for remote viewing and first-layer checks.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Setting up the Creality K1</h2>
<ol>
<li>Remove all foam, including the pieces around the print head.</li>
<li>Connect the screen cable and snap the screen into the base.</li>
<li>Remove the three fixing screws that hold the motion system for shipping.</li>
<li>Power on and follow the on-screen guide: language, network, time zone, Creality Cloud binding and the <strong>self-test</strong>, which levels the bed and tunes input shaping.</li>
<li>Load filament and start the test print.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> let the self-test finish without opening the lid or touching the printer; vibration during the input-shaping step affects the result.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Creality K1</h2>
<p>Join the printer to <strong>Wi-Fi</strong> or plug in a <strong>LAN</strong> cable during the first setup. You can then send jobs from <strong>Creality Print</strong> on your computer (add the printer by its IP address) or from the <strong>Creality Cloud</strong> app, which also handles remote monitoring. Printing from a <strong>USB drive</strong> works without a network. Use our <a href="/3mf-to-stl/">3MF to STL converter</a> if you need a plain mesh from a project file.</p>
<h2>Creality K1 manual</h2>
<p>Creality publishes the K1 user manual and quick start guide on the Creality Wiki under K1 Flagship Series → K1. It covers unpacking, first start and self-test, the screen menus, printing over the network, maintenance and troubleshooting.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Firmware updates arrive over the air, through Creality Cloud or as a file for a USB drive from Creality&#39;s K1 Flagship download page; Creality recommends running the self-test after an update. Creality&#39;s slicer is <strong>Creality Print</strong>; the spec sheet also lists Cura, PrusaSlicer and Simplify3D. Creality OS is based on Klipper, and Creality has published the K1-series Klipper source on GitHub.</p>
