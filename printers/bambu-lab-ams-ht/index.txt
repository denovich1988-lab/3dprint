It is meant for moisture-sensitive engineering filaments such as PA, PC and PP, and several units can be chained for more materials.</p>

<h2>What is inside the AMS HT</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>A display</strong> showing humidity, temperature and remaining drying time.</li>
<li><strong>A drying module</strong> with heater, fan and temperature sensor, plus a humidity sensor.</li>
<li><strong>A lid sensor</strong> that warns you if the lid opens during drying.</li>
<li><strong>An internal hub</strong> with a feeder, an assist motor and a retraction assembly, and an outlet sensor that finds the filament after a break or jam.</li>
<li><strong>An RFID coil</strong> that reads Bambu Lab filament tags for remaining filament and auto refill.</li>
<li><strong>Desiccant and a rubber gasket</strong> to keep the box sealed.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Bambu Lab AMS HT automatic material system: compatible printers</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>H2 series:</strong> the built-in buffer supports two AMS HT units; a tube expansion port raises this to eight. More than one unit needs a PTFE adapter.</li>
<li><strong>X1 and P1 series:</strong> one unit needs an additional buffer and several need the AMS Hub, up to four units.</li>
<li><strong>A2L:</strong> All3DP reports that the A2L accepts the AMS HT, with a separate power cable for drying.</li>
</ul>
<p>The AMS HT uses the 6-pin cable shared with the AMS 2 Pro; the cable from the first-generation AMS does not fit. Together with up to four AMS 2 Pro units, an H2 printer can draw from 24 filaments. See how all the units compare in our <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-ams/">Bambu AMS guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Supported filaments</h2>
<p>For automatic feeding, Bambu Lab lists PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, PET, PA, PC, PP, POM, HIPS, dried PVA and BVOH, Bambu PLA-CF, PAHT-CF and PETG-CF.</p>
<p><strong>Bypass only:</strong> TPE, TPU, Bambu PET-CF, Bambu TPU 95A and other soft or brittle fibre-filled filaments. Route them through the rear TPU outlet with a PTFE tube and push the filament into the extruder by hand. The AMS HT then cannot load or unload automatically, but it still dries the spool. Read our <a href="/blog/tpu-filament/">TPU filament guide</a> for printing tips.</p>
<h2>Drying with the AMS HT</h2>
<p>The heater circulates warm air inside the box; the intake is at the bottom and the exhaust at the back, so keep both clear. Drying needs the supplied power cable, because the printer cannot provide enough power. The desiccant changes colour as it absorbs moisture – replace it or dry it when it turns dark green. For drying temperatures by material, see our <a href="/blog/filament-dryer/">filament dryer guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Setting up the AMS HT</h2>
<ol>
<li>Remove the desiccant from its sealed bag and place it in the unit.</li>
<li>Connect the 6-pin cable and the PTFE tube to the printer – directly on H2, through a buffer or AMS Hub on X1 and P1.</li>
<li>Plug in the AMS HT power cable so drying works.</li>
<li>Insert the filament at the inlet; press the release button to remove it later.</li>
<li>Check the slot in Bambu Studio and map it to your model.</li>
</ol>
