Prusa 3D printers – every current model compared

Updated November 23, 2026

Key points
  • Prusa Research was founded in 2012 by Josef Prusa and builds its printers in Prague.
  • The current FDM lineup runs from the 180 mm MINI+ to the 360 mm XL+ toolchanger.
  • All Prusa printers use the free PrusaSlicer; newer models connect to PrusaLink and Prusa Connect.

Prusa 3D printers are made by Prusa Research, the Czech company Josef Prusa founded in 2012, and the current FDM lineup has five main models: the CORE One+, CORE One L+, XL+, MK4S and MINI+. Prusa builds them in its own factory in Prague and pairs them with free software: PrusaSlicer, Prusa Connect and the Printables model library.

Prusa printer lineup at a glance

On 13 August 2026 Prusa updated three models – the XL became the XL+, the CORE One L the CORE One L+ and the CORE One+ moved to Gen 2 – adding GT1.5 belts and a nozzle wiper.

Prusa CORE One+

The CORE One+ is Prusa's enclosed CoreXY all-rounder with an actively heated chamber. It comes as a kit or assembled and suits everyone from beginners to professionals who print ABS, ASA or other warp-prone materials.

Prusa CORE One L+

The larger CORE One L+ has a 300 × 300 × 330 mm build volume and a chamber heated up to 60 °C. It ships assembled and is aimed at workshops, small businesses and production work.

Prusa XL+

The XL+ is the biggest Prusa FDM printer, with a 360 mm cube build volume and an automatic toolchanger for up to five toolheads, so each material gets its own nozzle. Read our Prusa XL page for specs and setup.

Original Prusa MK4S

The MK4S is the latest version of Prusa's classic open-frame i3 design, with a loadcell for automatic first-layer calibration. It is sold as a kit or assembled and replaced the MK4 in 2024 – see our Prusa MK4 page.

Original Prusa MINI+

The MINI+ is the compact, entry-level model with a 180 mm cube build volume. It suits beginners, small parts and print farms.

Other Prusa printers

Prusa also lists the Pro HT90 for industrial use, the SL1S SPEED resin printer and the Prusa Signature Oak in its printer range.

Prusa software and connectivity

  • PrusaSlicer – the free slicer with profiles for every Prusa printer.
  • PrusaLink – a local web interface built into newer printers, opened in a browser at the printer's IP address.
  • Prusa Connect – Prusa's free cloud service for sending and monitoring prints remotely.
  • Printables – Prusa's free model library.

Prusa printers read STL, 3MF and STEP models in PrusaSlicer. Our STEP to STL converter helps if you need a mesh from a CAD file.

How to choose a Prusa printer

  1. Want an enclosed printer for ABS or ASA? CORE One+, or CORE One L+ for bigger parts.
  2. Want the classic open printer, possibly as a kit? MK4S.
  3. Need several materials in one print with little waste? XL+ with multiple toolheads.
  4. Short on space or budget? MINI+.

Questions and answers

Which Prusa 3D printer should a beginner buy?

The MK4S and the CORE One+ are Prusa's main all-rounders; the MINI+ is the compact, lower-cost option. Pick the CORE One+ if you want an enclosed printer.

Are Prusa printers sold as kits?

Some are. The MK4S and CORE One+ come as a kit or assembled; the CORE One L+ and XL+ ship assembled.

What is the largest Prusa printer?

Among the FDM printers listed here, the XL+ has the largest build volume, 360 × 360 × 360 mm, and up to five toolheads.

Do Prusa printers work with Bambu Studio or Cura?

Prusa's own slicer is PrusaSlicer, which ships profiles for every Prusa printer. Other slicers can produce G-code, but Prusa supports and tests PrusaSlicer.
