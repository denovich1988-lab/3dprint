Flashforge 3D printer lineup – every current model compared

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points
  • Flashforge's current FDM range is the Adventurer 5M, Adventurer 5M Pro, AD5X, Creator 5, Creator 5 Pro and the large Guider 3 Ultra.
  • The AD5X prints four colours through one nozzle, while the Creator 5 and Creator 5 Pro swap between four separate toolheads.
  • Flashforge's main slicer is Flash Studio Desktop, the renamed Orca-Flashforge, and the phone app is Flash Studio Mobile (formerly Flash Maker).

A Flashforge 3D printer today means one of six FDM models: the Adventurer 5M, Adventurer 5M Pro, AD5X, Creator 5, Creator 5 Pro or the large-format Guider 3 Ultra. This page lists the models on Flashforge's official store as of November 2026, with Flashforge's own figures, so you can see which one fits your space and the colours you want to print.

Adventurer 5M and 5M Pro: fast single-colour printers

Both have a 220 mm cube build volume, up to 600 mm/s travel speed, 20,000 mm/s² acceleration, a 280 °C quick-release nozzle and automatic levelling. The Adventurer 5M is open-frame (Flashforge sells a separate DIY enclosure kit). The Adventurer 5M Pro adds a full enclosure with HEPA and activated-carbon filtration and a built-in camera; Flashforge announced it on 1 September 2023 at an introductory price of US$599, with the 5M at US$399.

Tip: if you plan to print ABS or ASA indoors, the enclosed 5M Pro is the better choice. See our Adventurer 5M and Adventurer 5M Pro pages.

AD5X: four colours in a compact printer

The AD5X keeps the 220 mm cube size but builds in Flashforge's IFS (Intelligent Filament System), which feeds four spools into one shared nozzle without an external box. Flashforge lists 300 mm/s print speed, 600 mm/s travel, a 300 °C nozzle and a 110 °C bed. It has no filtration; an optional DIY enclosure kit is sold separately. Full specs are on our Flashforge AD5X page.

Creator 5 and Creator 5 Pro: four-toolhead toolchangers

The Creator 5 series, launched in spring 2026, uses four independent toolheads (Flashforge calls the system FlashSwap). Each colour or material has its own nozzle, so there is no purge tower. Both have a 256 mm cube build volume, 300 mm/s print speed, 600 mm/s travel, 30,000 mm/s² acceleration, a 320 °C nozzle and a 120 °C bed. The Creator 5 Pro adds a full enclosure, active chamber heating to 65 °C and H13 HEPA plus activated-carbon filtration. Launch prices reported by 3DPrinting.com were US$649 for the Creator 5 and US$799 for the Pro.

Why it matters: a toolchanger wastes far less filament on colour changes than a single-nozzle system like the AD5X, but costs more.

Guider 3 Ultra: large and tall

The Guider 3 Ultra is Flashforge's large-format printer, with 330 × 330 × 600 mm using one extruder (300 × 330 × 600 mm using both). It has two extruders with automatic filament switching, a 350 °C nozzle, a 120 °C bed, 500 mm/s travel, an enclosed chamber with HEPA 13 filtration and a 7-inch touchscreen. It suits tall functional parts and small-batch work.

Which Flashforge printer to choose

  1. First printer, single colour: Adventurer 5M.
  2. Indoor use with ABS or ASA: Adventurer 5M Pro.
  3. Multicolour on a budget: AD5X.
  4. Multicolour or multi-material with little waste: Creator 5, or Creator 5 Pro for engineering filaments.
  5. Tall parts: Guider 3 Ultra.

Flashforge software and app

Flashforge renamed Orca-Flashforge to Flash Studio Desktop, an OrcaSlicer-based slicer for Windows and macOS (Linux in beta) with LAN or cloud printing and a camera view. Flashforge's slicer guide lists Flash Studio Desktop or OrcaSlicer for the Creator 5 series and AD5X, and adds FlashPrint 5 for the Adventurer 5M series and Guider 3 Ultra. The phone app is Flash Studio Mobile (formerly Flash Maker) for remote monitoring and printing. To check a model before slicing, open it in our STL viewer.

Manuals and support

Flashforge publishes quick start guides and manuals on the Flashforge Wiki and in the Download Center on its website, together with firmware and slicer downloads. Our Adventurer 5M manual guide summarises the setup of the most popular model.

Questions and answers

Which Flashforge 3D printer is best for beginners?

The Adventurer 5M is Flashforge's entry CoreXY printer with a 220 mm cube build volume and automatic levelling. Choose the AD5X if you want four-colour printing at a similar size, or the 5M Pro if you want an enclosure.

Which Flashforge printers are enclosed?

The Adventurer 5M Pro, the Creator 5 Pro and the Guider 3 Ultra. The Creator 5 Pro also heats its chamber to 65 °C.

Which Flashforge printers can print in multiple colours?

The AD5X has a built-in four-colour filament system with one shared nozzle. The Creator 5 and Creator 5 Pro have four independent toolheads, so no purge tower is needed.

What slicer do Flashforge printers use?

Flash Studio Desktop (formerly Orca-Flashforge) for current models. The Adventurer 5M series and Guider 3 Ultra also work with FlashPrint 5, and Flashforge lists OrcaSlicer as compatible.
