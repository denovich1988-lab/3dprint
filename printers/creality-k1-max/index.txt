<h2>Who the K1 Max suits</h2>
<p>The K1 Max suits makers who need larger parts than a 220 mm printer allows – helmets, storage boxes, long brackets – and want a fast enclosed machine for ABS, ASA and carbon-fibre filaments. The hardened-steel nozzle and the filament list (including PA-CF and PC) point to functional parts. It is a single-nozzle printer unless you add the CFS upgrade.</p>

<h2>AI camera and LiDAR</h2>
<p>The <strong>AI camera</strong> watches for print failures and foreign objects on the bed and records time-lapses. The <strong>LiDAR</strong> (1 μm resolution, according to Creality) scans the first layer after it is printed to check it. Strain sensors in the bed handle automatic levelling, and a G-sensor in the print head measures vibration for input shaping.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> reviewers noted the AI failure monitoring uses Creality Cloud, so bind the printer to an account if you want those alerts.</p>
<h2>Setting up the K1 Max</h2>
<p>The official user manual describes this first-run flow:</p>
<ol>
<li>Unbox the printer (it arrives almost fully assembled), remove the foam, connect the touchscreen cable and fit the spool holder at the back.</li>
<li>Power on and choose a language.</li>
<li>Remove the three transport screws that hold the bed, as shown on screen.</li>
<li>Make sure nothing blocks the LiDAR&#39;s view, then continue.</li>
<li>Follow the power-on guide: network (Ethernet or 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi), time zone, Creality Cloud binding, then the self-test that runs auto levelling and input shaping.</li>
<li>Load filament with the Extrude function and start your first print.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Connecting the K1 Max</h2>
<p>Print from a <strong>USB drive</strong>, send files over the <strong>LAN</strong> from Creality Print when the computer and printer are on the same network, or print remotely through <strong>Creality Cloud</strong>. Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only; use Ethernet if your router only offers 5 GHz near the printer.</p>
<h2>K1 Max manual</h2>
<p>Creality lists the K1 Max user manual in the Download Center on its website under the K1 Max page, and on the Creality Wiki under K1 Flagship Series → K1 Max → Quick Start Guide. The manual recommends greasing the marked parts every 300 hours. Many steps match the K1; our <a href="/printers/creality-k1-manual/">Creality K1 manual guide</a> covers them.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Creality&#39;s K1-series firmware updates <strong>over the air</strong> when the printer is online, from a <strong>USB drive</strong> with the file from the download page, or through the <strong>Creality Cloud</strong> app. Creality has published the Klipper-based firmware source on GitHub. Slice in <strong>Creality Print</strong>; Creality also lists Cura, PrusaSlicer and Simplify3D as compatible. To preview a sliced file, use our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a>.</p>
<h2>CFS multicolour upgrade</h2>
<p>Creality sells a <strong>K1 Series CFS Upgrade Accessory Kit</strong> for the K1, K1 Max, K1C and K1 SE. It adds a new extruder with a filament cutter, a run-out sensor and RFID filament recognition so the printer can work with the Creality Filament System. Units with the older separate hotend also need the integrated-nozzle hotend, sold separately. After fitting, the firmware is updated from the included USB drive.</p>
