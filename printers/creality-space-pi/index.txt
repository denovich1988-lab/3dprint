<h2>SpacePi and SpacePi Plus</h2>
<p>The SpacePi and the SpacePi Plus share the same 45–70 °C range, 1–48 hour timer and basic material presets. The Plus simply takes two spools instead of one. Both take 1.75 mm and 2.85 mm filament. They cover the common drying temperatures for PLA, PETG and TPU.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> if you mostly print one material at a time, the single SpacePi is enough; the Plus is useful for two-colour work or keeping a second spool ready.</p>
<h2>SpacePi X4</h2>
<p>The SpacePi X4 is the flagship. Each of its two chambers has its own 200 W PTC heater and reaches 85 °C, so it can dry higher-temperature materials such as nylon. Creality lists:</p>
<ul>
<li>built-in desiccant that does not need replacing, a sealing ring and a one-way valve;</li>
<li>a 3.2-inch touchscreen with an auto drying mode and three custom &quot;My Drying&quot; slots;</li>
<li>silent mode, a beep when drying ends and automatic LED lights;</li>
<li>support for 1 kg and 2 kg spools up to 200 mm in diameter and 150 mm wide;</li>
<li>firmware updates by TF card.</li>
</ul>
<p>Third-party coverage reported the X4 shipping in July 2025 at a US$199 launch price (US).</p>
<h2>Setting up a Space Pi dryer</h2>
<p>Creality&#39;s guide for the Space Pi Plus describes the same basic steps for all models:</p>
<ol>
<li>Place the dryer on a flat, stable surface with room for airflow and for the lid to open.</li>
<li>Put the spool or spools on the rollers.</li>
<li>Feed the filament out through an outlet, adding a PTFE tube if you print straight from the dryer.</li>
<li>Close the lid and power on.</li>
<li>Choose a material preset, or set the temperature and time manually, and start.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Drying tips</h2>
<p>Start below the filament&#39;s glass-transition temperature – PLA softens at much lower temperatures than nylon, and an over-hot spool can fuse its windings. Dry for several hours rather than turning the heat up. For typical temperatures by material, see our <a href="/blog/filament-dryer/">filament dryer guide</a>, and for material-specific advice read <a href="/blog/petg-vs-pla/">PETG vs PLA</a> and our <a href="/blog/tpu-filament/">TPU filament guide</a>.</p>
