Creality SparkX i7 – specs, CFS Lite multicolour and setup

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points
  • The Creality SparkX i7 is an entry-level bed-slinger with a 260 × 260 × 255 mm build volume, rated at 500 mm/s and 10,000 mm/s².
  • With the CFS Lite or CFS Nano filament system it prints in up to four colours; a built-in 720p AI camera watches for failed prints.
  • Creality announced it at the end of December 2025 and opened pre-orders on 6 January 2026, with a US early-bird launch price of $339 for the combo with CFS Lite.

The Creality SparkX i7 is an entry-level bed-slinger 3D printer with a 260 × 260 × 255 mm build volume, automatic calibration, a built-in AI camera and optional four-colour printing with the CFS Lite. Creality announced it at the end of December 2025 and opened pre-orders on 6 January 2026, with a US early-bird launch price of $339 for the combo with CFS Lite. Creality pitches it as a printer for the home rather than the workshop.

Who the SparkX i7 suits

It suits beginners, families and anyone who wants colour prints without tuning a printer. Creality lists PLA, PETG, PLA-CF and TPU, so it is not meant for ABS or nylon. The open frame and moving bed need some space; if you want an enclosed multicolour printer, look at the Creality K2 Combo.

SparkX i7 features

  • Automatic calibration of bed levelling, Z-offset and input shaping, plus pressure advance.
  • Multicolour printing with the CFS Lite or CFS Nano, with tangle and run-out detection.
  • 720p AI camera that detects spaghetti failures and printing in the air, with a privacy cover.
  • Tool-free quick-swap hotend for changing nozzle sizes.
  • RGB status light and night mode, plus Creality's CubeMe tool that turns a portrait photo into a printable figure.

Setting up the SparkX i7

  1. Remove the two shipping brackets on the Z axis.
  2. Clip on the spool holder or place the CFS Lite next to the printer and connect its single cable.
  3. Fit the build plate and power on.
  4. Follow the on-screen setup to join Wi-Fi and run the first calibration (levelling and Z-offset).
  5. Load filament and print a test model.

Tip: the printer has build plate detection, so if a print refuses to start, first check that the plate is in place and seated flat.

Connecting the SparkX i7

Join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi during setup, then print from Creality Print on your computer or from the Creality Cloud app, which also shows the camera feed. You can also print from a USB drive. Multicolour models usually come as 3MF projects; preview them in our 3MF viewer before slicing.

SparkX i7 manual

Creality publishes the SparkX i7 manual and component guides on the Creality Wiki in the SparkX → i7 section. It covers the parts, unpacking, first start and calibration, the CFS connection, maintenance and troubleshooting.

Firmware and software

Firmware downloads and release notes are on Creality's SparkX i7 support page. Use Creality Print 6.3 or newer for slicing as Creality recommends, and Creality Cloud for remote control.

Questions and answers

What is the Creality SparkX i7 build volume?

260 × 260 × 255 mm, a little larger than the common 220–256 mm class of entry-level printers.

Can the Creality SparkX i7 print in multiple colours?

Yes, up to four colours with the CFS Lite or CFS Nano filament system. The CFS Mini is a two-spool autoloader without multicolour printing.

Which slicer does the SparkX i7 use?

Creality Print 6.3 or newer, plus the Creality Cloud app for remote printing and monitoring.

Is the SparkX i7 good for beginners?

Creality aims it at beginners and home users. Levelling, Z-offset and input shaping are calibrated automatically and there is very little to assemble.
