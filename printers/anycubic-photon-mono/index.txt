<h2>Why the mono screen mattered</h2>
<p>A monochrome LCD lets more UV light through than the older colour (RGB) screens, so layers cure faster. Anycubic quotes exposure from about 1.5 seconds per layer and up to 50 mm/h, and an LCD life of up to 2,000 hours. The original <a href="/printers/anycubic-photon/">Anycubic Photon</a> used a 2K colour screen with longer exposures.</p>
<h2>Who the Photon Mono suits</h2>
<p>It suits beginners printing miniatures and small detailed parts who find a used unit at a good price. The build plate is small, and replacement screens and parts for an end-of-life model may be harder to find than for current models.</p>
<h2>Setting up the Photon Mono</h2>
<p>Based on Anycubic&#39;s package contents and published reviews, at a high level:</p>
<ol>
<li>Fit the build platform and resin vat, which ship separately.</li>
<li>Connect the power supply.</li>
<li>Level the platform (see below).</li>
<li>Secure the vat with its thumbscrews and add resin.</li>
<li>Fit the UV cover.</li>
<li>Slice in Photon Workshop, copy the file to the USB drive and start the print on the touchscreen.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Levelling the build platform</h2>
<p>Loosen the four bolts on the platform, lay a sheet of paper over the LCD and home the platform to Z = 0 from the touchscreen. Press the platform flat onto the paper, tighten the bolts and save Z = 0. Remove the paper before adding resin.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> re-level after changing the vat film or if first layers stop sticking.</p>
<h2>Slicing with Photon Workshop</h2>
<p>Anycubic Photon Workshop runs on Windows and macOS. Orient, hollow and support your model, then slice to the printer&#39;s file format and copy it to the USB drive. Check the mesh first in our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>, and convert other formats with the <a href="/obj-to-stl/">OBJ to STL converter</a>.</p>
<h2>Manual</h2>
<p>Anycubic ships a printed user manual in the box along with tools, a USB drive, a scraper, gloves and a funnel. Guides for current Photon models are on the Anycubic Wiki.</p>
<h2>What replaced the Photon Mono</h2>
<p>Anycubic recommends the Photon Mono 2. The newer <a href="/printers/anycubic-photon-mono-4/">Photon Mono 4</a> is another current option. See the full line-up in our <a href="/printers/anycubic-3d-printers/">Anycubic 3D printers guide</a>, or use the <a href="/printers/compare/">printer comparison tool</a>.</p>
