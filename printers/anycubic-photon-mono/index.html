Anycubic Photon Mono – specs, levelling and Photon Workshop

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • The Anycubic Photon Mono is an MSLA resin printer with a 6-inch 2K monochrome LCD (2560 × 1620), 0.051 mm XY resolution and a 130 × 80 × 165 mm build volume.
  • Anycubic opened pre-orders in September 2020 at a US$209 launch pre-order price for the first units.
  • Anycubic now lists the Photon Mono as end-of-life and points buyers to the Photon Mono 2.

The Anycubic Photon Mono is a compact MSLA resin 3D printer with a 6-inch 2K monochrome LCD (2560 × 1620) and a 130 × 80 × 165 mm build volume – Anycubic's first entry-level "Mono" model. Anycubic opened pre-orders in September 2020 at a US$209 launch pre-order price for the first 2,000 units (US). The store now lists it as end-of-life and recommends the Photon Mono 2. This page covers specs, setup, levelling and slicing for owners and second-hand buyers.

Why the mono screen mattered

A monochrome LCD lets more UV light through than the older colour (RGB) screens, so layers cure faster. Anycubic quotes exposure from about 1.5 seconds per layer and up to 50 mm/h, and an LCD life of up to 2,000 hours. The original Anycubic Photon used a 2K colour screen with longer exposures.

Who the Photon Mono suits

It suits beginners printing miniatures and small detailed parts who find a used unit at a good price. The build plate is small, and replacement screens and parts for an end-of-life model may be harder to find than for current models.

Setting up the Photon Mono

Based on Anycubic's package contents and published reviews, at a high level:

  1. Fit the build platform and resin vat, which ship separately.
  2. Connect the power supply.
  3. Level the platform (see below).
  4. Secure the vat with its thumbscrews and add resin.
  5. Fit the UV cover.
  6. Slice in Photon Workshop, copy the file to the USB drive and start the print on the touchscreen.

Levelling the build platform

Loosen the four bolts on the platform, lay a sheet of paper over the LCD and home the platform to Z = 0 from the touchscreen. Press the platform flat onto the paper, tighten the bolts and save Z = 0. Remove the paper before adding resin.

Tip: re-level after changing the vat film or if first layers stop sticking.

Slicing with Photon Workshop

Anycubic Photon Workshop runs on Windows and macOS. Orient, hollow and support your model, then slice to the printer's file format and copy it to the USB drive. Check the mesh first in our STL viewer, and convert other formats with the OBJ to STL converter.

Manual

Anycubic ships a printed user manual in the box along with tools, a USB drive, a scraper, gloves and a funnel. Guides for current Photon models are on the Anycubic Wiki.

What replaced the Photon Mono

Anycubic recommends the Photon Mono 2. The newer Photon Mono 4 is another current option. See the full line-up in our Anycubic 3D printers guide, or use the printer comparison tool.

Questions and answers

What is the Anycubic Photon Mono build volume?

130 × 80 × 165 mm, according to Anycubic's product page.

How do you level the Anycubic Photon Mono?

Loosen the four bolts on the build platform, lay a sheet of paper on the LCD, home the platform to Z = 0, press it flat, tighten the bolts and set the zero position.

Is the Anycubic Photon Mono discontinued?

Yes. Anycubic's store lists it as end-of-life and delisted, and recommends the Photon Mono 2 instead.

What slicer does the Photon Mono use?

Anycubic Photon Workshop, for Windows and macOS. It saves sliced files in the printer's own format for printing from USB.
