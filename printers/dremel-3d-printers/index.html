Dremel 3D printers – DigiLab 3D45, 3D40 Flex and 3D20 compared

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • Dremel's DigiLab 3D printers – the 3D45, 3D40 Flex and 3D20 – are small, fully enclosed FFF printers aimed mainly at schools.
  • The 3D45 is the most capable, with a 280 °C all-metal extruder, a 100 °C heated glass bed, a built-in camera, Wi-Fi and Ethernet.
  • Dremel no longer supplies DigiLab printers itself; the 3D45 and 3D40 Flex are sold under licence by 3PI Tech Solutions.

Dremel 3D printers are the DigiLab range – the 3D45, 3D40 Flex and 3D20 – small, fully enclosed FFF printers designed mainly for classrooms and makerspaces. Dremel, the US tool brand owned by Bosch, built them for easy, safe use with its own filament. Dremel's DigiLab page now says the products will no longer be supplied by Dremel, and the 3D45 and 3D40 Flex are sold under licence by 3PI Tech Solutions. This guide compares the three models.

Dremel DigiLab 3D45

The 3D45 is the flagship. Its all-metal direct-drive extruder reaches 280 °C and its heated glass platform reaches 100 °C, so it prints PLA, ECO-ABS, nylon and PETG. It adds a built-in HD camera for remote monitoring, a 4.5-inch colour touchscreen, Wi-Fi, Ethernet and USB, RFID filament recognition and carbon and particulate filters. Dremel announced it in July 2017 for schools and universities. It measures 515 × 406 × 394 mm and weighs 19.4 kg.

Tip: the 3D45 recognises Dremel spools by RFID and is designed around Dremel filament, so check which spools your printer accepts before buying third-party filament.

Dremel DigiLab 3D40 Flex

The 3D40 Flex launched in early 2019 for classrooms, with lesson plans for grades 3–12. It prints PLA on a flexible metal build plate that makes removing parts easier, has a 254 × 152 × 170 mm build volume, semi-automatic two-point levelling, a colour touchscreen and Wi-Fi, Ethernet and USB. It has no heated bed and weighs 16 kg.

Dremel 3D20

The 3D20 is the simplest and oldest model: a fully enclosed PLA printer with a 230 × 150 × 140 mm build volume, a 230 °C nozzle, a 3.5-inch touchscreen and SD card printing. 3PI Tech Solutions does not list it, so expect to find it only second-hand.

Who Dremel 3D printers suit

They suit schools, libraries and beginners who value an enclosed machine with simple software and support. The build volumes are small and the filament range is narrow, so hobbyists who want more speed and freedom often choose an open-filament printer such as the Bambu Lab A1 mini or the Prusa MINI.

Software: DigiLab 3D Slicer

Dremel's DigiLab 3D Slicer is based on Cura, and the 3D40 and 3D45 can also print through the web-based Dremel Print Cloud. Students can turn a drawing into a printable model with our image to STL converter and check it in the STL viewer.

Where to find Dremel manuals

Dremel keeps the DigiLab manuals and FAQ in the support section of its DigiLab pages on dremel.com. For current sales and service, 3PI Tech Solutions handles the 3D45 and 3D40 Flex.

Questions and answers

Does Dremel still make 3D printers?

Dremel's own DigiLab page says the products will no longer be supplied by Dremel. The 3D45 and 3D40 Flex are now sold under licence by 3PI Tech Solutions.

Which Dremel 3D printer has a heated bed?

Only the 3D45, with a heated glass build platform that reaches 100 °C. The 3D40 Flex and 3D20 print PLA without a heated bed.

What filament do Dremel 3D printers use?

1.75 mm filament. The 3D45 manual lists Dremel PLA, ECO-ABS, nylon and PETG; the 3D40 Flex and 3D20 print PLA.

What slicer do Dremel 3D printers use?

The Dremel DigiLab 3D Slicer, which is based on Cura. The 3D40 and 3D45 can also print through Dremel Print Cloud.
