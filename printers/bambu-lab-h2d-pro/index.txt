<h2>Who the H2D Pro suits</h2>
<p>It is built for companies, R&amp;D labs, schools and small-batch production. The network features – a physical switch to cut the network, a removable network module, Ethernet and 802.1X Wi-Fi – are aimed at IT departments that do not allow consumer devices on the network. The hard nozzles and heated chamber suit carbon- and glass-filled nylon, PPA and PPS. Bambu Lab also offers help with custom fleet-management tools through resellers.</p>
<h2>Setting up the H2D Pro</h2>
<p>Setup follows the H2D: unpack and remove the shipping fixings, connect the AMS units, power on and let the printer run its first calibration. Then connect it to the network – Ethernet or Wi-Fi – and bind it in Bambu Studio or the Bambu Handy app. Bambu Lab&#39;s H2D Pro FAQ notes that most H2D answers apply to the Pro too.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> the printer ships without a USB drive. Without one, time-lapse recording, local video storage and LAN-only printing are not available, so add a drive before you put it into service.</p>
<h2>Connecting the H2D Pro</h2>
<p>Send jobs from <strong>Bambu Studio</strong> over the network, from the Bambu Handy app or over LAN. In environments with strict IT rules, use the Ethernet port and the enterprise Wi-Fi options, or turn the network off entirely with the physical switch. To check an exported part before you slice it, open it in our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a> or convert CAD files with the <a href="/step-to-stl/">STEP to STL converter</a>.</p>
<h2>H2D Pro manual</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab publishes the H2D Pro documentation on the Bambu Lab Wiki under the H2 series manual, including an H2D Pro FAQ page. The H2D quick start, maintenance and troubleshooting guides on the wiki also apply to the Pro in most cases.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>Firmware updates are installed from the printer screen or through Bambu Studio and Bambu Handy. Slicing is done in <strong>Bambu Studio</strong>, which has profiles for both nozzles and the AMS units.</p>
