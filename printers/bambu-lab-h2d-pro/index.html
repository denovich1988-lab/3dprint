Bambu Lab H2D Pro – specs, differences from the H2D and setup

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points
  • The Bambu Lab H2D Pro is an enterprise version of the H2D dual-nozzle printer, launched in August 2025.
  • It keeps the H2D's 350 × 320 × 325 mm total build volume, 350 °C nozzles and 65 °C heated chamber, and adds tungsten carbide nozzles, Ethernet and a standard vision encoder.
  • Bambu Lab launched it at $3,799 (US launch price) bundled with an AMS 2 Pro and an AMS HT.

The Bambu Lab H2D Pro is the enterprise version of the Bambu Lab H2D dual-nozzle 3D printer, with tungsten carbide nozzles, Ethernet, enterprise network security and a vision encoder as standard. Bambu Lab launched it in August 2025, five months after the H2D, at $3,799 (US launch price) bundled with an AMS 2 Pro and an AMS HT. The build volume, 350 °C nozzles and 65 °C heated chamber are the same as on the H2D.

Bambu Lab says the tungsten carbide nozzle lasts about 50% longer with abrasive filaments. The Pro's nozzle and fan were also announced as upgrades for the regular H2D after a firmware update. For the full background on the platform, see our Bambu Lab H2D page.

Who the H2D Pro suits

It is built for companies, R&D labs, schools and small-batch production. The network features – a physical switch to cut the network, a removable network module, Ethernet and 802.1X Wi-Fi – are aimed at IT departments that do not allow consumer devices on the network. The hard nozzles and heated chamber suit carbon- and glass-filled nylon, PPA and PPS. Bambu Lab also offers help with custom fleet-management tools through resellers.

Setting up the H2D Pro

Setup follows the H2D: unpack and remove the shipping fixings, connect the AMS units, power on and let the printer run its first calibration. Then connect it to the network – Ethernet or Wi-Fi – and bind it in Bambu Studio or the Bambu Handy app. Bambu Lab's H2D Pro FAQ notes that most H2D answers apply to the Pro too.

Tip: the printer ships without a USB drive. Without one, time-lapse recording, local video storage and LAN-only printing are not available, so add a drive before you put it into service.

Connecting the H2D Pro

Send jobs from Bambu Studio over the network, from the Bambu Handy app or over LAN. In environments with strict IT rules, use the Ethernet port and the enterprise Wi-Fi options, or turn the network off entirely with the physical switch. To check an exported part before you slice it, open it in our STL viewer or convert CAD files with the STEP to STL converter.

H2D Pro manual

Bambu Lab publishes the H2D Pro documentation on the Bambu Lab Wiki under the H2 series manual, including an H2D Pro FAQ page. The H2D quick start, maintenance and troubleshooting guides on the wiki also apply to the Pro in most cases.

Firmware and software

Firmware updates are installed from the printer screen or through Bambu Studio and Bambu Handy. Slicing is done in Bambu Studio, which has profiles for both nozzles and the AMS units.

Questions and answers

What is the difference between the Bambu Lab H2D Pro and the H2D?

The H2D Pro adds tungsten carbide nozzles, a stronger toolhead cooling fan, a vision encoder as standard, Ethernet and enterprise Wi-Fi security. Build volume and temperatures are the same, and the Pro ships without laser or cutting modules.

Does the Bambu Lab H2D Pro work with the AMS Lite?

No. Bambu Lab lists the AMS 2 Pro and AMS HT as supported – up to four AMS 2 Pro and eight AMS HT units – but not the AMS Lite.

Can I add a laser to the H2D Pro?

It ships without laser or blade modules, but Bambu Lab says both can be added later.

Who is the H2D Pro for?

Bambu Lab aims it at companies, R&D teams, small-batch production and education, where network security and abrasive engineering filaments matter.
