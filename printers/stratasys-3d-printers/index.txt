Its printers serve aerospace, automotive, consumer products, dental and medical work. This guide explains each family, its build sizes and who it suits.</p>

<h2>FDM: the original Stratasys technology</h2>
<p>Co-founder Scott Crump developed fused deposition modelling, and <strong>FDM</strong> is still a registered Stratasys trademark – the generic name is FFF or material extrusion. The office-friendly <strong>F170</strong> (254 × 254 × 254 mm) and <strong>F370</strong> (355 × 254 × 355 mm) print PLA, ABS, ASA, TPU and carbon-filled ABS. The F370CR is a composite-ready version.</p>
<p>For bigger work, the <strong>F770</strong> offers 1,000 × 610 × 610 mm, the <strong>Fortus 450mc</strong> 406 × 355 × 406 mm and the <strong>F900</strong> 914.4 × 609.6 × 914.4 mm with high-performance materials such as ULTEM 9085, ULTEM 1010, PEKK-based Antero, Nylon 12CF and PC. The newer, faster <strong>F3300</strong> has a 600 × 600 × 800 mm build size.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> these materials are certified and repeatable, which is what aerospace and automotive buyers pay for. Hobby FFF printers such as the <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-x1e/">Bambu Lab X1E</a> work on the same principle at a much smaller scale.</p>
<h2>PolyJet: full colour and multi-material</h2>
<p>PolyJet printers jet tiny droplets of photopolymer and cure them with UV light, a technology Stratasys gained with Objet. The <strong>J55 Prime</strong> has a rotating round tray and runs five resins at once for realistic colour and texture. The <strong>J850 Prime</strong> has a 490 × 390 × 200 mm tray and loads up to seven materials. The <strong>J5</strong> family includes the DentaJet for dental labs.</p>
<h2>P3 DLP: Origin Two</h2>
<p>The <strong>Origin Two</strong> uses Programmable PhotoPolymerization, a DLP resin process from Stratasys&#39; 2020 purchase of Origin. It has a 192 × 108 × 370 mm build volume and is aimed at accurate end-use parts. Open materials are available through an OpenAM licence.</p>
<h2>SAF: H350 powder printing</h2>
<p><strong>Selective Absorption Fusion</strong> spreads powder, jets an absorbing fluid and fuses it with infrared heat. The <strong>H350</strong> has a 315 × 208 × 293 mm build size and prints PA11, PA12 and PP – useful for producing batches of functional parts without supports.</p>
<h2>Neo stereolithography</h2>
<p>The <strong>Neo450s</strong> (450 × 450 × 400 mm) and <strong>Neo800/Neo800+</strong> (800 × 800 × 600 mm) are laser stereolithography machines for large, smooth parts. The Neo800+ uses a 4 W laser instead of 2 W. They run Stratasys&#39; Titanium control software.</p>
<h2>Software: GrabCAD Print</h2>
<p><strong>GrabCAD Print</strong> prepares jobs for FDM, PolyJet, P3 and SAF printers, with a paid Pro tier. Engineers often start from CAD files – open a STEP file in our <a href="/step-viewer/">STEP viewer</a> or check a mesh in the <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>.</p>
<h2>Who Stratasys printers suit</h2>
<p>Stratasys builds for companies, universities, hospitals and dental labs that need certified materials, service contracts and repeatable results. Home users and hobbyists get far more for their money from consumer brands – see our guides to <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-3d-printers/">Bambu Lab</a> and <a href="/printers/prusa-3d-printers/">Prusa</a> printers.</p>
<h2>Recent company news</h2>
<p>In May 2026 Stratasys signed an agreement to buy Markforged from Nano Dimension, with closing expected in the second half of 2026. Until the deal closes, Markforged remains a separate business.</p>
