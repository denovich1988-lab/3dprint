Anycubic Photon – the original resin printer and today's Photon range

Updated November 25, 2026

Key points
  • The original Anycubic Photon (2018) was an MSLA resin printer with a 2K (2560 × 1440) LCD, 47 µm XY resolution and a 115 × 65 × 155 mm build volume.
  • Anycubic now sells the Photon Mono 4, Mono 4 Ultra, Mono M7, M7 Pro, M7 Max, Photon P1 and Photon P1 Max under the Photon name.
  • All Photon models slice in Anycubic's Photon Workshop, and quick start guides and firmware guides are on the Anycubic Wiki.

The Anycubic Photon is Anycubic's resin 3D printer line: the original 2018 Anycubic Photon had a 2K LCD and a 115 × 65 × 155 mm build volume, and today's Photon range runs from the 7-inch Photon Mono 4 to the large Photon Mono M7 Max and Photon P1 Max. This page covers the original model's specs, the current Photon printers with Anycubic's own figures, and the software they use.

Why it matters: the original Photon was one of the first low-cost MSLA printers, and most "Anycubic Photon" settings, profiles and guides you find online date from it. Newer models print several times faster with much finer pixels, so check which model a guide was written for.

The Photon P1 Max is the large-format model in the same collection; we will add its figures once we have checked them on Anycubic's spec page.

Which Anycubic Photon to choose

  • First resin printer, small minis: the Photon Mono 4 is the entry model with a 7-inch 10K screen and USB-only printing.
  • Faster printing and Wi-Fi: the Mono 4 Ultra keeps the same volume but adds a brighter COB light source and Wi-Fi.
  • Bigger parts: the M7 and M7 Pro have a 10.1-inch 14K screen; the M7 Pro adds a heated vat (20–40 °C) and LAN.
  • Large prints: the M7 Max has a 14.7 L build volume.

Tip: resin printers have no "compare by nozzle" logic – compare build volume, XY pixel size and whether the vat is heated, because cold resin is a common cause of failed prints in winter.

Setting up an Anycubic Photon

Anycubic's quick start guides follow the same pattern for the Photon line: unpack, attach the build platform, level it with the levelling paper on the screen, run an exposure test, fill the vat below the max mark and print a test file from the USB drive. Models with auto levelling, such as the Photon P1, skip the paper step.

Connecting and software

Older and entry models print from a USB drive. The M7 series and P1 add Wi-Fi (and LAN on the M7 Pro) and work with the Anycubic App for one-click printing and monitoring. Every Photon model slices in Photon Workshop. Preview your model before slicing with our STL viewer, or convert other formats with OBJ to STL.

Anycubic Photon manual and firmware

Anycubic publishes the quick start guide, FAQ and firmware update guide for each Photon model on the Anycubic Wiki under resin 3D printers. Firmware is usually updated from a USB drive; Wi-Fi models can also update over the air.

Questions and answers

What is the Anycubic Photon?

The Anycubic Photon is Anycubic's resin (MSLA) 3D printer line. The first Photon came out in 2018 with a 2K LCD; the name now covers the Photon Mono 4, Mono M7 series and Photon P1 models.

Is the original Anycubic Photon still sold?

Anycubic's current Photon collection lists the Mono 4, Mono 4 Ultra, Mono M7, M7 Pro, M7 Max, Photon P1 and P1 Max. The original 2K Photon is an older model you are more likely to find used.

What slicer does the Anycubic Photon use?

Photon Workshop, Anycubic's own resin slicer. The Photon P1 product page lists Photon Workshop 4.0 with remote operation.

Which Anycubic Photon has the largest build volume?

Of the models checked, the Photon Mono M7 Max has 298 × 164 × 300 mm (14.7 L). The newer Photon P1 Max is also a large-format model.
