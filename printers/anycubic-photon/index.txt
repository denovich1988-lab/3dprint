<h2>Which Anycubic Photon to choose</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>First resin printer, small minis:</strong> the <a href="/printers/anycubic-photon-mono-4/">Photon Mono 4</a> is the entry model with a 7-inch 10K screen and USB-only printing.</li>
<li><strong>Faster printing and Wi-Fi:</strong> the Mono 4 Ultra keeps the same volume but adds a brighter COB light source and Wi-Fi.</li>
<li><strong>Bigger parts:</strong> the M7 and M7 Pro have a 10.1-inch 14K screen; the M7 Pro adds a heated vat (20–40 °C) and LAN.</li>
<li><strong>Large prints:</strong> the M7 Max has a 14.7 L build volume.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> resin printers have no &quot;compare by nozzle&quot; logic – compare build volume, XY pixel size and whether the vat is heated, because cold resin is a common cause of failed prints in winter.</p>
<h2>Setting up an Anycubic Photon</h2>
<p>Anycubic&#39;s quick start guides follow the same pattern for the Photon line: unpack, attach the build platform, level it with the levelling paper on the screen, run an exposure test, fill the vat below the max mark and print a test file from the USB drive. Models with auto levelling, such as the Photon P1, skip the paper step.</p>
<h2>Connecting and software</h2>
<p>Older and entry models print from a <strong>USB drive</strong>. The M7 series and P1 add <strong>Wi-Fi</strong> (and LAN on the M7 Pro) and work with the <strong>Anycubic App</strong> for one-click printing and monitoring. Every Photon model slices in <strong>Photon Workshop</strong>. Preview your model before slicing with our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>, or convert other formats with <a href="/obj-to-stl/">OBJ to STL</a>.</p>
<h2>Anycubic Photon manual and firmware</h2>
<p>Anycubic publishes the quick start guide, FAQ and firmware update guide for each Photon model on the Anycubic Wiki under resin 3D printers. Firmware is usually updated from a USB drive; Wi-Fi models can also update over the air.</p>
