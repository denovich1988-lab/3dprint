<h2>Who the Voron 2.4 suits</h2>
<p>The Voron 2.4 suits experienced makers who want a fast, enclosed printer for ABS and ASA and enjoy building and tuning their own machine. It is not a plug-and-play printer: depending on skill, assembly takes many evenings, and you will need to wire electronics, flash Klipper and calibrate the machine yourself. If you want an enclosed CoreXY printer that works out of the box, compare it with ready-made models such as the <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-p2s/">Bambu Lab P2S</a> or <a href="/printers/creality-k2-plus/">Creality K2 Plus</a>.</p>
<h2>Voron 2.4 design: CoreXY with a flying gantry</h2>
<p>Most CoreXY printers move the bed up and down. The Voron 2.4 does the opposite: the bed stays fixed and the whole gantry rides up and down on four Z belts, each with its own motor and rail. X and Y use two stacked belts with no crossover. In total the printer uses seven NEMA 17 motors – four for Z, two for X/Y and one for the extruder.</p>
<p>Because each corner of the gantry has its own motor, Klipper can level the gantry to the bed automatically with <strong>Quad Gantry Level</strong> (QGL). The documentation suggests homing, then running <code>QUAD_GANTRY_LEVEL</code> several times so the gantry settles.</p>
<h2>Voron 2.4 sizes and printed parts</h2>
<p>The three sizes share the same design; only the extrusion, rail and belt lengths change. The frame is aluminium extrusion and the printed parts are made in ABS. The Voron team suggests planning about 2 kg of filament in your main colour for the printed parts. If you cannot print them yourself, the community Print It Forward (PIF) service connects you with vetted members who print the parts for you.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> pick the size by what you print most. A larger chamber takes longer to heat-soak, and the documentation recommends heat-soaking for 1.5–2 hours before final gantry squaring.</p>
<h2>Toolhead, probes and electronics</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>StealthBurner</strong> is the current standard toolhead for the Voron 2.4. It uses the Clockwork 2 extruder, has built-in lighting and works with common hotends.</li>
<li><strong>Probes:</strong> most builds use an inductive probe, Klicky (a magnetic microswitch probe docked by macro) or <strong>Voron Tap</strong>, which probes with the nozzle itself.</li>
<li><strong>Electronics</strong> depend on the build or kit. A typical setup is a 24 V power supply, a multi-driver control board and a Raspberry Pi running Klipper; some kits add a USB toolhead board to reduce wiring.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to source a Voron 2.4</h2>
<ol>
<li>Use the Configurator on the Voron Design website to make a bill of materials for your size and options.</li>
<li>Use the sourcing guide to see where parts can be bought, but take quantities from your BOM.</li>
<li>Print the ABS parts yourself or request them through Print It Forward.</li>
<li>Or buy a complete kit from a third-party vendor. Voron Design does not endorse any kit vendor, and kit specs can differ slightly from the reference design.</li>
<li>When the printer is finished, you can apply for a Voron serial number from the community.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Voron 2.4 manual and build guides</h2>
<p>The official assembly manual for the 2.4R2 is published as a PDF in the Voron-2 repository on GitHub. The Voron Documentation site holds the sourcing guide and build guides such as gantry squaring and belt tensioning. We do not reproduce the manual; use the official files and check the revision matches your kit.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>The Voron 2.4 runs <strong>Klipper</strong>. Klipper runs on a Raspberry Pi and is controlled through a web interface such as Mainsail or Fluidd, so many builders skip the printer display entirely. Slice with any slicer that can export Klipper-compatible G-code, such as PrusaSlicer, SuperSlicer or OrcaSlicer. You can check a sliced file in our <a href="/gcode-to-stl/">G-code to STL tool</a> or preview a model in the <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a>.</p>
