<h2>Entry level: Ferret and Lizard</h2>
<p>The <strong>Ferret</strong>, <strong>Ferret SE</strong> and <strong>Ferret Pro</strong> share 0.1 mm accuracy, a 150–700 mm working distance and a 50 mm minimum object size. The Pro scans at up to 60 fps and works wirelessly over Wi-Fi 6, including with an iPhone; the Ferret and SE run at 30 fps over a cable. The <strong>CR-Scan Lizard</strong> reaches up to 0.05 mm without markers and adds colour by mapping photos onto the mesh.</p>
<p><strong>Who they suit:</strong> hobbyists scanning medium objects such as shoes, helmets or furniture parts.</p>
<h2>Mid range: Otter and Otter Lite</h2>
<p>The <strong>CR-Scan Otter</strong> uses near-infrared structured light with four stereo lenses, captures full colour and works with objects from 10 mm to 2 m. The <strong>Otter Lite</strong> (launched in May 2025 at US$759 regular price) weighs 300 g, captures 24-bit colour, runs at up to 30 fps and connects by USB-C or by Wi-Fi 6 through its bridge. It works with Windows, macOS, Android and iOS.</p>
<p><strong>Who they suit:</strong> makers who scan a mix of small and large objects and want colour textures.</p>
<h2>Blue laser: the Raptor family</h2>
<p>Blue laser lines capture fine edges on small, shiny or dark parts better than infrared light. The <strong>CR-Scan Raptor</strong> uses 7 parallel blue lines plus NIR, with 0.02 mm accuracy and up to 60 fps. The <strong>RaptorX</strong> adds 34 cross lines, 24-bit colour and Wi-Fi 6 at 405 g. The <strong>Raptor Pro</strong> (February 2025, US$1,799 regular price) uses 22 cross lines and connects by cable or through a Scan Bridge. The <strong>Raptor Pro Max</strong>, available from 19 November 2026 at a US suggested price of US$1,799, has 42 cross lines and a working distance of up to 550 mm.</p>
<p><strong>Who they suit:</strong> reverse engineering, replacement parts and miniatures where detail matters.</p>
<h2>Newest models: Pika and Sermoon P1S</h2>
<p>The <strong>Pika</strong> went on sale on 22 July 2026 at US$699. It weighs 260 g, combines a 7-line blue laser with NIR, and lists 0.03 mm accuracy in laser mode and up to 110 fps with a PC. The <strong>Sermoon P1S</strong>, launched on 22 September 2026 at US$2,299, has a built-in computer and a 6-inch touchscreen so it can scan without a PC, with 34 cross lines, 7 parallel lines and a single line for deep holes.</p>
<h2>Creality Scan software</h2>
<p>Creality Scan runs on a computer and as a phone app. It removes noise, simplifies meshes and maps colour textures, then exports <strong>STL, OBJ or PLY</strong>. For the RaptorX and Raptor Pro, Creality lists Windows 10/11 64-bit with an Nvidia graphics card, or macOS 11.7.7 or newer on Apple M1–M3.</p>
<h2>From scan to 3D print</h2>
<ol>
<li>Scan the object and let Creality Scan build the mesh.</li>
<li>Clean up noise, fill holes and simplify the mesh in Creality Scan.</li>
<li>Export as STL – or export PLY or OBJ and convert with our <a href="/ply-to-stl/">PLY to STL</a> or <a href="/obj-to-stl/">OBJ to STL</a> converter.</li>

</ol>
