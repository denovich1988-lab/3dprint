2a:Tf3d,<p><strong>The Prusa CORE One is an enclosed CoreXY 3D printer from Prusa Research with a 250 × 220 × 270 mm build volume, a 290 °C Nextruder and an actively controlled chamber.</strong> Josef Prusa announced it on 19 November 2024 at Formnext. It is open source and repairable: the steel frame comes apart with a screwdriver and nothing is glued. This page covers the official specs, how the CORE One, CORE One+ and Gen 2 differ, setup and connection.</p>
<h2>Who the Prusa CORE One suits</h2>
<p>The CORE One suits buyers who want an enclosed CoreXY printer they can repair and upgrade themselves. It handles PLA and PETG with the door closed, and Prusa lists ABS, ASA, HIPS and PA as advanced materials that need the optional Advanced Filtration System. MK4S owners can convert their printer with an official conversion kit.</p>

<h2>First setup</h2>
<p>Prusa&#39;s &quot;Unpacking and first run&quot; guide for the current version goes like this:</p>
<ol>
<li>Lift the printer out, stick on the anti-vibration feet and remove the cardboard fixtures on the X axis.</li>
<li>Mount the screen and peel the protective films off the panels.</li>
<li>Clean the heatbed, lay the print sheet on it and fit the spool holder.</li>
<li>Power on, choose your language and confirm the printer model.</li>
<li>Set up Wi-Fi through the Prusa app, or skip it.</li>
<li>Run the self-test wizard with the door closed – it checks the load cell, the gearbox and the filament sensors.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> the load cell measures the first layer automatically and levels only the area you print on, so there is no manual Z offset to set.</p>
<h2>Connecting the CORE One</h2>
<p>The printer has Ethernet and Wi-Fi; Wi-Fi is set up by holding a phone to the NFC receiver. Remote control runs through Prusa Connect and the Prusa app, and you can also print from a USB drive or run the printer fully offline.</p>
