2b:T56e,<p>The <strong>Bambu Lab A1</strong> is Bambu Lab’s full-size open printer. It moves the bed back and forth (a “bed-slinger”) rather than using a CoreXY frame, but adds the automation Bambu printers are known for: automatic bed levelling and Z-offset, vibration compensation, flow-rate compensation and motor-noise cancelling. Bambu Lab launched it in December 2023 at US$399, or US$559 with the AMS lite.</p>
<h2>Who the A1 suits</h2>
<p>It suits beginners and hobbyists who print mostly PLA and PETG and want a large build area without an enclosure. With the AMS lite it prints up to four colours. It is quieter than many printers thanks to active motor-noise cancellation.</p>
<h2>Setup</h2>
<p>The A1 ships partly assembled. Follow the quick-start guide in the box and on the Bambu Lab Wiki, run the on-screen calibration, then bind the printer in the <strong>Bambu Handy</strong> app. Send prints from <strong>Bambu Studio</strong>; Bambu Studio saves projects as 3MF.</p>
<h2>Manual</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab publishes the A1 user manual, maintenance guides and troubleshooting on its official wiki. We do not copy the manual here; use the official version so instructions match your firmware.</p>
<h2>A1 or A1 mini?</h2>
<p>Read our <a href="/blog/bambu-lab-a1-vs-a1-mini/">A1 vs A1 mini comparison</a>. In short, choose the A1 if any part you plan to print is larger than 180 mm.</p>
