Anycubic Kobra X – specs, multi-colour head and setup

Updated November 24, 2026

Key points
  • The Anycubic Kobra X is an open-frame printer with a 260 × 260 × 260 mm build volume and a four-colour filament changer built into the print head.
  • It was first shown at Formnext 2025; the US early-bird launch price was US$279 against a US$399 list price.
  • With ACE 2 Pro units it expands to up to 19 colours.

The Anycubic Kobra X is an open-frame multi-colour 3D printer with a 260 × 260 × 260 mm build volume and a four-colour filament changer built into the print head. Anycubic first showed it at Formnext 2025 and opened deposits on 26 December 2025; the US early-bird launch price was US$279 against a US$399 list price. It is aimed at beginners who want colour printing without a separate feeder box.

How the Kobra X colour system works

Most multi-colour printers park a feeder box beside or on top of the printer and push long lengths of filament through a tube at every change. On the Kobra X, Anycubic puts the changer – which it calls ACE Gen 2 – in the print head itself, so four filaments sit right above the nozzle. Anycubic presents this as a way to reduce waste at each colour change.

Expanding colours: Anycubic sells the Kobra X alone (4 colours), as a Combo (7 colours), and with one or three extra ACE 2 Pro units (11 or 19 colours). The ACE 2 Pro used with the Kobra X has no active drying of its own.

Who the Kobra X suits

The Kobra X suits first-time buyers and families who want multi-colour toys, signs and gifts in PLA and PETG. It also lists TPU, PVA support material, carbon-fibre PLA and PETG, and ASA. Because it is open-frame, ABS is not listed; for that, the enclosed Anycubic Kobra S1 Combo is the better choice.

Setting up the Anycubic Kobra X

At a high level, setup follows these steps:

  1. Remove the shipping ties and fixtures.
  2. Mount the gantry on the base and snap on the covers.
  3. Plug in the labelled connectors.
  4. Fit the four-spool holder on top of the frame.
  5. Power on, join Wi-Fi and let LeviQ 3.0 level the bed and set the Z offset.

Anycubic publishes the full quick start guide and the user manual on the Anycubic Wiki under FDM 3D printer → Anycubic Kobra X.

Connecting the Kobra X

Send files over Wi-Fi from Anycubic Slicer Next or the Anycubic app, or print from a USB drive. The 720p camera supports remote monitoring and AI spaghetti detection.

Firmware and software

The Kobra X runs Anycubic's Kobra OS. Anycubic's firmware update guide for the printer is on the Anycubic Wiki in the Kobra X section. Slice in Anycubic Slicer Next, which is based on OrcaSlicer. Check colour projects in our 3MF viewer before slicing.

Kobra X in the Anycubic range

The Kobra X shares its 260 mm build volume with the Kobra 4, which uses an external ACE 2 Pro feeder instead of a changer in the head. See our Anycubic 3D printer lineup for every model, or compare the Kobra X with rivals such as the Bambu Lab A1 in our printer comparison tool.

Questions and answers

How many colours can the Anycubic Kobra X print?

Four from the changer built into the print head. Adding ACE 2 Pro units raises that to 7, 11 or up to 19 colours.

What was the Anycubic Kobra X launch price?

In the US, early-bird buyers paid US$279 against a US$399 list price. Check Anycubic's store for current prices.

Is the Anycubic Kobra X enclosed?

No. It is an open-frame bed-slinger. For an enclosed Anycubic, look at the Kobra S1.

What slicer does the Kobra X use?

Anycubic Slicer Next, which is based on OrcaSlicer. You can also print from a USB drive or start jobs from the Anycubic app.
