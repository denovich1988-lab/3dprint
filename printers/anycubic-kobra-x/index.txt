<h2>How the Kobra X colour system works</h2>
<p>Most multi-colour printers park a feeder box beside or on top of the printer and push long lengths of filament through a tube at every change. On the Kobra X, Anycubic puts the changer – which it calls ACE Gen 2 – in the print head itself, so four filaments sit right above the nozzle. Anycubic presents this as a way to reduce waste at each colour change.</p>
<p><strong>Expanding colours:</strong> Anycubic sells the Kobra X alone (4 colours), as a Combo (7 colours), and with one or three extra ACE 2 Pro units (11 or 19 colours). The ACE 2 Pro used with the Kobra X has no active drying of its own.</p>
<h2>Who the Kobra X suits</h2>
<p>The Kobra X suits first-time buyers and families who want multi-colour toys, signs and gifts in PLA and PETG. It also lists TPU, PVA support material, carbon-fibre PLA and PETG, and ASA. Because it is open-frame, ABS is not listed; for that, the enclosed <a href="/printers/anycubic-kobra-s1-combo/">Anycubic Kobra S1 Combo</a> is the better choice.</p>
<h2>Setting up the Anycubic Kobra X</h2>
<p>At a high level, setup follows these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Remove the shipping ties and fixtures.</li>
<li>Mount the gantry on the base and snap on the covers.</li>
<li>Plug in the labelled connectors.</li>
<li>Fit the four-spool holder on top of the frame.</li>
<li>Power on, join Wi-Fi and let LeviQ 3.0 level the bed and set the Z offset.</li>
</ol>
<p>Anycubic publishes the full quick start guide and the user manual on the Anycubic Wiki under FDM 3D printer → Anycubic Kobra X.</p>
<h2>Connecting the Kobra X</h2>
<p>Send files over <strong>Wi-Fi</strong> from Anycubic Slicer Next or the <strong>Anycubic app</strong>, or print from a <strong>USB drive</strong>. The 720p camera supports remote monitoring and AI spaghetti detection.</p>
<h2>Firmware and software</h2>
<p>The Kobra X runs Anycubic&#39;s Kobra OS. Anycubic&#39;s firmware update guide for the printer is on the Anycubic Wiki in the Kobra X section. Slice in <strong>Anycubic Slicer Next</strong>, which is based on OrcaSlicer. Check colour projects in our <a href="/3mf-viewer/">3MF viewer</a> before slicing.</p>
