Bambu Lab 3D printers – every model compared

Updated November 23, 2026

Key points
  • Bambu Lab's range runs from the open 180 mm A1 mini to the enclosed H2 series with heated chambers.
  • Every current Bambu printer works with Bambu Studio, the Bambu Handy app and an AMS unit for multi-colour printing.
  • The X1 series, Bambu Lab's first printers, went out of production on 31 March 2026.

Bambu Lab 3D printers range from the compact, open A1 mini to the enclosed H2 series with heated chambers, dual nozzles and laser modules. All of them use the same software – Bambu Studio for slicing and the Bambu Handy app for monitoring – and all current models can print in several colours with an AMS unit. This overview helps you pick a Bambu printer; each model links to its own page with full specs.

A1 series: open printers for beginners

The Bambu Lab A1 mini and A1 are open-frame bed-slingers. They suit PLA, PETG and TPU, and pair with the four-spool AMS lite. Bambu Lab does not recommend them for ABS, ASA, PC or nylon, which need an enclosure.

Tip: choose the A1 over the A1 mini if you print helmets, storage boxes or other parts wider than 180 mm.

P series: enclosed all-rounders

The P1S and its successor the P2S are enclosed CoreXY printers with a 256 mm cube build volume. The enclosure lets them print ABS and ASA. The P2S, announced in October 2025, adds a 5-inch touchscreen, a 1080p camera and a servo extruder.

X series: from X1 Carbon to X2D

The X1 Carbon was Bambu Lab's first printer (2022), with lidar and a hardened steel nozzle. It went out of production on 31 March 2026. The X2D, announced in April 2026, takes its place in the range with two switchable nozzles, a 65 °C heated chamber and HEPA plus carbon filtration.

H2 series: large format, lasers and many materials

The H2 printers share one large frame, a 350 °C hot end and a 65 °C heated chamber:

  • H2S – one nozzle and the tallest build volume (340 mm).
  • H2D – two nozzles; optional 10 W or 40 W laser.
  • H2C – the Vortek system swaps between six hot ends for multi-material prints.

Multi-colour printing with AMS

Bambu Lab's automatic material systems feed up to four spools each. The AMS lite fits the A1 series; the AMS and AMS 2 Pro fit the enclosed printers, and the AMS HT holds one spool for drying and high-temperature filaments. See our Bambu Lab AMS page for compatibility.

Software for every Bambu printer

  • Bambu Studio – Bambu Lab's free slicer for Windows, macOS and Linux. It saves projects as 3MF files; our 3MF viewer and 3MF to STL converter open them in a browser.
  • Bambu Handy – the phone app for binding, starting and watching prints.
  • MakerWorld – Bambu Lab's model library, linked from Bambu Studio and Bambu Handy.
  • LAN mode – lets every model print without the cloud.

How to choose a Bambu Lab printer

  1. Materials: only PLA, PETG and TPU → A1 or A1 mini. ABS, ASA → P1S or P2S. Nylon, PC and fibre-filled at scale → X2D or the H2 series.
  2. Size: 180 mm (A1 mini), 256 mm (A1, P series, X2D) or about 330–350 mm (H2 series).
  3. Supports and colours: two nozzles (X2D, H2D) for soluble or break-away supports; the H2C for many materials with little waste.
  4. Extra tools: a laser module is only available on the H2 series.

Questions and answers

Which Bambu Lab 3D printer is best for beginners?

The open-frame A1 mini and A1 are Bambu Lab's entry printers. Choose the A1 for a 256 mm build volume or the A1 mini if 180 mm is enough and space is tight.

Which Bambu printers are enclosed?

The P1S, P2S, X2D, H2S, H2D and H2C are enclosed. The X2D and the H2 series also heat the chamber up to 65 °C.

Which Bambu printer has two nozzles?

The X2D and H2D have two nozzles. The H2C goes further, swapping between six hot ends with its Vortek system.

Is the Bambu Lab X1 Carbon still sold?

Bambu Lab ended X1-series production on 31 March 2026. Resellers may still have stock, and Bambu Lab keeps providing spare parts until 31 March 2031.
