OBJ to STL converter

Convert OBJ files to STL in your browser. Preview the model in 3D, check its size, then download. Free, no sign-up, and your file never leaves your device.

Files are processed on your device and never uploaded.

How to use it

  1. Drop your OBJ file on the 3D preview or click “Upload your model”.
  2. Check the preview and size. Adjust Scale or Up axis if the model looks wrong.
  3. Click “Convert to STL”, then download the STL file.

About OBJ to STL conversion

OBJ files from Blender, ZBrush or 3D scanners often come with textures. STL keeps only the geometry, which is what a slicer needs.

OBJ is usually Y-up. We rotate the model to Z-up so it lies correctly on the build plate.

What is a OBJ file?

OBJ is a plain-text 3D format supported almost everywhere. It stores vertices, faces and texture coordinates; materials live in a separate .mtl file. More about OBJ.

What is a STL file?

STL is the most common file for 3D printing. It stores a surface as a list of triangles and nothing else: no colours, no materials, no units. Every slicer can open it.

What each format keeps

OBJ

Geometry, UV coordinates, normals, groups. Colours and textures only through the companion .mtl and image files.

Used by: Blender, Maya, 3ds Max, ZBrush, game engines, photogrammetry and 3D-scanning apps.

STL

Triangle geometry only. Binary STL is about five times smaller than ASCII STL.

Used by: Every slicer (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, Cura), Thingiverse and Printables downloads, most CAD tools as an export.

Questions and answers

Is it free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no file limit set by us. Very large files are limited only by your device’s memory.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The conversion runs in your web browser with JavaScript and WebAssembly. Your file is read from your device and the result is created on your device, so nothing is sent to our server.

What is lost when converting OBJ to STL?

STL keeps: Triangle geometry only. Binary STL is about five times smaller than ASCII STL. Anything OBJ stores beyond that is dropped.

Why is my model the wrong size?

Formats store units differently: STL has none, glTF uses metres, 3MF and STEP record units. If the result looks 1000 times too small or large, pick a different option in Scale (for example “Metres → mm”) and convert again.

Why is my model lying on its side?

Some formats treat Y as up (glTF, OBJ, FBX), others Z (STL, 3MF, STEP). “Up axis: Automatic” rotates the model for the target format. Choose “Keep as is” if you prefer the original orientation.
