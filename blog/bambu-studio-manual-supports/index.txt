<h2>Manual supports in Bambu Studio at a glance</h2>

<th>What to do</th>

<td>Type = normal(manual) or tree(manual), then paint with the left mouse button</td>

<h2>Step 1: choose a manual support type</h2>
<p>Open the <strong>Support</strong> tab of the process settings and tick <strong>Enable support</strong>. In <strong>Type</strong>, Bambu Studio offers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>normal(auto)</strong> and <strong>tree(auto)</strong> – the slicer decides where supports go.</li>
<li><strong>normal(manual)</strong> and <strong>tree(manual)</strong> – only areas you mark as support enforcers get supports.</li>
</ul>
<p>The Bambu Lab Wiki also describes a <strong>hybrid(auto)</strong> type in some versions. Pick <strong>tree(manual)</strong> for organic shapes such as figures, and <strong>normal(manual)</strong> for flat overhangs and bridges on mechanical parts.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> in a manual type, nothing gets support until you paint it. If you slice without painting, the model prints with no supports at all.</p>
<h2>Step 2: open the Supports Painting tool</h2>
<p>Select one object on the plate, then click <strong>Supports Painting</strong> in the top toolbar. Bambu Studio&#39;s shortcut list assigns the <strong>L</strong> key to it. The model turns grey, other objects are hidden while you paint, and painted support areas show in green.</p>
<p>Tick <strong>Highlight overhang areas</strong> to see the faces that would need support at the current threshold angle. This shows you where to paint.</p>
<h2>Step 3: paint where supports are needed</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Action</th>
<th>Result</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>Left mouse button</td>
<td>Enforce supports</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Right mouse button</td>
<td>Block supports</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Shift + left mouse button</td>
<td>Erase</td>
</tr>

<td>Pen size</td>
</tr>

<td>Section view, to paint inside the model</td>
</tr>

<p>Use <strong>Erase all painting</strong> to start again. Bambu Studio will not paint below the build plate.</p>
<h2>Brush types: Circle, Sphere, Fill and Gap fill</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Circle</strong> – a round brush that paints only the faces you can see.</li>
<li><strong>Sphere</strong> – like Circle, but paints every face inside the sphere, including hidden ones.</li>
<li><strong>Fill</strong> – paints a connected area from the face under the cursor and stops at sharp edges. Set the stopping point with <strong>Smart fill angle</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Gap fill</strong> – finds small gaps left between painted areas and fills them. Set <strong>Gap area</strong> and press <strong>Perform</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> tick <strong>On overhangs only</strong> before you paint large areas. The brush then marks only the overhanging faces, so you do not add support to walls by accident.</p>
<h2>Step 4: slice and check the preview</h2>
<p>Slice the plate and switch to the preview. Check that supports appear under every painted area and nowhere else. If a painted overhang has no support, open the tool again, zoom in and repaint the edges – small unpainted gaps are common on curved surfaces.</p>
<p>Bambu Studio 1.10.0 and later can also paint supports on vertical surfaces, which helps with sideways features such as hooks.</p>
<h2>Support enforcer and blocker shapes</h2>
<p>For large or regular regions, painting is slow. Right-click the object and choose <strong>Add support enforcer</strong> or <strong>Add support blocker</strong>. A shape appears as a part of the object. Move and scale it over the region, and Bambu Studio will add supports inside an enforcer or remove them inside a blocker.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> shapes are quicker than painting for whole sections, such as one side of a box, and they are easy to move if you rotate the model.</p>
<h2>Settings that change manual supports</h2>

<td>Overhangs below this angle get auto support; default 30°. Also drives the overhang highlight</td>

<td>Supports may stand only on the plate, never on the model</td>

<h2>Manual supports vs auto supports</h2>
<p>Auto supports are faster to set up and safe for most prints. Manual supports are worth the time when the model is decorative, when you print many copies, or when auto supports cover a surface you need smooth. A common middle ground is to keep <strong>tree(auto)</strong> and block supports on a few cosmetic faces with the right mouse button.</p>
<h2>Checklist before you print</h2>
<ol>
<li>Enable support is on.</li>
<li>Type is normal(manual) or tree(manual), or auto with painting on top.</li>
<li>Every overhang in the highlight is painted (or deliberately left out).</li>
<li>The preview shows supports under each painted area.</li>
<li>On build plate only is set if supports must not touch the model.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>Painting supports in Bambu Studio takes a few minutes and gives you full control: supports where the model needs them and nowhere else. Start with tree(manual) and On overhangs only, check the preview, and switch to enforcer or blocker shapes for large regions.</p>
