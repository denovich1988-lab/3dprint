<h2>When to use PLA</h2>
<p>Use PLA for anything you look at more than you load: figures, vases, display models, prototypes and props. It prints fast, needs little tuning, sticks well to most beds and gives crisp detail and good overhangs.</p>
<h2>When to use PETG</h2>
<p>Use PETG for parts that bend, take impacts, get warm or live outdoors: hooks, brackets, cable clips, planters and enclosures. Prusa uses PETG for many of its own printer parts.</p>
<h2>Printing tips</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bed adhesion.</strong> PETG can stick too well to smooth PEI. Prusa’s guide suggests glue stick as a release layer on some sheets.</li>
<li><strong>Stringing.</strong> PETG strings more. Use your slicer’s PETG profile, dry the filament, and adjust retraction only if needed.</li>
<li><strong>Cooling.</strong> PLA likes strong part cooling; PETG prints better with less fan.</li>
<li><strong>Enclosure.</strong> Neither needs one. Prusa notes that printing PLA or PETG inside a hot enclosure can hurt cooling.</li>
</ul>
