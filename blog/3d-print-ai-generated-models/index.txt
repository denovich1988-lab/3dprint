28:T7b2,<p><strong>To 3D print a model from an AI 3D generator, download it as GLB, convert it to STL or 3MF, fix the scale and orientation, then check it in your slicer.</strong> AI tools such as Meshy, Tripo and Rodin focus on games and the web, so their default files need a few adjustments before they print well.</p>
<h2>Step by step</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Download GLB.</strong> It is the most common export and packs geometry and textures into one file.</li>
<li><strong>Open it in a viewer.</strong> Use our <a href="/glb-viewer/">GLB viewer</a> to check the shape and dimensions.</li>
<li><strong>Convert.</strong> Use <a href="/glb-to-stl/">GLB to STL</a> or <a href="/glb-to-3mf/">GLB to 3MF</a>. Leave “Up axis” on Automatic so the model stands upright.</li>
<li><strong>Fix the scale.</strong> If the size readout shows something like 0.2 × 0.3 × 0.4, the model is in metres: choose “Metres → mm”.</li>
<li><strong>Repair and slice.</strong> Import into Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer or Cura and let the slicer repair the mesh if it reports errors. Add supports where needed.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Colours</h2>
<p>STL is single-colour. If you want colour, export 3MF and paint or assign colours in a multi-colour slicer. Texture colours from the AI model are not transferred automatically to filament colours.</p>
