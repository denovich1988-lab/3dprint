29:T1b22,<p><strong>Bambu filament is Bambu Lab&#39;s own range of 1.75 mm 3D printing filament, with RFID-tagged spools that the AMS reads automatically, from everyday PLA Basic to carbon-fibre engineering grades.</strong> You do not have to use it – Bambu printers print other brands too – but the tags save setup and the profiles are built into Bambu Studio. Facts are from Bambu Lab&#39;s store and wiki, checked November 2026; the range changes often, so check the store for current products.</p>

<h2>What the RFID tag does</h2>
<p>Every official Bambu spool has an RFID tag. When you load it into the AMS, the printer reads the material type and colour automatically, and Bambu Lab says the printing parameters are stored on the tag too. You can view these details but not edit them.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> with several spools in an AMS, the tags stop you printing PETG with PLA settings by mistake. Third-party spools have no tag, so you enter the type and colour on the screen or in Bambu Studio. See our <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-ams/">Bambu Lab AMS</a> page for how the unit works.</p>
<h2>Refills and the reusable spool</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab sells many grades both &quot;with spool&quot; and as a <strong>refill</strong> – the filament wound without a spool. A refill must be fitted onto a spool, such as the Bambu reusable spool; the wiki shows how to swap a refill onto it. The refill collection currently includes PLA Basic, PLA Matte, PLA-CF, PETG HF, PETG Translucent, PETG-CF and ABS.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> buy one or two reusable spools first, then switch to refills for the colours you use most.</p>
<h2>PLA: Basic, Matte, Silk+ and specialty PLA</h2>
<p>PLA Basic is the default for most prints. Bambu Lab&#39;s PLA Basic page gives a nozzle range of 190–230 °C and a bed of 35–45 °C with glue, and recommends staying under 300 mm/s. PLA Matte hides layer lines, Silk+ gives a shiny finish, and the specialty PLAs (Wood, Glow, Marble, Sparkle, Metal) are for decorative prints. For PLA in general, the wiki&#39;s material table gives 190–240 °C at the nozzle.</p>
<p>For food contact, read our guide <a href="/blog/is-pla-food-safe/">Is PLA food safe?</a>.</p>
<h2>PETG: Basic, HF and Translucent</h2>
<p>PETG handles more heat and outdoor use than PLA. The wiki gives 240–270 °C at the nozzle and 60–80 °C on the bed. PETG HF is Bambu Lab&#39;s high-flow PETG for faster printing, and PETG Translucent lets light through. Choosing between the two families? Read <a href="/blog/petg-vs-pla/">PETG vs PLA</a>.</p>
<h2>ABS and ASA</h2>
<p>ABS and ASA need 240–280 °C at the nozzle and a 90–100 °C bed, according to the wiki. Both warp in a cold room, so print them in an enclosed printer such as the <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-p1s/">Bambu Lab P1S</a> or X1 Carbon. ASA adds UV resistance for outdoor parts.</p>
<h2>TPU: which one goes through the AMS</h2>
<p>The AMS and AMS lite do not take filament that is too soft, too brittle or too hard and rough. Bambu Lab lists soft TPUs (95A, 85A and softer) as not AMS-compatible. <strong>TPU 95A HF</strong> is marked &quot;AMS &amp; AMS lite not compatible&quot;: feed it from the external spool holder at 220–240 °C, with a 30–35 °C bed and glue. Bambu Lab also sells a <strong>TPU for AMS</strong> grade made to run through the AMS. More in our <a href="/blog/tpu-filament/">TPU filament guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Carbon- and glass-fibre grades need a hardened nozzle</h2>
<p>The wiki marks PLA-CF, PETG-CF, PAHT-CF and the PA-CF/GF grades as needing a <strong>hardened steel nozzle</strong>, and does not recommend stainless steel or brass nozzles for them, because the fibres wear soft nozzles quickly. PETG-CF runs in the AMS but is not recommended for the AMS lite. Engineering grades such as PAHT-CF and PC also need a hot bed (100–120 °C) and an enclosure.</p>

<h2>How to choose a Bambu filament</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Decorative or everyday print:</strong> PLA Basic or PLA Matte.</li>
<li><strong>Outdoor, warm or load-bearing:</strong> PETG HF, or ASA in an enclosed printer.</li>
<li><strong>Flexible:</strong> TPU for AMS in the AMS, or TPU 95A HF from the external holder.</li>
<li><strong>Stiff engineering part:</strong> a CF grade, only with a hardened steel nozzle.</li>
<li><strong>Multi-material with clean overhangs:</strong> add a Support for PLA or PVA interface.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>Bambu filament is the easiest choice on a Bambu printer because the RFID tags and built-in profiles remove setup, and refills cut the cost of spools. It is not required: any good 1.75 mm filament prints well once you set the type and colour by hand. Check AMS compatibility and nozzle needs before buying specialty grades.</p>
