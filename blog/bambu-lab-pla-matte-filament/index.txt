<h2>What is Bambu Lab PLA Matte filament?</h2>
<p>PLA Matte is part of Bambu Lab&#39;s PLA family, alongside PLA Basic, Silk+, Marble, Wood, Metal and other decorative grades. Bambu Lab describes it as having a matte, non-glossy finish and a curated range of muted colours aimed at art, décor and design objects. The wiki&#39;s material table groups it with PLA Basic as &quot;normal PLA&quot;, which means the same general rules apply: no enclosure needed, no hardened nozzle, and the same AMS compatibility.</p>
<p>The matte look changes how a print reads. Glossy PLA reflects light along every layer line, so each step of a curved surface shows. A matte surface scatters light, so the eye sees the shape rather than the layers. Matte filaments in general get this finish from additives in the plastic; Bambu Lab does not publish its formula, but the data sheet does show a higher density than PLA Basic (1.31 vs 1.24 g/cm³), which is consistent with a filled material.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> the higher density means a 1 kg spool holds slightly less length. By our calculation from the published densities, 1 kg of 1.75 mm PLA Matte is roughly 320 m of filament, against roughly 335 m for PLA Basic. That is close enough not to matter for most jobs, but worth knowing if a large print is near the end of a spool.</p>
<p>For the whole Bambu range, see our overview of <a href="/blog/bambu-filament/">Bambu filament</a>.</p>

<p>The data sheet&#39;s test specimens were printed at 220 °C on the nozzle, 35 °C on the bed and 200 mm/s, which is a sensible middle point if you set things by hand on a non-Bambu printer.</p>
<p><strong>Bed temperature by plate.</strong> The 35–45 °C range on the data sheet matches the Cool Plate. The wiki&#39;s material table gives 45–65 °C for normal PLA on the Smooth PEI (High Temperature) Plate and Textured PEI Plate, and marks the Engineering Plate as not recommended for PLA. The store page also lists the Cool Plate SuperTack, Smooth PEI, Textured PEI and 3D Effect plates in its compatibility table.</p>
<p><strong>Speed.</strong> The store page quotes up to 270 mm/s, measured with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.4 mm line width and 0.2 mm layers; the data sheet simply says under 300 mm/s. Treat 270 mm/s as a realistic ceiling for the published finish, and slow down if you see dull or rough patches on outer walls.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> if you print on an enclosed Bambu printer, open the door or lift the top glass for PLA Matte as for any PLA. The wiki warns that a hot chamber can soften PLA early in the hotend and cause heat creep, and the data sheet&#39;s chamber range tops out at 45 °C.</p>

<h2>RFID, AMS and AMS lite compatibility</h2>
<p>Every official Bambu PLA Matte spool carries an RFID tag. When you load it into an AMS, the printer reads the material type, colour and print settings, so you load and print without choosing a profile. Bambu Lab&#39;s store page lists PLA Matte as compatible with the AMS and AMS lite, and the drying figures on the same page mention the AMS 2 Pro and AMS HT, which can dry the spool while it is loaded.</p>
<p>Refills are sold without a spool. Bambu Lab says a refill must be fitted to a spool, such as its reusable spool, and the wiki shows how to swap one on. Follow those steps exactly so the AMS still recognises the spool afterwards.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> PLA Matte works well as the main colour in multi-colour prints because matte surfaces hide the slight colour bleed and transition marks that glossy PLA can show. See our <a href="/printers/bambu-lab-ams-lite/">Bambu Lab AMS lite</a> page for how the unit handles spool changes.</p>
<h2>Bambu Lab PLA Matte colours</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab describes the PLA Matte colours as a curated range of muted, elegant shades rather than bright, saturated ones. The range covers whites and ivories, greys and charcoal, earth tones such as tans and browns, soft pastels such as pinks, and deeper blues, greens and reds. Retailer listings show names such as Ivory White, Charcoal, Desert Tan, Sakura Pink and Marine Blue. The exact list changes over time, and some colours are sold only with a spool or only as a refill, so check the store for what is current.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> dark matte colours show dust and fingerprints less than glossy black, but they also show white stress marks where a support or a clip has been bent. Light matte colours photograph well because they do not throw reflections.</p>

<h2>Tips for a clean matte finish</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Use the built-in profile first.</strong> On a Bambu printer, start with the RFID profile before you tune anything. It already sets 100% fan and a suitable temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Clean the plate.</strong> Fingerprints and grease are the usual cause of poor adhesion, so clean the plate before a long job and use a thin layer of glue on the Cool Plate as the data sheet suggests.</li>
<li><strong>Watch the first layer.</strong> A slightly squashed first layer gives the best grip. If corners lift, raise the bed by 5 °C within the plate&#39;s range rather than changing the nozzle temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the temperature in range.</strong> Stay within 190–230 °C. If walls look weak or rough at high speed, raise the nozzle by 5–10 °C; if you see stringing, lower it by 5–10 °C before changing retraction.</li>
<li><strong>Choose layer height for the look you want.</strong> Matte already hides lines, so 0.2 mm layers often look like finer layers on glossy PLA. Use 0.12–0.16 mm for figurines and curved surfaces.</li>
<li><strong>Orient parts for strength.</strong> Put loads along the layers, not across them, because the Z values on the data sheet are much lower.</li>
<li><strong>Remove supports carefully.</strong> With low elongation across layers, thin parts can snap with supports. Use tree supports or a support interface, and let the part cool before removal.</li>
<li><strong>Open the chamber.</strong> On enclosed printers, open the door or remove the top glass to avoid heat creep.</li>
<li><strong>Dry an old spool.</strong> If a spool has been out for a while, dry it before an important print.</li>
</ol>
<p>Before you start, preview the model in an STL viewer to check thin walls and the best orientation.</p>
<h2>Who Bambu Lab PLA Matte suits</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Display models and décor:</strong> figurines, architecture models, vases and planters where a flat, painted look matters more than strength.</li>
<li><strong>Product mock-ups and prototypes:</strong> a matte surface looks closer to injection-moulded plastic, which helps when you show a design to someone.</li>
<li><strong>Multi-colour prints:</strong> muted colours and hidden layer lines make AMS prints look cleaner.</li>
<li><strong>Beginners:</strong> the settings are the same as PLA Basic, so it is just as easy to print.</li>
</ul>
<p>It is a poor fit for clips, snap-fits, brackets and anything that sits in a hot car or near a heater. For those, use PLA Basic, PETG or a heat-resistant material.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab PLA Matte prints like ordinary PLA – 190–230 °C on the nozzle, 35–45 °C on the bed, 100% fan – and gives a flat, non-glossy surface that hides layer lines well. Bambu Lab&#39;s own data sheet shows the cost: lower strength than PLA Basic, especially across layers. Use it for anything you want to look good and Basic or a tougher filament for anything that has to hold up under load.</p>
