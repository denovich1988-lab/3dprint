<h2>Useful things to 3D print at home</h2>
<p>These prints pay for your filament quickly:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Replacement knobs and handles</strong> for appliances and furniture. Measure the shaft with calipers first.</li>
<li><strong>Wall hooks and key holders</strong> that fit exactly where you need them.</li>
<li><strong>Furniture feet and levellers</strong> for wobbly tables.</li>
<li><strong>Shelf brackets and clips</strong> for glass or wooden shelves.</li>
<li><strong>Door stops and wedges.</strong></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> For anything near a window, in a car or outdoors, print in PETG or ASA. PLA softens in strong sun and heat. Our <a href="/blog/petg-vs-pla/">PETG vs PLA guide</a> explains the difference.</p>
<h2>Organisers and storage</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Drawer dividers</strong> sized to your drawers.</li>
<li><strong>Modular storage bins</strong> (the gridfinity system is a popular standard with thousands of compatible bins).</li>
<li><strong>Tool holders</strong> for pegboards and walls.</li>
<li><strong>Battery and SD-card organisers.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Filament spool holders and swatches</strong> for your own printer.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Desk and tech accessories</h2>
<ul>
<li>Phone and tablet stands</li>
<li>Cable clips and under-desk cable trays</li>
<li>Headphone hooks</li>
<li>Laptop risers</li>
<li>Controller and remote holders</li>
<li>Monitor-mounted shelves</li>
</ul>
<h2>Fun things to 3D print</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Articulated animals</strong> – dragons, snakes and fish that print in one piece with moving joints.</li>
<li><strong>Fidget toys</strong> – spinners, clickers and infinity cubes; read <a href="/blog/3d-printed-fidget-toys/">3D printed fidget toys</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Puzzles</strong> – interlocking cubes, mazes and puzzle boxes.</li>
<li><strong>Print-in-place mechanisms</strong> – gears, hinges and chains that come off the bed already working.</li>
<li><strong>Miniatures and terrain</strong> for tabletop games (resin gives the most detail; FDM works for terrain).</li>
</ul>
<h2>3D printed gifts</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lithophanes</strong> – a photo that appears when lit from behind. Make one from any picture with our free <a href="/lithophane-maker/">lithophane maker</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Name signs and logos</strong> – turn an SVG into a 3D model with <a href="/svg-to-stl/">SVG to STL</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Vases</strong> in spiral (vase) mode – one wall, fast to print, watertight with the right settings.</li>
<li><strong>Personalised keychains and bookmarks.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Planters</strong> with drainage trays.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Kitchen ideas (and a warning)</h2>
<p>Bag clips, spice racks, utensil holders and cookie cutters are popular. Items that touch food need care: layer lines trap bacteria. Read <a href="/blog/is-pla-food-safe/">is PLA food safe?</a> before printing anything for food contact.</p>
<h2>Things to 3D print for your printer</h2>
<ul>
<li>Spool holders and filament guides</li>
<li>Tool trays that clip to the frame</li>
<li>Nozzle and scraper holders</li>
<li>Enclosure parts and cable chains</li>
<li>Calibration models (temperature towers, retraction tests)</li>
</ul>
<h2>Turn your own ideas into prints</h2>
<p>You do not need CAD skills for simple custom prints:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>From a picture:</strong> convert a logo or drawing with <a href="/image-to-stl/">image to STL</a>.</li>
<li><strong>From an AI 3D model:</strong> read <a href="/blog/3d-print-ai-generated-models/">how to 3D print AI-generated models</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Check before slicing:</strong> open any file in the free <a href="/3d-viewer/">3D viewer</a> to see its size and shape.</li>
</ol>
<h2>What to 3D print first</h2>
<ol>
<li>Print the test model that came with your printer to confirm it works.</li>
<li>Print one useful thing for your home – a hook or a clip.</li>
<li>Print one fun thing – an articulated animal or a fidget toy.</li>
<li>Try something custom from your own image or SVG.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>Start with small, useful prints that fix everyday annoyances, then mix in fun and gift projects. PLA covers most of them; switch to PETG for outdoor or warm places and TPU for anything flexible.</p>
