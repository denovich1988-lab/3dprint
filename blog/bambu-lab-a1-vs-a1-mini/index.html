Bambu Lab A1 vs A1 mini – the real differences

Updated November 22, 2026

Key points
  • The A1 prints up to 256 mm per side; the A1 mini up to 180 mm.
  • The A1 bed reaches 100 °C; the A1 mini bed reaches 80 °C.
  • Speed (500 mm/s), acceleration (10,000 mm/s²) and the 300 °C hot end are listed the same.

The Bambu Lab A1 and A1 mini are the same kind of printer in two sizes. Both are open bed-slingers with automatic calibration, a 300 °C quick-swap hot end and AMS lite support. The real decision is how big your prints are.

Specifications below are from Bambu Lab’s published spec sheets.

Side-by-side

A1 A1 mini
Build volume 256 × 256 × 256 mm 180 × 180 × 180 mm
Max bed temperature 100 °C 80 °C
Max hot end temperature 300 °C 300 °C
Max speed / acceleration 500 mm/s / 10,000 mm/s² 500 mm/s / 10,000 mm/s²
Max hot end flow 28 mm³/s 28 mm³/s
Ideal materials PLA, PETG, TPU, PVA PLA, PETG, TPU, PVA
Launch price (US) $399 ($559 with AMS lite) $299

How much bigger is the A1?

A 256 mm cube holds about 2.9 times the volume of a 180 mm cube. In practice, the A1 prints helmets, large planters and long brackets in one piece, while the A1 mini is best for parts up to about the size of a coffee mug.

Check your models before you buy

Open a few models you want to print in our STL viewer or 3D viewer. The size readout shows the X, Y and Z dimensions in millimetres. If anything is over 180 mm, choose the A1.

Questions and answers

Is the A1 mini good enough for beginners?

Yes, if your prints fit in 180 mm. It has the same automatic calibration as the A1 and takes less space.

Do both work with the AMS lite?

Yes. Both printers support Bambu Lab’s four-spool AMS lite for multi-colour printing.

Which prints larger helmets or cosplay parts?

The A1, thanks to its 256 mm build volume. Bambu Lab advertises it as able to print a helmet in one piece.
