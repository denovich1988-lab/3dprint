<h2>Types of 3D printed snakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Articulated flexi snakes.</strong> The most common type: a chain of segments with ball, pin or slot joints, printed flat in one piece. They make great fidget toys and desk pets – see our wider list of <a href="/blog/3d-printed-fidget-toys/">3D printed fidget toys</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Realistic snakes.</strong> Models styled after real species – cobras with a raised hood, rattlesnakes, pythons, vipers – often with detailed scales. Some are articulated; others are static sculptures.</li>
<li><strong>Cartoon and cute snakes.</strong> Simplified shapes with big eyes, popular with kids and as keychains.</li>
<li><strong>Serpents and sea monsters.</strong> Fantasy serpents sit somewhere between snakes and dragons; if you like these, our <a href="/blog/3d-printed-dragon/">3D printed dragon guide</a> covers similar articulated models.</li>
<li><strong>Coiled sculptures and planters.</strong> Static snakes coiled around a pot, a branch or a stand – often printed in parts and glued.</li>
<li><strong>Resin display snakes.</strong> Small, highly detailed snakes for display or tabletop games, printed on resin printers.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where to find 3D printed snake models</h2>
<p>Model libraries such as Printables, MakerWorld, Thingiverse and Cults list thousands of snake models, from free flexi snakes to paid premium designs. When choosing a model:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Read the print notes.</strong> Good designers state the layer height, whether supports are needed and the tested scale.</li>
<li><strong>Check the makes.</strong> Photos of other people&#39;s prints show whether the joints work on ordinary printers.</li>
<li><strong>Check the licence.</strong> Many models allow personal printing only. If you plan to sell prints, pick models whose licence allows commercial use, or buy a commercial licence from the designer.</li>
<li><strong>Look at the file.</strong> Open the STL or 3MF in our <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a> to check its length and segment count before slicing. If you only need the mesh from a 3MF project, our <a href="/3mf-to-stl/">3MF to STL converter</a> extracts it.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How print-in-place snake joints work</h2>
<p>Each segment of a flexi snake has a hook, pin or ball that sits inside the next segment, with a small gap all round. The slicer prints both parts at the same time, layer by layer, and the gap keeps them from fusing. When the print finishes, you flex each joint to break any light strands and the snake moves freely.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> the gap is tiny – often a few tenths of a millimetre. Anything that fills it, such as over-extrusion, a squished first layer, stringing or supports, will lock the joint.</p>
<h2>Slicer settings for a 3D printed snake</h2>
<p>Start with the designer&#39;s notes, then use these defaults:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Scale: 100%.</strong> Scaling down shrinks the joint gaps too. If you need a smaller snake, look for a smaller version from the designer. Scaling up a little is usually fine.</li>
<li><strong>Layer height: 0.2 mm</strong> for a standard 0.4 mm nozzle, or the designer&#39;s value. Very coarse layers can reduce joint clearance.</li>
<li><strong>Supports: off.</strong> Articulated snakes are designed to print flat.</li>
<li><strong>First layer:</strong> a clean, flat first layer without too much squish. Enable elephant&#39;s foot compensation in your slicer if available – the first layer is where most joints fuse.</li>
<li><strong>Flow and pressure advance:</strong> calibrated. Over-extrusion is a common cause of stuck joints.</li>
<li><strong>Walls and infill:</strong> keep the designer&#39;s settings. Segments are small, so they are often printed almost solid anyway.</li>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> normal speeds work on well-tuned printers; slow the first layer for good adhesion.</li>
<li><strong>Retraction:</strong> tuned to avoid stringing between segments.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> on a long snake, place it diagonally or curled on the plate if the model allows it. Many designs are made to print in a curve so a long snake fits on a small bed.</p>
<h2>Best filament for 3D printed snakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>PLA</strong> – easy to print, accurate and stiff enough that joints stay open. Our <a href="/blog/pla-filament/">PLA filament guide</a> covers settings.</li>
<li><strong>Silk PLA</strong> – a glossy finish that looks like scales. It can be slightly weaker, so be gentle when freeing joints.</li>
<li><strong>Gradient and rainbow PLA</strong> – colour shifts along the snake with no colour changes needed.</li>
<li><strong>Multi-colour</strong> – printers with automatic filament systems can print stripes or patterned scales. Change colours by height or paint stripes in the slicer.</li>
<li><strong>Glow-in-the-dark PLA</strong> – fun for kids, but abrasive to brass nozzles over time.</li>
<li><strong>PETG</strong> – tougher, but it strings more, which can bridge joint gaps.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to print a 3D printed snake, step by step</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Pick a model</strong> with good makes and clear print notes.</li>
<li><strong>Clean the build plate</strong> so the first layer sticks evenly.</li>
<li><strong>Slice at 100%</strong> with supports off and the designer&#39;s settings.</li>
<li><strong>Watch the first layer</strong> – it decides whether the joints will work.</li>
<li><strong>Let the plate cool</strong> before removing the snake, so it releases without bending.</li>
<li><strong>Free the joints</strong> by gently flexing each segment from head to tail. Do not twist hard; work along the snake a little at a time.</li>
<li><strong>Clean up</strong> any strings carefully with flush cutters or a hobby knife.</li>
</ol>

<td>Wet filament or retraction too low</td>

<h2>Ideas and uses for 3D printed snakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Fidget toys</strong> for desks and classrooms.</li>
<li><strong>Gifts and party favours</strong> – small snakes print quickly and use little filament.</li>
<li><strong>Halloween decorations</strong> in black, green or glow filament.</li>
<li><strong>Keychains</strong> – short snake versions with a loop.</li>
<li><strong>Display pieces</strong> – realistic snakes painted by hand.</li>
</ul>
<p>For more animal models, see our guide to <a href="/blog/3d-printed-animals/">3D printed animals</a>, and for kid-friendly prints, our list of <a href="/blog/3d-printed-toys/">3D printed toys</a>.</p>
<h2>Safety notes</h2>
<p>Small segments can be a choking hazard, so keep snakes away from young children and pets. PLA prints are not designed for chewing, and broken segments can have sharp edges.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>A 3D printed snake is one of the easiest ways to make something that moves and impresses straight off the printer. Choose a well-reviewed articulated model, print it at full size in PLA with no supports, get the first layer right and flex the joints gently. Once your first snake works, try silk, gradient or multi-colour filament for a far more striking result.</p>
