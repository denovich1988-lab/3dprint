<h2>What is PETG HF?</h2>
<p>Every hot end has a limit to how much plastic it can melt per second, usually given as maximum volumetric flow in cubic millimetres per second. Standard PETG is fairly viscous when molten, so on a fast printer it often becomes the bottleneck: push it too fast and the nozzle cannot keep up, which shows as thin, weak or gappy walls.</p>
<p>High-flow filaments are formulated to melt faster and flow more easily. That raises the volumetric flow the same hot end can sustain, so the printer can use its speed. PETG HF does this for PETG; there are also high-speed PLA grades that do the same for PLA.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> if you own a fast CoreXY printer and your PETG prints take far longer than your PLA prints, a high-flow PETG is the simplest way to close the gap.</p>

<p>PETG HF is still PETG. It keeps the material&#39;s strengths – toughness, low warping, water resistance and a glass transition around 80 °C – and its weak points, such as stringing and very strong adhesion to smooth PEI. <p><strong>Tip:</strong> do not expect PETG HF to fix stringing or adhesion problems by itself. Those come from moisture, temperature and bed surface, not from flow.</p>

<p><strong>Max volumetric speed.</strong> The filament profile contains a maximum volumetric speed value. That number, not the speed slider, decides how fast the printer actually extrudes. If you see under-extrusion at high speed, lower this value a little; if a calibration test shows your hot end can do more, raise it carefully.</p>
<p><strong>Enclosed printers.</strong> Bambu Lab recommends opening the top cover when the bed is above 70 °C with PETG, so heat does not build up and soften the part.</p>
<h2>Drying PETG HF</h2>
<p>PETG absorbs moisture from the air. Bambu Lab&#39;s wiki has a page specifically on printing PETG HF when it is not fully dried: water in the filament turns to steam in the nozzle and causes stringing, oozing, small holes and weaker parts. The wiki also warns against running the fast Bambu PETG HF profile on damp filament and describes slower, more forgiving settings if you cannot dry it.</p>
<p>Bambu Lab&#39;s drying recommendation for PETG HF is 65 °C for 8 hours. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-dry-filament/">how to dry filament</a> covers dryers, ovens and storage.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> if PETG HF suddenly strings badly or the surface looks rough and bubbly, dry the spool before changing any settings. Store it in a sealed box with desiccant afterwards.</p>
<h2>Bed adhesion and part removal</h2>
<p>PETG HF behaves like other PETG on the bed:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Textured PEI</strong> is the easiest surface; parts release once the plate cools.</li>
<li><strong>Smooth PEI</strong> needs a thin layer of glue stick, which acts as a release agent so PETG does not tear the surface.</li>
<li><strong>Cool Plate</strong> is not recommended for PETG in Bambu Lab&#39;s table.</li>
<li><strong>Clean the plate</strong> with dish soap and water or isopropyl alcohol so oils do not cause lifting.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> most &quot;PETG ruined my plate&quot; stories come from smooth PEI without glue. A textured plate avoids the problem.</p>
<h2>Using PETG HF in an AMS or other filament changer</h2>
<p>PETG is on Bambu Lab&#39;s list of filaments supported in the AMS, and PETG HF on Bambu Lab spools carries an RFID tag so the AMS reads the type and colour. For multi-material prints, Bambu Lab also sells a support filament marketed for PLA and PETG, which can be used as a support interface for cleaner overhangs. Our explainer on <a href="/blog/what-is-ams-in-3d-printing/">what AMS is in 3D printing</a> covers which filaments work in which unit.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> when mixing PLA and PETG in one print, keep them apart or use them only as support and interface, because the two do not bond strongly to each other.</p>
<h2>What PETG HF is good for</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Functional parts printed fast</strong> – brackets, clips, enclosures and organisers.</li>
<li><strong>Large parts</strong> where print time matters, such as storage bins and tool holders.</li>
<li><strong>Outdoor or warm locations</strong> where PLA would soften, as long as the part stays below about 80 °C.</li>
<li><strong>Watertight items</strong> such as planters, thanks to PETG&#39;s good layer bonding.</li>
</ul>
<p>It is less suited to fine display models, where PLA gives sharper detail, and to parts in full sun for years, where ASA is the better choice.</p>
<h2>PETG HF availability in 2026</h2>
<p>Bambu Lab once sold a PETG Basic line, which later disappeared from its store, and PETG HF took its place. In March 2026 Bambu launched a new PETG Basic in seven colours. According to the figures Bambu published at launch, PETG Basic is stronger than PETG HF in bending (75 vs 64 MPa in XY) and layer strength (56 vs 48 MPa in Z), and Bambu says it should be dried before use. Colour availability for PETG HF differs between regional stores and changes over time.</p>
<p><strong>What to do:</strong> check your regional Bambu Lab store for current colours. If the colour you need is gone, a standard PETG (such as PETG Basic) or another brand&#39;s high-speed PETG can replace it; use the matching profile and run a quick flow test. Our <a href="/blog/bambu-filament/">Bambu filament guide</a> explains how the Bambu lines fit together.</p>
<h2>Checklist for your first PETG HF print</h2>
<ol>
<li>Dry the spool if it has been open for a while (65 °C for 8 hours).</li>
<li>Select the &quot;Bambu PETG HF&quot; or &quot;Generic PETG HF&quot; profile.</li>
<li>Use a textured PEI plate, or glue stick on smooth PEI.</li>
<li>On an enclosed printer, open the top cover for hot-bed PETG prints.</li>
<li>Print a small test part at the profile&#39;s default speed.</li>
<li>Only then raise speed or tune retraction.</li>
<li>Check the model in our <a href="/3mf-viewer/">3MF viewer</a> or <a href="/stl-viewer/">STL viewer</a> before slicing.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>PETG HF is regular PETG tuned for speed. If you own a fast printer and print a lot of functional parts, it saves real time while keeping PETG&#39;s toughness. If your printer is slow or you mostly print small parts, standard PETG gives the same result. Either way, dry filament and the right plate matter more than the label on the spool.</p>
