27:T17ba,<p><strong>A 3D printed dragon is usually an articulated, print-in-place model: dozens of linked segments that print as one piece without supports and move as soon as they come off the plate.</strong> Flexible dragons are among the most popular things to 3D print, and there are free and paid designs ranging from palm-sized toys to crystal dragons around two feet long when stretched out. This guide explains the main types, the filament and slicer settings that make the joints work, and how to fix a dragon that comes off the bed stuck solid.</p>

<h2>What &quot;print-in-place articulated&quot; means</h2>
<p>The designer leaves a small gap between each segment and the next, and shapes the links so they can bridge or self-support without touching. The whole dragon prints in one go, and the segments are already free. Because the parts are designed to support themselves, adding supports only traps plastic inside the joints.</p>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> never enable supports for a print-in-place dragon unless the designer asks for them.</p>
<h2>Why the joint gap matters</h2>
<p>Designers usually set the clearance between moving parts at about 0.3 mm. Prusa recommends at least 0.3 mm for movable parts, and All3DP says 0.3 mm suits most printers, with a working range of roughly 0.2–0.6 mm. The gap is in the model, not in your slicer, so do not rescale a dragon much smaller than designed: shrinking it shrinks the gaps too, and they can close.</p>
<h2>Best filament for a 3D printed dragon</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>PLA</strong> is the standard choice. It prints sharp details and rigid joints.</li>
<li><strong>Silk PLA</strong> gives the shiny, metallic look many dragon photos show.</li>
<li><strong>Multicolour or gradient PLA</strong> changes colour along the body without a multi-material printer.</li>
<li><strong>Several colours by part or layer:</strong> on a printer with an AMS or similar unit, or with a manual colour change at a chosen layer.</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid flexible TPU for print-in-place joints; it can fuse or stick in the gaps. Read our <a href="/blog/petg-vs-pla/">PETG vs PLA</a> guide if you want a tougher toy.</p>

<h2>After printing: free the joints</h2>
<p>Let the plate cool, remove the dragon in one piece, then flex each joint gently through its full range. Start at the tail and work towards the head. Joints that feel stiff loosen as you move them. If one segment will not move at all, check whether it fused (see below) before forcing it.</p>
<h2>Fixing a dragon with fused joints</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Elephant&#39;s foot:</strong> the first layer is squashed outwards and bridges the gap. Turn on elephant foot compensation or raise the first-layer height slightly.</li>
<li><strong>Over-extrusion:</strong> too much plastic closes the gaps. Calibrate flow for your filament.</li>
<li><strong>Layers too thick:</strong> go to 0.2 mm or thinner.</li>
<li><strong>Scaled down:</strong> print at the designed size, or pick a smaller design.</li>
<li><strong>Stringing inside joints:</strong> dry the filament and check retraction. Our <a href="/blog/filament-dryer/">filament dryer guide</a> helps with damp spools.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Big dragons on small printers</h2>
<p>Crystal dragons are often printed curled up so the long body fits a smaller bed. Check the bounding box before you slice: open the file in our <a href="/3d-viewer/">3D viewer</a> to see its size in millimetres. If the dragon came as a 3MF project, extract the model with the <a href="/3mf-to-stl/">3MF to STL converter</a>.</p>
<h2>Where to find dragon models</h2>
<p>Search &quot;articulated dragon&quot; or &quot;flexi dragon&quot; on large model libraries such as Printables or MakerWorld. Check each model&#39;s licence before you sell prints, as many designs are for personal use only. For more ideas, see our list of <a href="/blog/what-to-3d-print/">things to 3D print</a> and <a href="/blog/3d-printed-fidget-toys/">3D printed fidget toys</a>.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>A 3D printed dragon is a satisfying print because it works the moment it leaves the plate. Choose a print-in-place design, print it without supports at 0.2 mm layers or thinner, keep the first layer from squashing, and flex the joints gently. Silk or gradient PLA makes the result look its best.</p>
