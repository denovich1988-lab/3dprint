<h2>What is PLA+?</h2>
<p>PLA+ is not a separate plastic. It is PLA – usually from the same type of plant-based resin – changed so that printed parts are less brittle. Vendors do this by adding impact modifiers, using a different PLA resin grade, or both. 3D-Fuel, for example, publishes that its standard PLA+ uses NatureWorks Ingeo 4043D, while its tougher &quot;Pro&quot; grade uses Ingeo 3D870, a grade made for 3D printing.</p>
<p><strong>The key point: &quot;PLA+&quot; has no fixed definition.</strong> No standard says how much tougher a filament must be to carry the name. Two spools labelled PLA+ from different brands can be quite different materials, and some may behave much like regular PLA. That is why reading the brand&#39;s data sheet matters more than the label.</p>
<p>How three well-known brands describe theirs:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>eSUN PLA+:</strong> a modified formula that eSUN says is stronger and tougher than standard PLA and resists breaking from handling and minor bumps.</li>
<li><strong>SUNLU PLA+:</strong> described as reinforced and toughened PLA, less prone to breaking and more impact-resistant, while keeping PLA&#39;s easy printing.</li>
<li><strong>Polymaker PLA Pro:</strong> Polymaker&#39;s own name for a toughened PLA. Its wiki positions it between its rigid but brittle PolyLite PLA and its very ductile PolyMax PLA – stiff, with high impact strength. (Polymaker notes this grade is being replaced by a new PLA Pro by the end of 2026.)</li>
</ul>
<h2>What actually changes: strength, toughness and heat</h2>
<p>The main gain is <strong>toughness</strong> – how much a part bends or absorbs a knock before it breaks. eSUN&#39;s filament data sheet lists higher elongation at break and roughly double the Izod impact strength for its PLA+ compared with its standard PLA, with tensile strength about the same.</p>
<p>What does <strong>not</strong> improve much is heat resistance. In the same eSUN data sheet, PLA+ has a heat distortion temperature of 52 °C against 56 °C for standard PLA. Most PLA+ grades still soften around the same 55–60 °C point as regular PLA, so a PLA+ phone mount on a car dashboard can still sag in summer.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> choose PLA+ for parts that get dropped, clipped together, screwed into or bent slightly – brackets, clips, enclosures, toys, tool holders. It does not turn PLA into a heat- or weather-resistant material.</p>

<p><strong>Tips:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Start with your slicer&#39;s generic PLA profile and raise the nozzle by 5–10 °C.</li>
<li>If parts snap cleanly along a layer line, the layers are not bonding well: print hotter or slightly slower.</li>
<li>Keep part cooling on, as with PLA, unless you see layer adhesion problems.</li>
<li>Higher temperatures help layer bonding but can add stringing, so check retraction if strings appear.</li>
<li>PLA+ absorbs moisture like PLA. If it starts to pop, string or snap on the spool, dry it – see our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-dry-filament/">how to dry filament</a>.</li>

<h2>PLA plus vs PLA: which should you print?</h2>
<p><strong>Stick with standard PLA for:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Decorative prints, figures, vases and display models.</li>
<li>Fine-detail prints where stiffness and crisp edges matter most.</li>
<li>Specialty finishes – silk, matte, marble, glow – which are mostly sold as PLA.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Pick PLA+ for:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Functional parts that get handled, dropped or flexed.</li>
<li>Snap-fits, clips and parts with screws or heat-set inserts.</li>
<li>Kids&#39; toys and fidget toys – see our <a href="/blog/3d-printed-fidget-toys/">3D printed fidget toys</a> ideas.</li>
</ul>
<p>PLA+ usually costs a little more than basic PLA, but the difference is small, so many people simply use PLA+ as their everyday filament.</p>
<h2>PLA Pro vs PLA plus (and Tough PLA)</h2>
<p>These names overlap:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>PLA+ / PLA Plus:</strong> a toughened or improved PLA, defined by each brand.</li>
<li><strong>PLA Pro:</strong> another brand name for much the same idea. Polymaker uses &quot;PLA Pro&quot; for its toughened PLA; eSUN&#39;s product URL says &quot;pla-pro&quot; while the product itself is sold as PLA+.</li>
<li><strong>Tough PLA / PLA Tough:</strong> names used for PLA modified to absorb impact. 3D-Fuel calls Tough PLA the closest thing to a real category among these terms, because these grades are engineered for impact resistance. Bambu Lab sells its own &quot;PLA Tough&quot;.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>In practice:</strong> treat PLA Pro and PLA+ as the same kind of product and compare data sheets – impact strength, elongation at break, heat distortion and print temperature – rather than names.</p>

<h2>How to choose: a quick checklist</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Will the part see heat above about 50 °C or sit in the sun?</strong> Use PETG or ASA, not PLA or PLA+.</li>
<li><strong>Will it be dropped, clipped, screwed or flexed?</strong> PLA+ or PLA Pro.</li>
<li><strong>Is it decorative or detailed?</strong> Standard PLA or a specialty PLA.</li>
<li><strong>Comparing two PLA+ spools?</strong> Check the data sheet for impact strength and elongation, not the word &quot;plus&quot;.</li>
<li><strong>Switching to PLA+?</strong> Start with PLA settings, add 5–10 °C, and print a test clip or bracket before a big job.</li>

<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>PLA+ is a real improvement over basic PLA when it comes from a brand that publishes data: it is tougher and less likely to snap, and it prints almost as easily. It is not a heat- or weather-resistant material, and because &quot;PLA+&quot; has no standard, the improvement varies from one spool to the next. Use standard PLA for decorative prints, PLA+ for everyday functional parts, and PETG or ASA when heat, sunlight or flexing are involved.</p>
